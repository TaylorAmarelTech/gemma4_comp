AbstractFrontier language models usually recognize a migrant-worker trafficking scheme when they see one - yet they often stop short of what actually protects a worker: naming the specific exploitation indicator, citing the controlling statute, refusing to operationalize the scheme, and routing to concrete help. This benchmark measures whether a thin, model-agnostic layer of legal grounding - fired indicator rules, retrieved law, and deterministic tools, added to the prompt and nothing else - closes that gap, and by how much. Every model answers each adversarial, disguised prompt twice (raw, then harness-wrapped); a panel of frontier judges, each from a different model family and never grading its own, scores both replies 0–100 on five reasoned safety criteria. The reported metric is the paired, per-prompt lift, which cancels each judge's absolute scale. Across models the harness yields large, statistically decisive gains - headline models exceed Cohen's d = 3 at a 100% win rate (p < 0.001) - that hold across difficulty, recruitment corridors, and scheme typologies, and survive a length-matched placebo (the lift is the injected knowledge, not the preamble: +3.34 beyond placebo, p < 0.001) and a judge-independent citation check (0% hallucinated statutes). We present this as a reproducible engineering benchmark and a preliminary study; the standing limitation is that the judges are language models, not anti-trafficking professionals, so a blinded human-expert validation remains the honest precondition for a peer-reviewed claim.

How it works

1Answer twice

The model answers each hard, disguised prompt raw, then again wrapped in the harness: fired indicator rules, retrieved law, and deterministic tools, added to the prompt. Same model, same question.

2A panel scores 0 to 100

A diverse panel of frontier judges, each from a different model family and never grading its own, scores both replies on five criteria: name the indicator, cite the specific law, refuse, give concrete resources, protect the worker.

3Report the lift

The score is harnessed minus baseline. The paired design cancels each judge's own scale, so the lift is what survives judge disagreement. Models are ranked by it.

Construct validity (does the lift hold across the space, or one slice?)

A gain that only appears on one typology or corridor would be a narrow trick. Pooled across all models, the lift holds across difficulty levels, recruitment corridors, and scheme typologies - evidence the mechanism (name the indicator, cite the law, refuse, route to help) generalizes rather than overfitting one slice.

Controls & external validity (is the lift real, or an artifact?)

Four independent checks attack the result from the angles a reviewer would: is it just "any preamble"? does the rubric grade the right things per prompt? does a second, unrelated grader agree? are the cited laws real?

Negative control (placebo)The lift is the knowledge, not the preamble. A length-matched placebo arm (generic "be careful, be ethical" boilerplate - no citations, indicators, or statutes) isolates the "any preamble" effect. On the LLM judge: baseline 5.08 → placebo 6.24 → harnessed 9.58 - the real grounding adds +3.34 beyond the placebo (z=9.60, p<0.001). negative_control.md
Applicability auditThe rubric scores the right dimensions per prompt. An independent judge (gpt-oss:120b, outside the candidate families) re-decides which criteria apply, 3 passes, over 800 (prompt×dimension) pairs: 68% agreement with the deterministic gate (Cohen's κ=0.36), 86% cross-pass unanimity. applicability_audit.md
Convergent validityTwo unrelated graders agree on direction. A deterministic rule-based grader and the LLM judge both independently find the harness helps (det +0.18, judge +1.73, both >0) and trend together in aggregate. Honest caveat: the per-prompt magnitude correlation is weak (Pearson r=0.18), so we report both and treat neither as a proxy. convergent_validity.md
Citation accuracyThe added law is real, not invented. A deterministic, judge-independent check of the citations it can verify: 0% hallucination, and 100% of section numbers fall within the instrument's real range. citation_accuracy.md

Validity and limitations (read before citing)

A reproducible engineering benchmark and a preliminary study, not yet a peer-review-validated measure of real-world trafficking-safety. It measures safety as scored by language-model judges against an author-designed rubric. The strengths are real: a paired design that cancels judge bias, a diverse self-family-excluded panel with strong agreement, and controls (a knowledge-free length-matched placebo, a negative control, and adversarial input attacks). The challenges a reviewer would raise, stated up front:

  1. No human-expert ground truth (the main gap). The judges are language models, not anti-trafficking professionals. High agreement shows the judges are consistent, not that they are correct. A blinded human-expert validation is the next step and the honest blocker for a peer-reviewed claim.
  2. Author conflict and construct validity. The same author wrote the harness, the rubric, and the prompts. The gain on dimensions the harness never injects, plus the placebo control, mitigate circularity but do not eliminate it; the five-criterion rubric is not yet validated against expert consensus.
  3. Citation accuracy is judged only indirectly. The rubric rewards citing the specific law; the LLM judge scores citation presence, not correctness. A deterministic, judge-independent check now backs the lift: of the citations it can verify, the harnessed replies have a 0% hallucination rate and 100% of section numbers fall in the instrument's real range, so the added citations are real, not invented (citation_accuracy.md). It covers ILO conventions and section numbers, not yet every named national statute or citation relevance.
  4. Narrow inputs (partly addressed). The set is now 776 synthetic prompts across 77 typologies at easy/medium/hard difficulty (v1.1) - recruitment-fee schemes, pretext/override jailbreaks, evasion probes, and casefile-derived worker-support scenarios - broader than the original fee-focused, hard-only 210. It remains synthetic, English-only, and text-only (no multimodal documents yet).
  5. Model coverage growing; judges are mutable. A multi-model frontier sweep is actively widening the leaderboard, but it is still filling in; and the judges are cloud endpoints that can change or retire, which erodes long-term reproducibility unless versions are pinned or archived.
