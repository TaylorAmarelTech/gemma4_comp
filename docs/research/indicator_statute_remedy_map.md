## 4. Per-indicator detail (load-bearing specifics beyond the master table)

**4-7 Document retention `[S]` (source-verified).** Strongest governing provisions: **ICRMW Art. 21** (unlawful for anyone but a duly authorized public official to confiscate/destroy a migrant worker's identity, travel, residence, or work-permit documents; lawful official confiscation requires a detailed receipt); **ILO C189 Art. 9(c)** (domestic workers keep their own documents); **US TVPA § 1592** (directly criminalizes destroying/concealing/confiscating/possessing another's actual or purported passport/immigration document in furtherance of forced labour/trafficking). Forced-labour characterization via C029 Art. 2(1) + the ILO 11-indicator list; remedy duties via P029. Unlocks R1 + the full trafficking remedy set (R2–R9), plus R10 where wages are also withheld.

**4-8 Withholding of wages / unlawful deductions `[S]` (source-verified; remedies completed from the same approved instruments — the source JSON was truncated mid-C143-Art.9(1)).** Core instrument **ILO C095**: Art. 8 (deductions only under conditions/limits set by national law, collective agreement, or arbitration award), Art. 9 (prohibits any deduction to obtain/retain employment, incl. payments to an employer, representative, labour contractor, or recruiter), Art. 12 (regular payment + full final settlement of all wages due on termination), Art. 5 (direct payment to the worker), Art. 6 (no limiting the worker's freedom to dispose of wages), Art. 11 (worker ranks as a privileged creditor in employer insolvency). Equal-remuneration backstops: ICRMW Art. 25(1) and ILO C097 Art. 6(1)(a)(i). Status-proof recovery: ICRMW Art. 25(3) (obligations survive irregular status), Art. 22(6)/(9) (settlement notwithstanding expulsion), ILO C143 Art. 9(1) (equal treatment for rights arising from past employment where status can't be regularized), ICRMW Art. 18 (equality before courts). Where withholding operates as coercion it becomes forced labour (C029 Art. 2(1); criminal sanction C029 Art. 25; US TVPA § 1589). **Remedies:** R10 primary (back-pay/arrears, reversal of unlawful deductions, full final settlement, privileged-creditor priority); R3 compensation incl. P029 Art. 4(1), Warsaw Art. 15, EU 2011/36 Art. 17, ICRMW Art. 83, and US TVPA § 1593 restitution (full wage/loss amount) + § 1595 civil action; plus R2, R4, R5, R8 when the withholding is part of forced labour/trafficking, and R6/R7/R9 in a trafficking case.

**4-9 Debt bondage `[X]`.** Beyond R2–R10: the coercive debt is a means of exacting forced labour, so it is **not lawfully enforceable** as such, and worker-charged recruitment fees that created it are recoverable/refundable under **ILO C181 Art. 7** (no fees to workers) — fee abuse also reinforced by ILO C095 Art. 9 (no deductions to retain employment). Frame the debt as the coercion mechanism, then route to compensation (R3) and fee remediation, not as a private debt the worker owes.

**4-10 / 4-11 Abusive conditions & excessive overtime `[X]`.** These run on equal-treatment / decent-conditions standards (ICRMW Art. 25; ILO C097 Art. 6; ILO C143 Art. 1; ILO C189 Art. 6 and Art. 10) plus the wage machinery for unpaid overtime (R10: ILO C095; C097 Art. 6(1)(a)(i); C189 Art. 10). They escalate to R2/R3 only when conditions/overtime are exacted under menace (C029 Art. 2(1)).

## 5. Citation-coherence guardrails (negative cites — do NOT pair these)

- **ILO C105 (1957) — never cite for any indicator in this map.** C105 abolishes forced labour imposed as *political coercion/education, economic-development mobilization, labour discipline, punishment for strikes, or racial/social/national/religious discrimination* — none of which govern document retention, wage theft, debt bondage, or any economic-coercion indicator here. Citing C105 for these is a coherence error. (Carried verbatim from the source's verified note.)
- **ILO C181 Art. 7** governs recruitment-fee abuse / debt-bondage origin (indicators 2, 9) — **not** document retention (indicator 7).
- **ILO C190 (Violence and Harassment, 2019)** is the natural fit for indicator 5 but is **outside this task's approved citation set** — do not cite it; rely on C029 Art. 2(1) + Palermo/Warsaw/EU 2011/36 + US TVPA § 1589/§ 1591.
- **ILO C188 (Work in Fishing)** and the **1956 Supplementary Slavery Convention** (the textbook debt-bondage definition) are likewise outside the approved set — do not cite here even where sector-apt.
- **Ratification reality:** few destination states have ratified **C189 / ICRMW**, so in many corridors they operate as *normative standards*; the binding domestic route runs through the trafficking statute transposing **Palermo / Warsaw / EU 2011/36**, or, in the US, the **TVPA**. State an ICRMW/C189 article as the *standard*, and the trafficking-statute/TVPA route as the *enforceable* one, when the corridor's destination state has not ratified.

## 6. Synthesis notes

- **Two engines drive every row.** (a) The *forced-labour characterization* — C029 Art. 2(1) + the ILO 11-indicator checklist — is what turns a labour grievance into an FL case and pulls in P029's binding remedy duties (Arts. 1–4) and C029 Art. 25's penal duty. (b) The *trafficking framing* — Palermo Art. 3 / Warsaw Art. 4 / EU 2011/36 Art. 2 — is what unlocks the victim-protection machinery (R4–R7) and, via national transposition or the TVPA, the binding domestic remedy.
- **Remedy completeness rule.** When ≥2 indicators co-occur, treat as a possible FL/trafficking case (R8) and surface the *full* applicable cluster set, not just the single most obvious remedy (e.g., document retention + wage withholding ⇒ R1 *and* R10, not R10 alone).
- **All article numbers are in-range** against each instrument's real article count, so they pass `scripts/citation_accuracy.py`'s `_verify_section_numbers` check, and every ILO convention cited is within C001–C190.
- **Provenance honesty:** indicators 7 and 8 are reproduced from the verified source JSON (indicator 8's remedy list completed from the same approved instruments because the source was truncated mid-`C143 Art. 9(1)`); indicators 1–6 and 9–11 are synthesized to the identical approved instrument set with article-level cites confirmed real and on-point — no instrument outside the approved list is cited anywhere.
- Citation format aligned to the project house style in `docs/research/corridor_remedy_referral.md` and the verifier in `scripts/citation_accuracy.py`.
