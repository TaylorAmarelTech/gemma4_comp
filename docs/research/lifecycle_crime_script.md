# Trafficking lifecycle (crime-script) — stage scenarios & stage-appropriate action

> Composite, synthetic scenarios across the full lifecycle, each verified. Stage-appropriate action: recruitment→prevent/verify · transit→safety · exploitation→document+rights · control→safe exit+evidence · exit→identification+remedy+safeguard.

## Recruitment

**Scenario.** Composite, synthetic — no real people, agencies, or case numbers.

"Ramesh" (composite name) is a 27-year-old man from a rural district in a South Asian origin country who supports his parents and a younger sister on seasonal farm income. A locally trusted sub-agent — a fellow villager, not a licensed recruiter — tells him a recruitment agency in the capital can place him in a two-year CONSTRUCTION job in a Gulf destination country at a wage roughly triple what he earns at home, with "free food, free accommodation, free flights." To lock in the slot, Ramesh is told he must pay a one-time "processing and placement fee" equal to about eight months of the promised salary, plus smaller charges for "medical, training, and documentation."

He cannot afford the fee, so the sub-agent arranges a loan from a moneylender connected to the agency, at high monthly interest, secured against the family's small plot of land. Ramesh signs a contract he cannot fully read — it is printed in the destination-country language and English — and is handed only a one-page summary in his own language. The agency keeps his passport "to process the work visa." A week before the flight, the sub-agent quietly tells him the placement has "changed": it is now domestic/cleaning work for a private household, not construction, the monthly wage is lower than promised, and there is no written amendment. When Ramesh hesitates, he is told it is "too late" — the fee is non-refundable, the loan is already accruing, and other candidates are waiting. No one has independently verified the employer, the job order, or the agency's license, and Ramesh has not yet left the country.

Corridor (generalized): South Asia origin → Gulf destination, low-wage domestic/construction stream. This is a pre-departure snapshot: travel and on-site exploitation have NOT yet occurred, which is precisely why the stage-appropriate response is prevention and verification rather than documentation of harm or extraction.

**Indicators.** deceptive_recruitment — job sector switched from construction to domestic/cleaning work, promised wage lowered, and only a partial-language summary provided; classic deception + contract-substitution setup at the recruitment stage; abuse_of_vulnerability — targets a rural, low-literacy, financially precarious worker through a trusted same-village broker, exploiting limited information and economic pressure; fee_camouflage — a single large outlay split into 'processing,' 'placement,' 'medical,' 'training,' and 'documentation' line items that disguise an illegitimate worker-paid recruitment fee; fee_bondage — fee charged to the worker (≈8 months' salary) and tied to keeping the placement, violating the employer-pays principle (ILO C181 Art. 7; Fair Recruitment 2016); debt_bondage — fee financed by a high-interest loan from an agency-linked lender, secured against the family's land, creating a debt that must be worked off (ILO C029 indicator); passport_retention — agency holds the passport pre-departure 'for visa processing,' unlawful document retention (ICRMW Art. 21); document_control — worker has no readable copy of the binding contract; identity and travel documents are held and controlled by the recruiter; retaliation_risk — coercive 'too late / fee non-refundable / loan already due / others are waiting' pressure, with the family's pledged land as implicit leverage against backing out; jurisdiction_shopping — recruitment routed through an unlicensed sub-agent outside the regulated channel, exploiting the accountability gap between origin regulator and destination employer

**Stage-appropriate action.** Recruitment stage → PREVENT and VERIFY (pre-harm). Because no travel or exploitation has happened yet, the goal is to stop entry into an exploitative placement and independently verify legitimacy BEFORE any departure — not to document on-site abuse (that is the employment/exploitation action) or to plan safe exit and preserve evidence for escape (that is the control/exit action).

Concrete prevent/verify steps:
1) Pause the deployment and reject the "too late" pressure. Withdrawal before departure is a right; a fee that should never have been charged, and a loan built on it, are reasons to STOP, not to proceed.
2) Verify the recruiter chain against the origin country's official labour-migration regulator's licensed-agency registry. An informal village sub-agent and any agency not listed (or listed but using unlicensed sub-agents) is a red flag — independent of all other facts.
3) Verify the job order and employer actually exist and match the offer. Request the attested job order / demand letter and the employer's real identity; cross-check the sector, wage, hours, and accommodation against the contract, and confirm with the destination-country embassy / labour attaché.
4) Verify the contract: insist on ONE contract in a language Ramesh reads, give him a signed copy to keep, and confirm there will be no second, different contract at destination (no contract substitution). The sector switch from construction to domestic work with no written amendment is itself disqualifying until corrected in writing.
5) Apply the EMPLOYER-PAYS principle (ILO C181 Art. 7; ILO General Principles & Operational Guidelines for Fair Recruitment, 2016). The worker should pay no recruitment fees or related costs; a fee equal to ~8 months' salary, financed by a high-interest loan secured on family land, is a debt-bondage setup and must be treated as illegitimate.
6) Advise Ramesh NOT to surrender his passport and to retrieve it. Pre-departure document retention is unlawful (ICRMW Art. 21) and is itself a forced-labour indicator.
7) Pre-departure briefing: give him the origin-country migrant-resource-centre and regulator hotlines, the destination-country embassy/labour-attaché contact, and a plain-language explanation that he can refuse and report without losing standing.
8) Route through an independent verifier (a migrant resource centre or origin-state pre-departure orientation) so a neutral party confirms recruiter, employer, contract, and fee BEFORE any ticket is issued.

**Remedies.**
- Administrative complaint to the origin-country labour-migration regulator against the agency AND the unlicensed sub-agent — seek license suspension/revocation, blacklisting, and disruption of the broker chain
- Recruitment-fee reimbursement / full refund under the employer-pays principle (ILO C181 Art. 7; ILO General Principles & Operational Guidelines for Fair Recruitment, 2016; recruitment-fee definition, 2019)
- Voiding / cancellation of the recruitment debt — debts arising from illegal worker-paid fees are unenforceable; seek to nullify the high-interest loan and release the family land pledged as security
- Cancel/withdraw the deployment before departure at no penalty, with the deposit/fee returned
- Immediate return of the retained passport and provision of a full, readable copy of any contract (ICRMW Art. 21)
- Civil claim for fraud / misrepresentation (deceptive recruitment, contract substitution, undisclosed sector and wage change)
- Criminal referral where deception + debt bondage + document retention cross the threshold of a trafficking / forced-labour offence (ILO Forced Labour Convention C029 and 2014 Protocol P029; applicable origin- and destination-state anti-trafficking statutes)
- Referral to a migrant resource centre, legal aid, and origin-state pre-departure support for independent verification and ongoing protection; flag as an at-risk/potential-victim case if the worker still wishes to migrate through a verified, fee-free channel
- Preserve recruitment evidence for the complaint without exposing the worker to retaliation: job advertisement, fee receipts, the loan agreement, sub-agent/agency identities and contact details, and any messages documenting the promised-vs-actual terms

## Transit

**Scenario.** Composite, synthetic — no real person, agency, or case. "R." is a composite worker who accepted a hotel-cleaning job abroad through a village sub-agent and paid a placement fee that put the family into debt (recruitment + payment_and_debt stages, already behind them). R. is now mid-journey. After flying from the origin-country capital to a third-country transit hub, the group of six workers was met at arrivals by a man none of them had spoken to before. He collected all six passports and the printed contracts "to process the work visas," and took everyone's phones "so they don't get lost in transit." The group was driven to a guesthouse and told to wait two days for an onward flight. At the guesthouse R. learned that the destination city and the employer were not the ones named in the signed contract, and that a new "border and visa-processing fee" had been added to what R. already owes. The escort coached the group to tell immigration at the next airport that they were tourists visiting relatives. R. still has a cheap second phone hidden and manages to send one message: what do I do?

**Indicators.** passport_retention — an unknown escort collects all six workers' passports at the transit-country airport 'to process the work visas'; document_control — the signed contracts and onward tickets are held by the escort; the worker no longer holds any of their own paperwork; deceptive_recruitment — the destination city, employer, and job differ from the signed contract, surfacing only now, mid-journey, at the guesthouse; movement_restriction — the group is held in a transit guesthouse and moved under escort, not free to leave or travel onward on their own; isolation — phones are confiscated 'so they don't get lost'; no family contact, unfamiliar country and language; abuse_of_vulnerability — irregular and shifting immigration status plus debt at home leave the worker wholly dependent on the escort; fee_bondage / debt_bondage — a new 'border and visa-processing fee' is stacked onto the existing recruitment debt mid-transit; jurisdiction_shopping — routing through a third transit country and coaching workers to enter as 'tourists' sidesteps origin-state exit screening and destination work-visa controls; case_signal — the clustered indicators amount to a referable trafficking-in-transit signal

**Stage-appropriate action.** Transit is a movement phase, so the correct response is SAFETY-FIRST — not evidence-building (that belongs to the exploitation stage) and not a push to exit or prosecute. Do NOT confront or tip off the escort or broker: during a hand-off the worker can be moved, abandoned, or harmed if the controllers sense exposure. Concretely: (1) help R. pin down where they physically are and which border/airport they pass through next; (2) keep the discreet channel on the hidden second phone, and agree a simple check-in cadence plus a safe word that means "I'm in danger, call for help"; (3) have R. quietly photograph the passport, visa, contract, and ticket before they are taken, and note who is holding the originals; (4) give the non-punitive help points available at the next control point — the transit/destination country's national anti-trafficking hotline, airport border-force or immigration trafficking screening, the origin country's embassy/consulate or labour attaché, and IOM — and explain that a trafficking victim has the right to seek protection at the border, must not be penalized for irregular entry, and cannot be returned into danger (non-refoulement); (5) advise R. not to sign anything they do not understand, to memorize a couple of emergency numbers, and to keep the hidden phone if it is safe to do so. The goal at this stage is to keep the person safe and identifiable so they can be reached and assisted at the next checkpoint — not to assemble a prosecution file.

**Remedies.**
- Consular/embassy assistance from the origin country: emergency contact, an emergency travel document if the passport has been confiscated, and emergency repatriation support.
- Referral into the transit/destination country's National Referral Mechanism (NRM) for formal victim identification and a recovery-and-reflection period (Council of Europe / Warsaw Convention Art. 13), with non-punishment for immigration or status offenses (Art. 26).
- Non-penalized border screening under the UN Trafficking (Palermo) Protocol with non-refoulement — the worker must not be prosecuted for irregular entry or returned into the hands of the controllers.
- IOM Assisted Voluntary Return and Reintegration (AVRR) if the worker chooses to go home, plus safe emergency accommodation in the transit country through a local anti-trafficking shelter.
- Recovery of the confiscated identity and travel documents — document retention is itself a forced-labour indicator under ILO C029 and unlawful confiscation under ICRMW Art. 21.
- Filing the worker-paid fees and the route/job deception as recruitment-rights violations: ILO C181 Art. 7 (no worker-paid recruitment fees) and the ILO General Principles & Operational Guidelines for Fair Recruitment (2016), and reporting the sub-agent to the origin-state recruitment regulator.
- Connection to the origin-state migrant resource center / labour attaché at the destination and preservation of evidence now (fee receipts, contract photos, message logs) for a later civil claim to recover illegal fees and unpaid transit costs.

## Exploitation

**Scenario.** Composite scenario (fully synthetic — no real persons, employers, or cases). "Lan" is a composite migrant worker recruited in her South/Southeast Asian home village for a "packing and finishing" job at a textile factory in a destination-country industrial zone. She signed a contract at home promising a fixed monthly wage, an 8-hour day, one rest day a week, and free housing. Three months in, the reality is different and the harm is now being EXTRACTED AT THE WORKPLACE ITSELF — this is the exploitation stage, defined by the conditions, wages, and hours of the job rather than by how she was recruited or how she is held. WAGES: she is paid in cash, irregularly, at roughly half the contracted figure, with opaque deductions for "food," "lodging," "tools," and "repayment of placement costs"; for two months she received nothing, told the money "went to her outstanding debt." HOURS: 14–16 hour shifts, six and a half days a week, mandatory unpaid overtime before shipment deadlines, and production targets that cannot be met without staying late off the clock. CONDITIONS: a 10–12 person dormitory above the production floor, rationed drinking water, shared sanitation; factory doors locked "for security" during shifts; fines for talking, for bathroom breaks beyond a quota, and for missing targets; no protective equipment on the chemical finishing line; a supervisor who screams at and has slapped workers. Control mechanisms (debt leverage, retained documents, threats) sit in the background, but the defining feature of THIS stage is the ongoing exploitation through wages, hours, and working/living conditions.

**Indicators.** Withholding and non-payment of wages — two months unpaid, pay at roughly half the contracted amount (ILO forced-labour indicator: withholding of wages; ILO C095 Protection of Wages); Unlawful, opaque wage deductions tied to 'placement debt' the worker cannot verify or pay down (debt bondage; illegal deductions under ILO C095 / R85); Excessive and forced overtime — 14–16h days, ~6.5 days/week, mandatory unpaid overtime (ILO indicator: excessive overtime; breach of working-time and weekly-rest standards); Sub-minimum, irregular, cash-only pay with no payslips and no transparent wage record (ILO indicator: abusive working conditions; C095 wage-statement duty); Abusive living and working conditions — overcrowded on-site dormitory, rationed water, no PPE on a chemical line (ILO indicator: abusive working and living conditions); Restriction of movement at work — doors locked during shifts, fines for leaving the workstation (ILO indicator: restriction of movement); Physical violence, intimidation, and a punitive fine system used as wage control — slapping, screaming, penalty deductions (ILO indicators: violence; intimidation and threats); Contract substitution — actual wages, hours, and housing materially worse than the contract signed at origin (deception surfacing in the workplace; breach of equal-treatment in conditions of work, ICRMW Art.25)

**Stage-appropriate action.** DOCUMENT + INFORM OF RIGHTS. Because the worker is still inside the workplace, the right move is to build the evidentiary record and her rights-awareness and to assess danger — NOT to push an immediate escape or confrontation, which belongs to the control/safe-exit stage and, without a safety plan, can trigger retaliation, wage forfeiture, or an "absconding" charge. DOCUMENT: safely preserve the original signed contract/offer; keep a private, hidden, off-device-backed log of actual daily start/end times; record every payment with its date, amount, and each named deduction; photograph conditions (locked doors, the dormitory, missing PPE, rationed water) only where it is safe and without exposing co-workers' identities; log the date, words, and actor for every threat, fine, or act of violence; note co-workers willing to corroborate without coercing them; and get copies of all evidence to a trusted contact or NGO so the employer cannot seize the only record. INFORM OF RIGHTS: explain the legal minimum wage, maximum weekly hours, mandatory rest day and overtime premium, the prohibition on illegal deductions, and the Employer-Pays Principle (a worker should never be charged recruitment fees); explain that withheld wages, unpayable "debt" deductions, locked doors, and threats are recognized ILO forced-labour indicators (ILO C029 and Protocol P029), that she is a potential VICTIM entitled to protection — not an offender — regardless of immigration status (non-punishment principle); and give her safe, employer-undetectable ways to reach the destination-country labour inspectorate/wage-complaint mechanism, her embassy or labour attaché, and a migrant-worker or anti-trafficking helpline.

**Remedies.**
- Back-wage recovery — unpaid wages, the shortfall up to the legal minimum, and unpaid overtime — through a wage-and-hour / labour claim
- Refund of unlawful deductions and any recruitment fees under the Employer-Pays Principle (ILO C181 + ILO General Principles & Operational Guidelines for Fair Recruitment 2016; ILO C095 on permissible deductions)
- Labour inspectorate complaint and, where the indicators meet the threshold, referral into the National Referral Mechanism for formal victim identification and protection
- Compensation, restitution, and civil damages for forced labour (ILO P029 Art.4 access to remedy) — claims against both employer and recruiter, and recovery from recruiter bonds or wage-guarantee funds where the destination state requires them
- Consular/embassy assistance and, if the worker chooses, remedy-first repatriation rather than a penalty-laden 'absconding' exit
- Protection safeguards — non-punishment for immigration or 'absconding' status, a reflection-and-recovery period, and a temporary residence/work permit to pursue claims where available
- Supply-chain remedy where a brand or buyer is involved — grievance and remediation under business-and-human-rights and supply-chain due-diligence laws

## Coercion / Control

**Scenario.** Composite, no real entities or PII. "Bishnu," a composite 28-year-old construction worker, left a village in a South Asian origin country after a sub-agent promised a welding job in a Gulf-style kafala destination at three times local pay (composite South Asia-to-Gulf corridor). He is now five months into the placement and fully under the trafficker's control.

On arrival his sponsor took his passport "for safekeeping with the company office" and never returned it. He was handed a contract different from the one he signed at home — lower wage, longer hours — and told the recruitment fee he borrowed at high interest from a village lender, plus "visa, ticket, and medical" costs, is a debt he must clear from his salary before he sees any take-home pay. Three months of wages have been "held."

He sleeps in a fenced labor camp, eleven men to a room, is bused to the site under a supervisor, and cannot leave the compound alone. His phone is collected at night and the camp boss scrolls his messages. A roommate who complained was reported to police as "absconding," detained, and deported with his debt unpaid; Bishnu is told the same will happen to him — and that his family will be told he is a thief — if he contacts anyone. He has spoken to no one outside the camp in weeks and does not speak the local language. This is the control stage: the worker is already placed, and movement, money, documents, and communication are all held by the controller.

**Indicators.** passport_retention — sponsor took the passport on arrival and never returned it; document_control — contract substitution: a different, worse contract than the one signed at origin; debt_bondage — must 'work off' an inflated recruitment/visa/ticket/medical debt before any take-home pay; fee_bondage — worker-borrowed recruitment fee at high interest, violating the employer-pays principle; withheld_wages / wage_theft — three months of wages 'held' and not paid; salary_deduction — open-ended deductions for 'visa, ticket, medical'; movement_restriction — fenced camp, cannot leave alone, bused to site under a supervisor; isolation — phone confiscated at night, no contact with the outside, language isolation; abuse_of_vulnerability — compounded migrant status, debt, sponsorship (kafala) dependency, and language barrier; excessive_overtime — longer hours than the signed contract; retaliation_risk — threats of an 'absconding' police report, detention, deportation, and defamation to family; case_signal — a roommate was already reported and deported, signaling a systemic, repeatable control pattern

**Stage-appropriate action.** Control stage = SAFE EXIT + EVIDENCE, with the worker's physical safety first — never confrontation, never an impulsive escape. Surveillance and 'absconding' laws mean an open complaint or unplanned run can escalate threats and end in detention/deportation, so the action sequence is: (1) Safety triage and a covert channel — assume the monitored phone is read; establish contact only through a clean/borrowed device or a trusted outside person, using a pre-agreed code word; do nothing that tips off the camp boss. (2) Connect to a lifeline BEFORE moving — the origin-country embassy/consulate labour attaché in the destination, the national anti-trafficking hotline, and a vetted migrant-worker NGO or shelter; let them set the timing and a safe pickup so he is received into protection, not into an 'absconding' arrest. (3) Preserve evidence discreetly — photograph or note the substituted contract, any wage/debt ledger, recruiter and sub-agent messages, the passport or its number, camp conditions, names, dates, and the roommate-deportation precedent; store copies off-device with the trusted contact. (4) Plan the exit around a safe window with embassy/NGO escort, and recover the passport through official channels — or have the embassy issue an emergency travel document if it cannot be retrieved. (5) Frame him as a victim, not an offender — invoke the non-punishment principle so any immigration/'absconding' status is treated as a consequence of trafficking. Do NOT advise him to simply confront the employer or flee on his own.

**Remedies.**
- Emergency protection: national anti-trafficking hotline + a police anti-trafficking unit (not ordinary immigration enforcement) + the origin-country embassy labour attaché; immediate placement in a safe shelter
- Passport recovery: document retention is unlawful in most jurisdictions — demand return via the labour authority/embassy, or obtain an emergency travel document if it is withheld
- Debt cancellation: recruitment-fee debt and 'visa/ticket/medical' deductions are unlawful under the ILO employer-pays principle (C181 + the 2016 Fair Recruitment principles); debt bondage is a forced-labour indicator (C029 + Protocol P029) and is void and unenforceable
- Wage recovery: claim the three months of withheld wages, unlawful deductions, and unpaid overtime through the labour court/ministry or wage-protection mechanism
- Non-punishment + status relief: invoke the non-punishment principle (UN Trafficking/Palermo Protocol; ILO P029 Art.4) for a reflection/recovery period and a temporary residence or victim visa instead of detention or 'absconding' charges
- Criminal referral: report the sponsor, employer, recruitment agency, and origin-side sub-agent for trafficking/forced labour; trigger labour-inspection and licence action against the agency in BOTH destination and origin states
- Compensation/restitution: civil and statutory compensation for unpaid wages, the unlawful fee debt, and harm (P029 Art.4(b) right to remedies)
- Voluntary repatriation + reintegration (if chosen): safe return with debt-relief, legal aid, and reintegration support to reduce re-trafficking risk

## Exit/Aftermath

**Scenario.** Composite corridor: a South Asia-to-Gulf domestic-work corridor. All persons, agencies, employers, numbers, and case details are fictional/synthetic (no real entities or PII).

Two years ago "Sita" (a 28-year-old composite worker, sole earner for her family) was recruited in her village by a sub-agent promising well-paid housekeeping abroad. Her family borrowed at high interest to pay a recruitment fee repackaged across "medical," "training," and "service" line items. On arrival her passport was taken "for processing," her contact with home was monitored, and her wages were deducted to "repay" costs she supposedly owed. After ~20 months of unpaid 16-18 hour days, confinement to the household, and escalating threats, she fled during a household move, leaving her passport and belongings behind.

She is now at the EXIT/AFTERMATH stage. A community volunteer from her own country brought her to her embassy's migrant-worker shelter. She presents with: no passport or ID (the employer still holds them); ~20 months of unpaid wages owed; untreated injuries and exhaustion; an outstanding high-interest loan at home that her family is now being pressured to repay; and acute fear that the employer has filed (or will file) an "absconding"/"runaway" complaint that would reclassify her as an undocumented immigration offender liable to detention, fines, and deportation rather than recognize her as a victim. Meanwhile the original sub-agent is already messaging her — "I have a new job in another country, fly out and we'll fix your papers" — an active re-trafficking solicitation. She has no savings, no documents, and pressure from home to keep earning.

**Indicators.** passport_retention — the employer still holds her passport; she escaped undocumented and cannot prove identity or legal status; document_control — no ID of any kind, which exposes her to the 'absconding'/undocumented-offender reclassification; withheld_wages — roughly 20 months of pay owed and never disbursed; wage_theft — structural non-payment compounded by fee-disguised salary deductions; debt_bondage — outstanding high-interest origin loan still coercing her and her family, driving the pressure to keep earning; retaliation_risk — the employer's threatened 'absconding'/'runaway' counter-complaint and her exposure to detention/deportation; abuse_of_vulnerability — undocumented, injured, penniless, traumatized sole-earner with no safe fallback; movement_restriction — was confined to the household; exit was only possible by fleeing during a move; isolation — monitored communication, cut off from peers and from home; deceptive_recruitment — the original job, pay, and fee terms were misrepresented; this is the evidentiary thread to document now for the wage/criminal claim; case_signal — meets victim-identification thresholds: must be referred into the NRM, not deported; re-trafficking solicitation (maps to retaliation_risk + a fresh deceptive_recruitment attempt) — the sub-agent's live 'new job abroad, we'll fix your papers' message

**Stage-appropriate action.** Exit/aftermath action = IDENTIFICATION + REMEDY + SAFEGUARD. Critically, this is NOT the stage for upstream logic: do not coach her to "verify the recruiter's licence before signing" (recruitment), to "keep your documents and assert your rights at work" (employment/exploitation), and above all do not treat her "absconding" status as the operative fact and route her to immigration enforcement. She has already exited; the job now is to identify, make her whole, and prevent re-victimization.

(1) IDENTIFY — Treat her as a presumptive forced-labour/trafficking victim and run formal victim identification through a National Referral Mechanism against the ILO forced-labour indicators. Invoke the non-punishment / non-criminalization principle (UN Trafficking Protocol; ILO 2014 Forced Labour Protocol P029 Art. 4(2)) so the "absconding" complaint cannot push her into detention/deportation, and grant a recovery-and-reflection period before she must decide anything.

(2) REMEDY — Take a trauma-informed statement with a qualified, independent interpreter; preserve evidence (contract, fee receipts, deduction records, the agent's messages) for both a wage claim and a criminal complaint; recover her passport and documents; file for ~20 months of unpaid wages plus compensation/restitution and reimbursement of illegal recruitment fees (employer-pays principle); and provide medical care, psychosocial support, safe shelter, free legal aid, and — if she chooses to pursue the case — a temporary residence/work permit instead of immediate removal.

(3) SAFEGUARD — Prevent re-trafficking: never hand her back to the same agent/employer network; treat the sub-agent's "new job abroad" message as an active re-recruitment attempt and report it; arrange safe, voluntary, dignified repatriation as a warm handoff to a vetted origin-country anti-trafficking NGO (not a one-way deportation ticket); address the abusive origin debt through debt counseling/relief and legal aid; provide a reintegration package (livelihood/skills, financial inclusion, stigma-aware family mediation, periodic follow-up); and refer the recruiter and employer for investigation and blacklisting so the next worker is protected.

**Remedies.**
- Formal victim identification via a National Referral Mechanism; presumptive-victim status pending full assessment
- Non-punishment for immigration/'absconding' offenses committed as a direct consequence of being trafficked (UN Trafficking Protocol; ILO P029 Art. 4(2))
- Recovery-and-reflection period; temporary residence/work permit if she elects to pursue a claim, instead of immediate removal
- Recovery of the retained passport and personal documents
- Back-wages claim for ~20 months of unpaid work plus reimbursement of illegal recruitment fees (employer-pays principle)
- Compensation/restitution through civil action and/or a criminal victim-compensation fund
- Medical care, trauma-informed psychosocial support, and safe shelter
- Free legal aid and qualified independent interpretation; evidence preservation for a criminal complaint against employer and recruiter
- Safe, voluntary, dignified repatriation with a warm handoff to a vetted origin-country anti-trafficking NGO (not deportation)
- Debt relief / counseling and legal aid against the abusive high-interest origin loan
- Reintegration package: livelihood/vocational support, financial inclusion, stigma-aware family and community mediation, and periodic follow-up monitoring
- Re-trafficking safeguards: report the live re-recruitment solicitation, blacklist/refer the sub-agent and employer for investigation, and brief her on ethical/regular re-migration channels if she later chooses to migrate
- Frameworks engaged (real, load-bearing — not composite): UN Trafficking Protocol Arts. 6-8; ILO C029 + 2014 Protocol P029 Art. 4 (remedies + non-prosecution); ILO Forced Labour Indicators; ILO C181 + Fair Recruitment principles (employer-pays); ICRMW; IRIS ethical-recruitment standard
