## Scope

No other sectors are fabricated. The **cross-sector layer is real and portable**: the ILO 11 indicators of forced labour and the core legal instruments are sector-neutral by design, so the closing summary states what is universal versus what is specific to domestic work, and how the same spine extends to other sectors.

## How to use this reference

- The **ILO 11 indicators of forced labour** are the universal detection spine; each sector section maps its case-level signals to that numbered list.
- **Known risks** = structural drivers (why the sector is hidden/high-risk). **Indicators / red flags** = case-level observables for fast triage. **Improvements** = framework-anchored remediation ("what good looks like").
- Audit questions are **non-leading and narrative** ("walk me through…"). Treat **non-mention as UNKNOWN, never as "no."** Age is a **hard interrupt**.

## Sector 1 — Domestic work / care in private homes

### Profile
Live-in and live-out domestic workers and home-based carers (childcare, elder/disability care, cooks, cleaners, household drivers) employed by **private households**. Heavy concentration of **migrant women** on corridors such as Indonesia / Philippines / Sri Lanka / Nepal / Bangladesh / Ethiopia / Kenya / Myanmar into the **Gulf states, Lebanon, Jordan, Malaysia, Singapore and Hong Kong**. Governed sector-specifically by **ILO C189 + R201**, overlaid on **ILO C29 + P029**, the **Palermo Protocol**, and the **ILO 11 indicators**.

### Known risks (structural drivers — why exploitation stays hidden)
- **Workplace beyond labour inspection.** The worksite is a private dwelling inspectors generally cannot enter without consent or a warrant — removing the single most important external detection mechanism. C189 Art. 17 asks states to build privacy-respecting home-visit/inspection measures; few do.
- **Legal exclusion or lesser protection.** Domestic workers are wholly or partly carved out of national labour law (minimum wage, hours, weekly rest, OSH, social security). C189 + R201 exist to close this gap; ratification is limited.
- **Sponsorship (kafala) ties legal status to one employer.** The worker cannot change employer or leave the country without the sponsor's consent; "absconding" is criminalized — the immigration architecture itself supplies the C29 "menace of any penalty." Domestic workers are often excluded from the partial kafala reforms granted to other sectors.
- **Live-in collapses home and workplace.** On-call 24/7, no defined hours, interrupted rest, no private space, structural isolation. C189 Art. 9 requires the worker be free to decide whether to live in and free to leave during rest — routinely breached.
- **Strong gender dimension.** Overwhelmingly women and girls; compounded exposure to sexual harassment/violence (C190) and gendered control. CEDAW GR26 flags this group as distinctly high-risk.
- **Recruitment-fee debt bondage.** Arrival owing several months' pay; "salary-deduction" schemes assign the first 6-9 months to the agency — breaching C181 Art. 7, EPP/IRIS, and C189 Art. 15. The debt is the lock that converts a placement into bondage.
- **Child domestic work.** Children placed in third-party households (incl. restavek-type "fostering") are hidden; hazardous/under-age domestic work is a worst form of child labour (C182), and under Palermo Art. 3(c) the means element is not required for a minor.
- **Contract substitution and "transfer" markets.** Terms signed at origin differ from or are replaced by the real destination terms; workers are passed or "sold" between households without consent, eroding free choice of employment (C189 Art. 7).
- **Sector salience.** The ILO consistently identifies domestic work as among the sectors most affected by forced labour and trafficking, precisely because isolation, legal exclusion, debt, document control and tied status co-occur in a single household.

### Indicators → red flags → improvements (mapped to the ILO 11)

| ILO indicator (#) | Red flag (observable, fast triage) | Improvement / remediation (framework-anchored) |
|---|---|---|
| **#7 Retention of documents** | Employer/agency holds passport + residence permit "for safekeeping"; worker cannot produce them on demand | Worker keeps own documents at all times; immediate return if held; lockable personal storage (C189 Art. 9; **ICRMW Art. 21**) |
| **#3 Restriction of movement / #4 Isolation** | Locked in; never seen leaving alone; no day off or confined on it; no own phone/SIM; sole worker, language barrier | Freedom to leave the home during rest and to choose whether to live in; retained personal phone; outside contact (C189 Art. 9) |
| **#9 Debt bondage** | Arrived owing money; first 6-9 months' wages assigned to agency; "can't leave until debt is worked off" | **Employer Pays Principle** — zero worker-paid fees; refund fees taken; ban salary-deduction repayment (C181 Art. 7; C189 Art. 15; IRIS; Supp. Slavery Conv. 1956 Art. 1(a)) |
| **#8 Withholding of wages** | "Salary kept safe"; lump-sum-at-end; paid to agency/third party; deductions for food/lodging/"breakages" below minimum; non/late pay | Written contract in the worker's language; **direct, regular, at-least-monthly** payment to the worker's own account at/above minimum; in-kind only where lawful, capped, consented (C189 Art. 11-12) |
| **#11 Excessive overtime** | No defined hours; on-call day and night; no weekly rest | Defined hours; guaranteed **≥24 consecutive hours weekly rest**; bounded, compensated on-call/night work (C189 Art. 10) |
| **#10 Abusive working/living conditions** | Sleeps in storeroom/kitchen/hallway/child's room; no private or lockable space; food deprivation; no OSH | Private, adequate, lockable accommodation; adequate food; occupational safety and health measures (C189 Art. 10; R201) |
| **#6 Intimidation and threats** | Threats of deportation, of being reported "absconding," of withheld final pay/return ticket, of corridor blacklisting, against family at origin | Decriminalize "absconding"; grievance access without status loss; no retaliation; the "menace of any penalty" removed (C29 + P029) |
| **#1 Abuse of vulnerability** | Indebted; sponsor-tied/irregular status; no local language or network; total dependence on employer for food/housing/status/exit | De-link visa from a single sponsor; lawful right to change employer; independent support/hotline access (Palermo Art. 3; kafala reform) |
| **#5 Physical and sexual violence** | Signs of assault; sexual abuse/harassment by household members (heightened by isolation + gender profile) | Zero-tolerance harassment policy; confidential, female-staffed reporting channel; safe-exit pathway (C190; CEDAW GR26) |
| **#2 Deception** | Contract signed at origin differs from real terms; deceived about employer, wages, duties, rest, or destination | Transparent, verifiable terms in the worker's language before departure; ban contract substitution (C189 Art. 7) |
| Structural — tied status (kafala) | Visa tied to one sponsor; cannot change employer or exit without consent; "absconding" criminalized | Right to change employer and to exit; decriminalize leaving; privacy-respecting inspection/home-visit mechanisms (C189 Art. 17) |

### Audit questions (trauma-informed, non-leading)
1. **Documents** (#7 / ICRMW Art. 21): "Tell me about your passport and residence permit — where are they kept right now, and what would happen if you needed them today?"
2. **Recruitment and fees** (#9 / C181 Art. 7 / EPP): "Tell me how you came to this job, and about any money you paid or owe to anyone — an agency, a sub-agent, or the employer — in your home country or here." Reconcile the **full total cost including interest**, and whether any of it was **deducted from wages** (C189 Art. 15).
3. **Wages** (#8 / C189 Art. 11-12): "Walk me through how you actually get paid — the amount, how often, in what form, and anything taken out." Establish written contract in the worker's language, pay at/above minimum, and any lump-sum-at-end or wage-withholding.
4. **Hours and rest** (#11 / C189 Art. 10): "Walk me through a normal day, from waking to going to sleep, and tell me about your days off." Establish ≥24 consecutive hours weekly rest and defined hours, or on-call day and night.
5. **Movement and the rest day** (#3/#4 / C189 Art. 9): "If you wanted to go out on your own — to a shop, a clinic, a place of worship, or to see someone — what is that like for you?" Establish free movement and free choice of whether to live in.
6. **Living conditions** (#10): "Describe where you sleep and where you eat, and how those connect to your pay." Establish private, adequate, lockable accommodation and whether in-kind deductions are lawful, capped and genuinely consented.
7. **Communication and isolation** (#4): "How do you keep in touch with your family and friends, and do you have your own phone?"
8. **Menace of penalty** (C29 / #6): "Tell me what you understood would happen if you stopped working, or wanted to leave or change employer."
9. **Sponsorship and exit** (structural): establish whether the visa is tied to this single employer (kafala), whether the worker can lawfully change employer or exit the country without the employer's consent, and whether "absconding" exposes them to arrest or deportation.
10. **Violence and harassment** (open, gentle, accept silence; #5 / C190): "Describe how you are treated in the household." Treat non-mention as **UNKNOWN**, never as "no."
11. **Age** (hard interrupt): establish date of birth. *[Source truncated here; completed from citations already present in the source:]* if the person is **under 18**, the **means element is not required** (Palermo Art. 3(c)) and hazardous/under-age domestic work is a **worst form of child labour** (C182) — escalate immediately as a child-protection matter rather than continuing a standard labour interview.

## Cross-sector summary: universal vs sector-specific indicators

### Universal (apply in every migrant-labor sector)
- **The ILO 11 indicators** (abuse of vulnerability, deception, restriction of movement, isolation, physical/sexual violence, intimidation/threats, retention of documents, withholding of wages, debt bondage, abusive conditions, excessive overtime) are **sector-neutral by design** — built to detect forced labour anywhere.
- **The legal spine is universal:** C29 + P029 (involuntariness under "menace of any penalty"); the Palermo act/means/purpose test (Art. 3(c) removing "means" for minors); C181 + EPP/IRIS on recruitment fees; and the document-confiscation prohibition (ICRMW Art. 21).
- **Debt bondage as the conversion mechanism** — recruitment-fee debt and salary-deduction schemes turning a placement into bondage — recurs across migration corridors regardless of sector.

### Sector-specific to domestic work (why the same indicators land harder here)
- **Workplace is a private home** → outside routine labour inspection. The single largest detection gap, and the most distinctive feature of this sector (C189 Art. 17).
- **Live-in arrangement collapses home and workplace** → the isolation (#4) and excessive-overtime (#11) indicators become structurally extreme and continuous (C189 Art. 9-10).
- **Legal exclusion** → domestic workers carved out of general labour law; C189 + R201 exist to close exactly this gap.
- **Tied immigration status (kafala)**, with domestic workers frequently excluded even from the partial reforms extended to other sectors.
- **Strong gender dimension** → overwhelmingly women and girls; heightened sexual-violence exposure makes C190 and CEDAW GR26 sector-salient.
- **Child domestic work / restavek-type fostering** → hidden minors; C182 and Palermo Art. 3(c).
- **Contract substitution and "transfer/sale" between households** → erodes any freely-chosen-employment basis (C189 Art. 7).

### Portability note
To assess any additional sector, **hold the ILO 11 indicator spine and the legal core constant** and swap in that sector's structural specifics (e.g., inspection access, accommodation/housing model, recruitment-chain depth, and corridor profile). Those sector-specific particulars are intentionally **not invented here** because the source supplied only the domestic-work sector; this document is structured so further sectors slot in beneath the same universal indicator framework.
