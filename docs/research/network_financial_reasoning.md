# Mapping the Network and the Money: Actor Roles and Network Signals in Migrant-Worker Trafficking

> **Analytical guidance for a safety reasoner.** Migrant-worker trafficking is, first and last, a **profit enterprise**. Yet baseline ("gold") reasoning considers the *network* in only ~8.5% of cases and the *money* in only ~3.6%. It defaults to a single-victim, single-bad-employer read. This document exists to correct that bias: to make the reasoner habitually ask **"who else is in this structure?"** and **"where does the money enter and exit?"** Every scenario below is **composite** — no real people, agencies, addresses, or case numbers.

---

## 1. The two corrections this guidance enforces

| Default (isolated) read | Required (network + money) read |
|---|---|
| One worker was deceived by one employer. | One worker is one *node* of a repeating structure — recruiter, financier, and facilitator are upstream and invisible. |
| The harm is the abuse (long hours, confinement). | The harm is the **business model**: the abuse *is* the margin. Find the fee, the deduction, the debt, the kickback. |
| Resolution = help this worker / refuse the request. | Resolution = also surface the **reusable infrastructure** (shared recruiter, shared lender, shared shell) that produces the next ten victims. |

Anchor every analysis to the **Palermo triad**: **Act** (recruit / transport / transfer / harbour / receive) × **Means** (deception, coercion, abuse of vulnerability, debt) × **Purpose** (exploitation for profit). The actor roles below *are* the "Act" elements, run as a business.

---

## 2. Actor and role map

Seven functional roles. In a small operation one person wears several hats; in a mature network each is a distinct, specialized node — and that specialization is itself a network signal.

| Role | Function in the chain | Primary profit mechanism | Strongest network tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Recruiter** (principal / licensed agency) | Visible front; sources workers, signs contracts, holds the origin-state license, interfaces with destination. | Placement/"service" fees above legal caps; kickbacks from destination agency; salary deductions. | One agency feeding *many* employers; a **license that is rented or sublet** (license laundering). |
| **Sub-agent** (village broker) | Capillary layer; finds and grooms candidates in the worker's community. | Per-head commission from the recruiter **and** a separate charge to the worker (double-dipping); often advances the first loan. | Many workers from one village/region tracing to one broker; broker who vanishes post-departure. |
| **Employer** (end-user / principal) | Receives and controls the labour. | Underpayment, unpaid overtime, "cost-recovery" deductions; the core margin is **coerced labour below market**. | Hires through an interposed manpower firm so it can **deny the employment relationship**. |
| **Transporter** (mover) | Domestic transport, flights/boats, border crossing, internal moves. | Transport folded into the debt; per-head smuggling fees on irregular routes. | Same travel agency / handler booking staggered **batches** of workers. |
| **Harbourer** (receiver / keeper) | Confines and "warehouses" workers — staging house, labour camp, dormitory, controlled accommodation. | Accommodation charges deducted from wages; maintains a **labour pool as inventory**. | One address linked to **multiple employers and multiple recruiters** = a pooling hub. |
| **Financier** (capital + money mover) | Pre-finances recruitment, runs the lending side of debt bondage, launders proceeds. | Coercive interest; fee-splitting; laundering-service margin. **This is where the crime is most clearly a profit enterprise.** | A **common lender/guarantor** across many unrelated workers; salaries routed through a third-party account. |
| **Facilitator** (professional enabler) | Manufactures legitimacy: forged/expedited visas, contracts, fitness certs; corrupt pass-through; shell-company formation. | Fee per document / per favor. | **Same notary, clinic, contract template, or formation agent** across "unrelated" employers. |

**Teaching point:** the **financier** and **facilitator** are the two roles the default read almost always omits — yet they are the load-bearing, reusable parts of the network. The employer is replaceable; the lender and the document mill are not. Always ask: *who fronted the money, and who made the paperwork look real?*

## 3. The money map — where the profit is taken

Trace value at every node. A trafficking situation is rarely one charge; it is a **stacked extraction** along the corridor (origin XX → destination YY). Name the mechanism, don't just label it "exploitation."

**Upstream (origin) extraction**
- **Excess placement fees** — worker pays for the job, violating the **Employer-Pays Principle** (ILO Fair Recruitment Guidelines 2016; ILO C181 Art. 7 bars charging fees to workers). *Any* worker-borne recruitment cost is a primary red flag.
- **Fee camouflage** — the fee is split and disguised as: *training fee, medical/fitness fee, processing fee, document/stamping fee, orientation fee, skill-test fee, welfare-fund contribution, insurance, uniform/tools, security/accommodation deposit, placement guarantee.* Multiple small "fees" summing past the cap is the signature.
- **Debt bondage** — an inflated loan for travel + fees at coercive interest. Test: **does the debt shrink as the worker is paid?** A debt engineered *not* to shrink is a profit engine, not a loan.

**Midstream (transit) extraction**
- Transport, visa, and "agency commission" rolled into the principal so the debt compounds before the first paycheck.
- **Contract substitution** — a fair contract signed at origin is swapped for a worse one at destination; the gap is margin. Keep **both** versions; the delta is evidence.

**Downstream (destination) extraction**
- **Wage withholding / deferred wages** ("paid at end of contract") — interest-free working capital for the employer and a powerful exit-blocker; the worker may never see the deferred balance.
- **Illegal deductions** — accommodation, food, transport, tools, "training cost recovery," and punitive **fines** (lateness, "damage," quota misses) clawed back out of pay until net wages approach zero.
- **Truck system / forced spending** — wages paid in scrip, or workers compelled to buy from a company store / use a company remittance channel at a markup, so part of the wage is recaptured immediately.
- **Document retention as leverage** — passport/ID held (an ILO forced-labour indicator). Not a direct charge, but it *protects every other extraction* by removing the worker's ability to leave or dispute.
- **Re-recruitment / renewal fees** — at contract end the worker is re-charged to renew or re-placed to a new employer, and the cycle restarts. The network monetizes the *same* worker repeatedly.

**Close the corridor.** Sum the extraction end-to-end and compare to two benchmarks: the **legal fee cap** for the XX→YY corridor and the **lawful minimum wage** at destination. The gap between "what was promised + what is lawful" and "what the worker nets" is the trafficking margin, expressed as a number. A reasoner that produces that number has done the money map; one that says only "the worker was exploited" has not.

## 4. When to suspect a network, not an isolated case

Because the network is structurally under-considered, **apply a deliberate prior: assume structure and rule it out, rather than assume an isolated case and stumble onto structure.** Trip the network hypothesis whenever **two or more** of the following are present:

**One-to-many tells (the core signal).**
- One recruiter / agency → **many employers**.
- One address (dormitory, staging house, PO box) → **many workers** or **many employers**.
- One lender / guarantor → **many unrelated debtors** (the financier fingerprint).
- One notary, clinic, contract template, or company-formation agent → **many "unrelated" firms** (the facilitator fingerprint).
- One origin village / region → **many placements** through the same broker.

**Specialization and role-separation.** When the recruiter, financier, transporter, and document provider are *distinct* parties (not one informal fixer), you are looking at a mature operation, not an opportunistic abuser.

**Repetition and batching.** Staggered **cohorts** moving on the same route, same handler, same template contract, sequential visa numbers, or recurring intake cycles.

**Denial architecture.** An **interposed manpower/labour-supply firm** sitting between the worker and the principal so the end-user can deny the employment relationship; layered subcontracting; a **sublet or rented license**; a shell with a registered address but no real operations.

**Cross-border money routing.** Recruitment financed from destination; wages or "fees" moving through third-party accounts, informal value transfer, or a jurisdiction unrelated to either the worker or the job.

> **Heuristic:** an isolated bad employer has a *victim*. A network has a *throughput*. If the facts imply a repeatable process — intake, financing, movement, placement, recapture — reason about the process, not just the person.

## 5. The follow-the-money checklist

Run this every time. It is ten ordered questions; each names the artifact to preserve. (Preserve raw documents **locally**; in any shareable artifact, generalize names and **hash** account/ID numbers — the privacy boundary in §6 is not optional.)

1. **Did money flow *from* the worker?** Any worker-paid recruitment cost is a primary red flag (Employer-Pays violation). *Artifact: receipts, transfer records, "fee" acknowledgements.*
2. **Enumerate every fee and its label.** List each charge and run camouflage detection (training/medical/processing/orientation/deposit/insurance…). *Artifact: itemized fee list with amounts and dates.*
3. **Is there a debt — and does it amortize?** Reconstruct principal, interest rate, and the balance trajectory. A balance that grows or stalls despite payments is debt bondage, not a loan. *Artifact: loan agreement, repayment ledger.*
4. **Who holds / financed the debt?** Identify the lender/guarantor — the **financier node**. *Artifact: lender identity, guarantor name, collateral terms.*
5. **Where do wages go?** Whose account receives them? Are they deferred, paid in scrip, or routed through a third party? *Artifact: payslips, bank/wallet account ownership, deferral terms.*
6. **What is deducted, by whom, under what authority?** Test each deduction for legality (consent, cap, written basis). *Artifact: deduction schedule.*
7. **Was the contract substituted?** Compare origin vs destination terms. *Artifact: both contract versions; the delta.*
8. **Map extraction across the corridor.** Place each charge at origin / transit / destination (XX→YY). *Artifact: corridor money map.*
9. **Cross-border / laundering signals.** Funnel accounts, mismatched payer jurisdiction, remittance capture, structuring, cash-only. *Artifact: payer/payee jurisdictions, channel.*
10. **Quantify and name the beneficiaries.** Total extraction vs legal cap and lawful wage; then ask **cui bono** — list every party who profited. *Artifact: the margin figure + a beneficiary roster → hand off to entity DD (§7).*

If steps 1–4 are blank in the source material, that absence is itself a finding: **the money was deliberately not asked about.** Flag it and state what evidence would close the gap.

## 6. How network + money reasoning changes the advice and the audit

### It changes the advice
- **Worker-facing (help the person first, always):** secure safety and the relevant hotline/rights first. *Then add the money-aware steps:* preserve financial evidence; do not repay coerced debt without advice (it may be unenforceable); document every deduction; understand that withheld wages and held documents are recoverable claims, not sunk costs.
- **Caseworker / NGO-facing:** treat the worker as an **index case**, not a closed case. Screen for the cohort (same broker / lender / address / corridor), map the corridor, and open a **financial-crime referral**, not only a labour-rights referral.
- **Regulator-facing:** pursue the **reusable infrastructure** — the license, the lender, the shell, the document mill — not only the end employer. Coordinate labour inspectorate **and** the financial intelligence unit **and** the origin-state counterpart, because the money and the workers cross the same border.

### It changes the audit
- Record not just the abuse but the **structure**: actors + roles, amounts, accounts, dates, and corridor (XX→YY), plus the matched **ILO forced-labour indicators** (debt bondage, wage withholding, document retention, abuse of vulnerability, deception). Persist these in the standard fact envelope with provenance and a content hash so the finding is reproducible.
- Record the **referral routing decision** (labour vs financial-crime vs cross-border) and the rationale.
- Record **confidence and the gap**: what is established, what is inferred, and the single piece of evidence that would confirm or deny the network hypothesis.

### The privacy boundary (non-negotiable)
Raw worker chats, IDs, contact details, and original documents stay **local**. Any artifact that leaves the device carries **generalized entities and hashed identifiers** only. Network reasoning expands *what we infer*; it does not expand *what we expose*.

## 7. Connection to entity due-diligence

The network + money read is the **front end of entity due-diligence**: it produces concrete entity leads — a recruiter, a financier, a facilitator, an interposed manpower firm, a pooling address. Entity DD then verifies and connects them. The handoff is propose-only: DD surfaces **leads for human verification**, never automated accusations.

For each named entity, screen against:
- **Recruitment-license registry (origin state):** Is the license valid? Active? **Sublet or rented** to another operator? A license appearing across many unrelated placements is the license-laundering tell.
- **Company registry + beneficial ownership:** Who actually owns/controls the manpower firm and the employer? Beneficial-ownership data (BODS-style `owns_or_controls` edges) exposes the **denial architecture** — e.g., the interposed firm and the principal sharing an owner.
- **Legal Entity Identifier (GLEIF LEI + `parent_of`):** corporate-family structure; shells with no operating footprint.
- **Sanctions / watchlists and forced-labour enforcement:** sanctions screening plus import/forced-labour actions (e.g., withhold-release-type orders, supply-chain enforcement lists) and prior labour-enforcement records.
- **Shared-attribute clustering:** cross-registry matching on shared officers, addresses, phones, formation agents, and notaries clusters "unrelated" firms into the **actual network**.

The output is a small set of **typed edges** — `recruits_for`, `lends_to`, `harbours_at`, `owns_or_controls`, `parent_of` — that turn one worker's account into a verifiable structure. This is precisely why the financier and facilitator (§2) matter: they are the **shared nodes** that link otherwise-disconnected cases, and they are exactly what registry-backed entity DD is built to find.

## 8. Composite worked scenarios

*All composite. Names (Maria, Ramesh, Sita) are labeled composites; corridors are placeholders.*

**Scenario A — "Free visa" domestic work (corridor XX→YY).**
*Surface read:* Maria (composite) has a cruel employer who won't let her leave. → "help her exit."
*Network + money read:* the "free visa" was sold by a sub-agent; Maria paid a "processing + medical" fee she's still repaying (debt that hasn't shrunk); her passport is held; **one staging address** hosts several domestic workers placed with **different** households. The constants are the broker, the lender, and the address — the household is interchangeable.
*Changed action:* exit + hotline **and** preserve fee receipts/loan terms, flag the cohort at the shared address, refer the financier. *Entity DD:* the named "employer" differs from the visa sponsor → sponsorship-trading; screen the sponsor and the recruiter's license.

**Scenario B — Construction wage complaint (corridor XX→YY).**
*Surface read:* Ramesh (composite) is underpaid by his employer. → "wage claim."
*Network + money read:* Ramesh is one of a **staggered batch**; he is employed by an **interposed manpower firm**, not the site principal (denial architecture); his contract was **substituted** on arrival; deductions for "accommodation + tools" gut his net pay; **one lender** financed the whole cohort.
*Changed action:* wage claim **and** map the batch, keep both contract versions, refer to financial intelligence. *Entity DD:* beneficial owner of the manpower firm is linked to the principal; the recruitment license is sublet.

**Scenario C — Distant-water fishing (corridor XX→YY).**
*Surface read:* Sita's brother (composite) works punishing hours at sea. → "labour-hours violation."
*Network + money read:* a "training + placement" debt plus **transshipment at sea** keeps him from ever landing (debt that cannot amortize because he cannot be paid out); wages route to a **third-party onshore account**; the **same manning agent and the same notary** appear across several vessels.
*Changed action:* welfare + repatriation pathway **and** preserve the debt ledger and account details, refer cross-border. *Entity DD:* vessel ownership is opaque (flag-of-convenience); cluster the shared manning agent across hulls.

## 9. The reasoner's standing checklist

Before concluding any migrant-worker case, confirm you have asked:

- [ ] **Roles:** Did I name the recruiter, sub-agent, employer, transporter, harbourer, **financier**, and **facilitator** — or explicitly note which are unknown? (Don't let the financier and facilitator default to invisible.)
- [ ] **Money in:** Did the worker pay anything? Under what (camouflaged) labels?
- [ ] **Debt:** Is there a debt, and **does it shrink** as wages are paid?
- [ ] **Money out:** Where do wages go, what is deducted, and is anything deferred or held?
- [ ] **Corridor margin:** Can I state the extraction as a number vs the legal cap and lawful wage?
- [ ] **Network:** Are there **one-to-many** tells (shared recruiter / lender / address / notary / village)? Did I trip the network hypothesis on ≥2 tells?
- [ ] **Advice:** Did I help the individual **and** flag the reusable infrastructure and the right referral (financial-crime, not only labour)?
- [ ] **Audit + privacy:** Did I record actors, amounts, accounts, corridor, ILO indicators, confidence, and the confirming evidence — with raw PII kept local and identifiers hashed?
- [ ] **Entity DD handoff:** Did I emit verifiable entity leads and edges for due-diligence screening?

> **Remember the base rates this guidance exists to fix:** without deliberate prompting, the network is considered ~8.5% of the time and the money ~3.6%. Treat both as **mandatory fields**, not optional enrichment. The case in front of you is a node; the money is the motive; the network is the next ten victims.
