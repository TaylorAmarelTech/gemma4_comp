# Corridor Remedy & Referral-Safety Reference

**Migrant-worker protection across labour corridors** · Domestic work and general labour exploitation

> **How to use this document.** It is written for a worker who may already be in an exploitative situation, and for the advisor, hotline operator, or peer helping them. The premise is honest: a worker reaching out for help is a potential victim, and not everyone who offers "help" is safe — some are linked to the people causing the harm. The goal is **calibrated trust, not paranoia.** Most listed bodies (embassies, UN agencies, government hotlines, established NGOs) are genuinely safe; the point is to verify *before* sharing sensitive data or changing location, prefer the publicly listed safe defaults, and **still reach out quickly** — the worst outcome is an isolated worker who never connects to real help.
>
> Nothing here accuses any named institution of wrongdoing. Where a corridor has a structural protection gap (e.g. a law that excludes domestic workers) that is stated as **law**, and where official channels can be unreliable that is stated as **variable reliability** — neither is an allegation of corruption.

---

## Part 1 — How to assess whether a referral or "helper" is safe (universal)

Use a short, **repeatable checklist** rather than blind trust or fear. Run any channel — a flyer, a phone number, a person who approaches you, an office — against these checks. A channel that fails checks 1–5 is a channel to route *around*: switch to an independently verified safe default (embassy, UN agency, national hotline, registered NGO) — but keep reaching out.

| # | Test | What a SAFE channel looks like | Red flag |
|---|------|--------------------------------|----------|
| 1 | **Cost** | Genuine embassy, UN (ILO/IOM), government-hotline, and established-NGO help is **free**. | Being asked to pay, deposit, or "commission" to be helped, referred, or "rescued." *This is the single biggest red flag.* |
| 2 | **Verifiability** | You can find the same contact independently — on the official embassy site, ILO/IOM pages, the government hotline, or a known NGO directory. Cross-check through **at least two** independent sources. | Reachable only via a flyer, a stranger's note, or one private message; no traceable office, website, registration, or named affiliation. |
| 3 | **Autonomy** | You keep your own **passport, phone, and money** and can communicate directly. | Help is conditioned on surrendering your documents/phone, or is reachable only through your employer, sponsor, or recruiter. |
| 4 | **Confidentiality & consent** | It explains what information it collects, where it goes, what stays local; lets you decline parts; never forces disclosure to your employer/recruiter. | It collects money or personal details but won't give ID, receipts, or any way to verify — or shares your data with the employer/agency. |
| 5 | **Independence** | It is **not** the recruiter, employer, broker — or anyone they personally nominate. | The agency that placed you (or a person linked to it) offers to "resolve" the dispute, or "recommends" the helper. Conflict of interest. |
| 6 | **Pressure & clarity** | Explains your rights in a language you understand; gives written information, a named contact, and a physical/registered presence; never rushes you. | Pressure to decide fast, sign/thumbprint documents you can't read, stay silent, or "let me handle everything." |
| 7 | **Accountability** | Answers to a known institution: a diplomatic mission, a UN agency, a registered NGO, or a government ministry. | "Guaranteed, very fast results" for money or data, with no written agreement, receipt, or named, accountable contact. |
| 8 | **Track record** | Recommended by other workers, by diaspora / domestic-worker networks, or by another trusted service. | Promoted only by anonymous accounts; unusual payment rails (crypto, e-wallet top-ups, gift cards, personal accounts). |
| 9 | **Onward connection** | Willing to be **one of several** contacts and to connect you onward to recognised mechanisms (national referral mechanism, legal-aid NGO, labour attaché). | Insists on being your *only* contact and discourages you from reaching the embassy, hotlines, or known NGOs. |
| 10 | **Safety of first contact** | Before meeting anyone in person, a **trusted third party** (family, fellow worker) knows where you are going and who you are meeting; first contact is by phone/hotline. | Pressure to move you to a new place or across a border before anything is documented or anyone independent knows where you are. |

**The safe default rule.** Start with the publicly listed **origin-state embassy/consulate**, the **UN agencies (ILO, IOM)**, the **national hotlines**, and the **well-established service NGOs**. Treat any unlisted intermediary with extra verification — while still reaching out, because connecting the worker to real help quickly is the priority.

**Recognising exploitation — ILO's 11 Indicators of Forced Labour** (the practical recognition checklist): including *debt bondage, retention of identity documents, deception about the job/wage/destination, restriction of movement, withholding of wages, abusive working/living conditions, excessive overtime, intimidation and threats, and isolation.* If several of these are present, treat it as a possible forced-labour/trafficking case and route to a referral mechanism, not just a wage complaint.

## Part 2 — Ethiopia → Lebanon (ET-LB)

**Snapshot.** Ethiopian women recruited mainly for **domestic work** in Lebanon under the **kafala (sponsorship) system**; high debt-bondage risk; typically ~24-month contracts. **Structural gap (this is law, not an accusation):** migrant domestic workers are **excluded from the Lebanese Labour Code of 1946 (Art. 7)**, so protection rests on the **Standard Unified Contract (SUC)**, anti-trafficking law, and external referral channels (embassy, UN agencies, NGOs) rather than ordinary labour-law enforcement.

### Remedies available

- **Wage recovery.** Document hours and unpaid wages and claim arrears — via the Ministry of Labour complaint process (**Hotline 1740**) and, where needed, a judicial labour/civil claim, *despite* the Labour Code exclusion. (Standard: **ILO C095, Protection of Wages**.)
- **Criminal complaint for forced labour / trafficking.** Under **Lebanon Law No. 164/2011** (Penal Code Art. 586) and, in Ethiopia, **Proclamation No. 1178/2020**, with referral into the **National Referral Mechanism** for victims (**Palermo Protocol Art. 6**).
- **Recovery of a confiscated passport or ID.** Document retention is prohibited and is an **ILO Forced Labour Indicator** — report it to the embassy, the 1740 hotline, or General Security.
- **Safe exit from an abusive employer**, with emergency shelter (e.g. Caritas Lebanon Migrants Centre safe houses, Amel) and protection while a complaint or transfer is processed.
- **Lawful contract termination or employer transfer** under the **Standard Unified Contract** — preferred over informal "running away," to preserve legal status where possible.
- **Voluntary return.** IOM Assisted Voluntary Return and Reintegration (**AVRR**) plus Ethiopian Embassy repatriation, with reintegration support on arrival through **IOM Ethiopia**, the **Refugees and Returnees Service (RRS)**, and **Caritas Ethiopia**.
- **Complaint against the recruiting Private Employment Agency in Ethiopia** under **Overseas Employment Proclamation No. 923/2016** (license sanction, bond/guarantee claim) via the **Ministry of Labour and Skills**.
- **Free legal representation, casework, and wage-recovery / case-support letters** through specialist NGOs (KAFA, Anti-Racism Movement, Egna Legna Besidet, Amel).
- **Victim assistance and compensation** (counselling, legal aid, restitution) under **Palermo Protocol Art. 6**, channelled through the National Referral Mechanism and NGOs.

### Referral risk patterns (verify, don't assume — these are patterns, not proof against any named body)

- Any "help" that asks for an **upfront fee, deposit, or commission** — legitimate embassy/UN/NGO/government assistance is free.
- A referral reachable **only through the employer, sponsor, or recruiter**, or one that insists they stay in the loop.
- An intermediary who **discourages contacting the embassy, 1740 / 8797, or known NGOs**, or who says "let me handle everything" and controls all communication.
- A channel that requires **surrendering the passport, ID, or phone** as a condition of help.
- The **recruiting agency** — or someone linked to it — offering to "resolve" the dispute (conflict of interest).
- Pressure to **sign or thumbprint documents** the worker cannot read or that are in an unfamiliar language.
- Promises of **guaranteed, very fast results** for money or personal data, with no written agreement, receipt, or named contact.
- A **"new employer" or informal transfer arranged off the books**, bypassing the Standard Unified Contract and the embassy/Ministry process.
- Contact **only through anonymous private accounts**, with no verifiable office, registration, or directory listing.
- Requests to **keep the matter secret from the worker's family or embassy**.
- Collecting money or details but **refusing to give ID, receipts, or any way to verify**.

### Key legal instruments

| Instrument | What it does |
|------------|--------------|
| **Ethiopia — Overseas Employment Proclamation No. 923/2016** (am. by No. 1246/2021) | Licenses Private Employment Agencies; pre-departure & bilateral-agreement requirements; bars prohibited worker-paid fees |
| **Ethiopia — Proclamation No. 1178/2020** (revising 909/2015) | Criminalises trafficking and smuggling; victim protection |
| **Lebanon — Law No. 164/2011** (Penal Code Art. 586) | Main criminal route for forced labour / trafficking |
| **Lebanon — Labour Code of 1946, Art. 7** | **The gap:** excludes migrant domestic workers — why the SUC and anti-trafficking law carry the protection |
| **Lebanon — Standard Unified Contract (SUC)** | Governing contract (wages, rest, repatriation); kafala residency administered by General Security |
| **ILO C029 (1930) + 2014 Protocol (P029)** | Forced-labour prohibition and victim-remedy duties (**ratified by both states**) |
| **ILO C105 (1957)** | Abolition of forced labour |
| **ILO C181 (1997), Art. 7** | Fair-recruitment benchmark — **no fees charged to workers**, directly or indirectly |
| **ILO C189 (2011)** | Domestic Workers Convention — written contract, weekly rest, wage protection, freedom of movement, no document retention (*normative; not ratified by either state*) |
| **ILO C095 (1949) & C190 (2019)** | Wage protection; violence and harassment standards |
| **ILO Indicators of Forced Labour (11)** + **General Principles & Operational Guidelines on Fair Recruitment (2016)** + 2019 definition of recruitment fees | Operational checklists for assessing a case |
| **UN Palermo Protocol (2000)** | Trafficking definition (Art. 3) and victim protection (Art. 6); **ICRMW (1990)** as migrant-worker rights standard (*normative*) |

## Part 3 — Myanmar → Thailand (MM-TH)

**Snapshot.** Cross-border labour migration into multiple sectors, with a notable **fishing / seafood** dimension. The "regular" route runs through the **Myanmar–Thailand MOU** (demand letter, contract, MOU recruitment); **irregular/broker routes fall outside those protections.** **General context (reliability, not an accusation):** state-mechanism reliability in Myanmar has been disrupted since 2021, so official channels can be intermittently unreachable or under-resourced — **independent cross-checking matters more here**, not because any named body is doing wrong, but because reliability genuinely varies.

### Remedies available

- **Unpaid / underpaid-wage claim** with the Thai **Department of Labour Protection and Welfare (DLPW)** under the **Labour Protection Act** — **available regardless of documentation status**; recovers back wages, unlawful deductions, overtime, severance.
- **Identification as a trafficking victim**, opening the **National Referral Mechanism**: shelter, medical/psychosocial support, a recovery/reflection period, and the **non-punishment principle** (not penalised for status offences resulting from being trafficked).
- **Criminal complaint for forced labour or trafficking** (Thai Anti-Trafficking Act, incl. the **2019 standalone forced-labour offence**), with court-ordered restitution/compensation and access to the **Anti-Trafficking Fund**.
- **Recovery of confiscated passport/ID and work permit** (document retention is a criminal offence under the Royal Ordinance), plus the **right to change employer** in abuse cases.
- **Civil claim for unpaid wages and damages** — including **transnational** claims pursued from Myanmar after returning home (the **Justice Without Borders** model), so leaving Thailand does not extinguish the claim.
- **Compensation for workplace injury or occupational illness** via the **Workmen's Compensation Fund**.
- **Reporting recruitment-fee overcharging, contract substitution, or broker abuse** — fees charged to the worker breach the Thai Royal Ordinance and the **C181 / Fair Recruitment** standard and may ground a refund/remediation claim.
- **Protection from immediate deportation** while a labour complaint or trafficking case proceeds, with consular documentation support to regularise status during the case.
- **Repatriation and reintegration assistance** (IOM / national mechanisms) where the worker chooses to return — **separate from** any compensation claim, which can continue afterward.

| **MAP Foundation** (Migrants Assistance Programme), Chiang Mai | Burmese-speaking | Labour-rights advice, legal aid, translation at hospitals/police; strong for northern Thailand |
| **Labour Protection Network Foundation (LPN)** | fishing/seafood focus | Rescue, shelter, victim assistance, prosecution support |

| **Myanmar labour attaché / embassy, Bangkok** | consular | Documentation and Assistance-to-Nationals — *use it, but cross-check and keep an independent NGO contact in parallel, since official-channel reliability can vary* |

### Referral risk patterns (verify, don't assume)

- A "helper" who needs to **hold your passport, ID, or work permit** — legitimate services may photograph or note documents but never need your originals.
- Any channel that **charges a fee** to refer you, file your complaint, or "rescue" you — genuine services are free.
- Pressure to **decide fast, stay silent, or not contact anyone else** — isolation is a control tactic, not a service.
- A referral that comes **through the same broker, recruiter, or employer** who caused the problem, or someone they personally recommend.
- Promises of a **guaranteed outcome** (guaranteed visa, back-pay, or new job) — real legal/labour processes carry uncertainty.
- A push to **move you to a new place or across the border** before anything is documented or any independent party knows where you are.
- Requests for **unusual payment rails** — crypto, e-wallet top-ups, gift cards, or a "deposit" to a personal account.
- An entity that **cannot be verified** — no traceable office, website, or email; contact only via a personal messaging account; no named affiliation to a known network.
- **Discouraging you from getting anything in writing** or from keeping your own copies of your contract and documents.
- **Context risk:** official channels can be intermittently unreachable, under-resourced, or unable to safeguard data — so independent cross-checking matters more here. This is about *variable reliability*, not wrongdoing by any named body.

### Key legal instruments

| Instrument | What it does |
|------------|--------------|
| **Myanmar — Anti-Trafficking in Persons Law (2005)** | Defines trafficking/forced-labour offences and victim-protection duties; basis for prosecuting recruiters/brokers from Myanmar |
| **Myanmar — Law Relating to Overseas Employment (1999)** | Licenses/regulates recruitment agencies — the reference for whether a recruiter is lawful. *Enforcement disrupted since 2021 — verify, don't assume* |
| **Myanmar–Thailand bilateral MOU** | The "regular" migration channel; irregular/broker routes fall outside its protections |
| **Thailand — Anti-Trafficking in Persons Act B.E. 2551 (2008, am. 2015/2017/2019)** | Core trafficking law; **2019** added forced labour/services as a standalone offence (Sec. 6/1) and strengthened victim compensation |
| **Thailand — Royal Ordinance on Foreign Workers' Employment B.E. 2560 (2017, am. 2018)** | Governs migrant registration/recruitment; **prohibits worker-paid recruitment fees**; criminalises passport confiscation and forced labour |
| **Thailand — Labour Protection Act B.E. 2541 (1998)** | Minimum wage, hours, overtime, deductions, severance — **applies regardless of immigration status**, so unpaid-wage claims survive even if the permit is irregular |
| **Thailand — Workmen's Compensation Act / Fund** | Injury and occupational-illness compensation, in principle irrespective of status |
| **ILO C029 (1930) + P029 (2014)** | Both states bound by C029; **Thailand ratified the 2014 Protocol** (first in Asia-Pacific, 2018) — prevention, victim remedy, access to compensation |
| **ILO C188 (2007), Work in Fishing** | **Ratified by Thailand (2019)** — central to this corridor's fishing/seafood sector: contracts, rest, repatriation, conditions at sea |
| **ILO C095 (1949)** | Standard against unlawful wage deductions and coerced/in-kind wage practices |
| **ILO C181, Art. 7 (1997)** + **Fair Recruitment Guidelines (2019)** | No recruitment fees charged to workers — the fee-abuse benchmark (*not ratified by either state; used as the standard*) |
| **ILO C097 (1949), C143 (1975), UN ICRMW (1990)** | Migrant-worker rights framework (*largely normative for this corridor; the standard advocacy and NGOs invoke*) |
| **UN Palermo Protocol (2000)** | International trafficking definition; underpins the **non-punishment principle** and cross-border cooperation |
| **ILO 11 Indicators of Forced Labour** | The practical recognition checklist an advisor uses (debt bondage, document retention, deception, restricted movement, withheld wages, abusive conditions, excessive overtime, intimidation, isolation) |

### One-line takeaway for the worker

> Genuine help — your **embassy, the UN (ILO/IOM), the national hotlines (Lebanon 1740 / Ethiopia 8797 / Thailand 1300), and established NGOs** — is **free**, lets you **keep your passport and phone**, and will be **one of several** contacts. If anyone asks for money, takes your documents, tells you to keep it secret, or is connected to your recruiter or employer — pause and verify through a second, independently listed channel. But **do reach out.** Staying silent and alone is the bigger danger.
