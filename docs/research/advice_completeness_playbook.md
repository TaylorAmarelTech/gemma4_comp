# The Advice-Completeness Playbook
## Compensation & Civil Redress for Exploited Migrant Workers

*A synthesis for a migrant-worker safety assistant. Every instrument below was adversarially verified against primary sources; nothing here is fabricated. Concrete schemes, deadlines, and contacts are volatile — route those to a live tool/RAG lookup or a qualified local specialist rather than asserting them.*

---

## Why complete remedy coverage matters

Ask an exploited worker what they actually want and two answers dominate: **get my money back**, and **don't let me be punished or deported for what I was forced into.** Those are precisely the two things first-contact advice drops most reliably. Coverage measurement on this project shows the specific, correctly-grounded money remedies — *civil claim for damages* and *statutory compensation* — are surfaced in only a low single-digit share of cases (**omitted on the order of 94–99% of the time**), while generic substitutes crowd them out: `embassy_assistance` 54.1%, `legal_aid` 35.8%, `regulator_complaint` 34.6%. A worker is told "see a lawyer" or "call your embassy" — gestures at help — without ever being told the actionable, citable thing: *you are owed money as of right, and you cannot be punished for what you were compelled to do.*

The gap is structural, not random. It comes from crisis-triage tunnel vision (safety first, money "later" — and "later" never arrives), a rescue-not-rights frame that casts the worker as a victim to protect rather than a **creditor owed money**, a frontline knowledge gap about the actual legal hooks, fear of giving false hope, mistaken status anxiety ("undocumented workers can't claim"), conflation of the full remedy with bare wage recovery, and the procedural friction of limitation periods and specialist referral.

**The completeness rule for this assistant:** whenever exploitation, forced labour, withheld wages, or recruitment-fee debt surfaces, the money remedy and the non-punishment guarantee must be named — not as a promise of payment, but as a right with multiple parallel routes worth pursuing early and in parallel. *Legal entitlement is not money in the hand; payout rates are low (absconded or insolvent offenders, uncollected orders, deportation before claims resolve). The honest message is "the law says you are owed redress and there is more than one route — pursue them early," never "you will be paid."*

## Read first — the two enabling principles that unlock both remedy families

Surface these with **every** money remedy below. They remove the worker's two biggest fears and are the single most load-bearing provisions for migrants:

1. **Remedy is not conditional on immigration status.** *ILO Protocol of 2014 to the Forced Labour Convention (P029), Art. 4(1)* guarantees all victims of forced labour effective remedies "**such as compensation**," **"irrespective of their presence or legal status in the national territory."** Past-pay specifically survives irregular status and departure: *ILO C143, Art. 9(1)* and *ICRMW, Art. 25(3)*. ("I was undocumented / I already left the country" is not, by itself, a bar.)
2. **Non-punishment.** A worker cannot be penalised for offences they were compelled to commit while exploited: *P029, Art. 4(2)*; *Council of Europe Warsaw Convention (CETS 197, 2005), Art. 26*.

> **Precision note:** *ILO C097* equal-treatment (Art. 6) attaches to migrants **lawfully** present — do not lean on C97 for an undocumented worker. The *status-blind* past-pay entitlement is carried by **C143 Art. 9 and ICRMW Art. 25(3)**, not C97.

## Remedy family 1 — Compensation / victim compensation (the full bundle)

**What it is.** Not one payout but a bundle of distinct, often parallel claims: (1) unpaid/underpaid wages and unlawful deductions; (2) reimbursement of unlawfully charged **recruitment fees and related costs** (Employer Pays Principle); (3) criminal/statutory **restitution** against the trafficker or employer; (4) **civil damages** for the harm; and (5) **state-administered victim/crime compensation funds** that can pay even when the offender is never identified or convicted. Frame it as **money owed as of right — not charity, aid, or a favour.**

**When to surface.** After immediate safety/shelter/hotline is handled — and *re-raise it*, because triage is exactly where it gets dropped. Any mention of unpaid wages, confiscated documents, recruitment debt, or "I worked and wasn't paid" is a trigger.

### Governing instruments

| Layer | Instrument | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Universal floor | **ILO P029 (2014), Art. 4(1)** | Effective remedies "such as compensation," status-blind; **Art. 4(2)** non-punishment |
| Universal floor | **ILO Recommendation 203 (2014), para. 12** | Elaborates access to compensation and remedies |
| Universal floor | **Palermo Protocol (2000), Art. 6(6)** | Domestic system must offer "the possibility of obtaining compensation for damage suffered" (and **Art. 6(2)** information on proceedings) |
| Recruitment fees | **ILO C181 (1997), Art. 7** + ILO Fair Recruitment General Principles (2016) + Definition of Recruitment Fees and Related Costs (2019) | No worker-paid fees → reimbursement (Employer Pays) |
| Wages / past pay | **ILO C095 (1949)**; **C143 Art. 9(1)**; **ICRMW Arts. 22(9), 25** | Unlawful deductions recoverable; pay claims survive irregular status / expulsion |
| Europe | **Warsaw Convention (CETS 197), Art. 15** (incl. **15(4)** state-guaranteed fund) + **Art. 26**; **EU Dir. 2011/36/EU, Art. 17** (as amended by Dir. (EU) 2024/1712); **Council Dir. 2004/80/EC** | Compensation/legal redress + state fund route |
| US | **18 U.S.C. § 1593** (mandatory criminal restitution); **§ 1595** (civil suit); state **Crime Victim Compensation** programs; **T-visa** | Restitution + civil + no-conviction state fund + status relief |
| UK | **Modern Slavery Act 2015, ss. 8–10** (Slavery & Trafficking Reparation Orders); **CICA** scheme | Reparation orders + state criminal-injuries fund |
| Origin states | **Philippines OWWA**; **Sri Lanka SLBFE**; **Bangladesh WEWB**; **India ICWF** (+ PBBY insurance) | Welfare-fund / consular claims, insurance, repatriation |

### Mechanism — four parallel routes (crucially, not all need a conviction)

1. **Criminal restitution** — when the offender is prosecuted, the court orders them to pay the victim (US § 1593 *mandatory*; UK MSA ss. 8–10).
2. **Civil / labour claims** — a civil suit (US § 1595) or an employment/labour tribunal claim for unpaid wages, illegal deductions, and **fee reimbursement** (C181 Art. 7).
3. **State victim / crime-compensation funds** — pay **even if the offender is never caught or convicted** (Warsaw Art. 15(4); EU Dir. 2011/36 Art. 17 + Dir. 2004/80; US state CVC; UK CICA).
4. **Origin-country welfare funds / consular routes** — claims assistance, insurance, repatriation via the embassy, labour attaché, or national welfare board (OWWA, SLBFE, WEWB, ICWF).

### Ready-to-use phrasing

> "There's something many people are never told: what happened to you may also mean you are owed money — not charity, but compensation the law says is your right. International rules (the ILO Forced Labour Protocol of 2014) and many national laws say people who were forced to work or trafficked can seek compensation no matter their immigration status, and you cannot be punished for things you were forced to do. That can include the wages you were never paid, the recruitment fees you should never have been charged getting paid back, and extra compensation for the harm caused. There's usually more than one way to claim — a case against the people responsible, a labour or civil claim for your wages, or in many countries a government victim-compensation fund that can pay even if no one is ever convicted. You don't have to decide anything today. If it's safe to do so, try to keep anything that could help prove what happened — your contract, pay records, receipts for fees, messages, and the names and dates you remember — somewhere your employer can't reach. Some claims have time limits, so it's worth asking sooner rather than later. I can connect you with a free, confidential lawyer or NGO and the compensation scheme for where you are — you'd only share your personal details directly with them, never here. Would that help?"

### Honest caveats & routing
- **State funds (route 3) are a backstop, not an automatic entitlement.** They are typically discretionary, capped, and conditioned (timely police report, cooperation, conduct exclusions, filing deadlines). EU Dir. 2004/80 is keyed to "violent intentional crime" and UK CICA to a "crime of violence" — framings that have **disadvantaged pure labour-exploitation cases**. Routes 1–2 and fee reimbursement are the stronger "owed as of right" heads.
- **Victim-identification gate.** Many funds require formal identification first — the **National Referral Mechanism (NRM)** in the UK/EU, or "continued presence"/certification in the US. Name that step; it is itself an action.
- **The T-visa is immigration relief, not compensation** — but it unlocks access to benefits and is worth pairing.

## Remedy family 2 — Civil claim for damages (the private money action)

**What it is.** A private monetary action the worker brings (as claimant/plaintiff) against the exploiter — employer, recruiter/agency, broker, or trafficker. Distinct from the two things advisors routinely confuse it with: **(a) criminal prosecution** (the *state* is the party; the worker is only a witness) and **(b) bare unpaid-wage recovery.** It is broader than back wages: recoverable heads typically include unpaid wages **plus** illegally charged recruitment fees and related costs, unlawful deductions, the value of coerced/inflated "debt" and withheld documents, **and damages for the harm itself** (personal injury, psychological/moral harm), with aggravated/exemplary or statutory-multiple damages and attorney's fees in some systems.

**When to surface.** Whenever the worker has named a responsible party and money harm. Pair it explicitly with "this is *separate from* and does *not require* a police case" — that distinction is the most under-known and most empowering.

### Governing instruments

- **Universal floor (the right's source):** *ILO P029, Art. 4(1)* (status-blind remedies "such as compensation"); *ILO R203, para. 12*; *Palermo Protocol, Art. 6(6)* (+ *Art. 6(2)* information). These oblige **states** to provide a domestic route — the worker does not sue on the treaty directly.
- **Recruitment-fee head:** *ILO C181, Art. 7* + ILO Fair Recruitment General Principles (2016) + Definition of Recruitment Fees and Related Costs (2019) — Employer Pays.
- **Migrant status-blind past pay:** *ILO C143, Art. 9*; *ICRMW Arts. 25 (incl. 25(3)), 22(6) & 22(9), 83*. (Use C143/ICRMW, not C97, for an undocumented worker.)
- **Domestic delivery vehicles — cite the one for the worker's forum:**
  - **US:** *18 U.S.C. § 1595* — civil action against the perpetrator **or a knowing beneficiary**; recovers "**damages and reasonable attorney's fees**"; **10-year limitation** (tolled for minors to age 18 + 10). Courts also award punitive damages; **treble** damages run through RICO (*18 U.S.C. § 1964(c)*) where trafficking is a predicate. Pair FLSA for wages and the T-visa for status.
  - **UK:** *Modern Slavery Act 2015, ss. 8–10* reparation orders; common-law tort/contract; employment tribunal for wages; **CICA**; **NRM** for status/protection.
  - **EU:** *Dir. 2011/36/EU, Art. 17* (compensation access); *Dir. 2004/80/EC* (cross-border state compensation); ***Dir. 2009/52/EC, Art. 6*** (back-payment of outstanding remuneration to irregularly employed third-country nationals).

### Mechanism — five delivery procedures (name whichever exists in the forum)

1. **Standalone civil lawsuit** — tort (negligence, breach of statutory duty, intimidation, conversion/unjust enrichment), contract, or a dedicated statutory cause of action (e.g. § 1595).
2. **Civil claim joined to the criminal case** — the *adhesion / "constitution de partie civile"* procedure in civil-law systems (e.g. France) claims damages inside the prosecution without a separate suit.
3. **Compensation / reparation order at criminal sentencing** — the under-known bridge between criminal and money routes (UK MSA ss. 8–10; ordinary compensation orders).
4. **Labour tribunal / employment claim** — for wages, unlawful deductions, holiday pay; often the **fastest, lowest-cost** forum.
5. **State victim-compensation fund** — where the perpetrator cannot pay or has absconded (EU 2004/80; UK CICA).

### Ready-to-use phrasing

> "You may also have the right to claim money — called compensation or damages — from the people who exploited you. This is separate from any criminal case against them, and it is more than just the wages you are owed: it can also cover the recruitment or 'training' fees you were charged, money that was taken from your pay, and the harm you went through.
>
> Two things matter for your situation specifically: (1) this right exists even if your visa has expired or you worked without papers; and (2) talking to a lawyer is private and is not the same as going to the police — you decide if police are ever involved.
>
> If it is safe, keep your own copies of anything that shows what happened — your contract, payslips, fee or 'training fee' receipts, WhatsApp messages, photos, and a simple note of names, places and dates — and store them somewhere your employer or agency cannot reach.
>
> There are usually deadlines (time limits) to bring a claim, so when you feel safe and ready, it is worth asking a free legal-aid service, an anti-trafficking organisation that has its own lawyers, or a trade union about your options. You stay in control of whether and when to act."

### Honest caveats & routing
- **UK reparation orders (ss. 8–10) in practice ride on a confiscation order** (the offender must have realisable assets) and are discretionary — so tort/contract, the employment tribunal, and CICA often do more of the real work.
- **Civil litigation is lawyer-gated, slow, and the worker may have left the jurisdiction** — counter the silent drop by naming legal aid, contingency, NGO/pro-bono legal programs, the *partie civile* shortcut, and state funds.
- **Amounts, available heads, and whether punitive/multiple damages exist vary sharply** — treat figures as never guaranteed and route to a qualified local specialist.

## Shared operational checklist (apply to both families)

- **Evidence preservation, safely.** Encourage the worker to keep *their own* copies of contract, payslips/pay records, fee or "training fee" receipts, messages (WhatsApp), photos, and a simple note of names/places/dates — stored where the employer or agency **cannot reach**.
- **Limitation periods.** Many claims have **short, varying deadlines**; flag "sooner rather than later" without pressuring a decision today.
- **Victim-identification gate.** Name it explicitly where it applies (UK/EU **NRM**; US continued presence/certification) — it is a prerequisite step, not a formality.
- **Volatile vs stable content.** Stable and fine-tunable: *the right exists, is status-independent, pairs with non-punishment, and runs through criminal-restitution / civil-labour / state-fund / origin-welfare routes.* Volatile (route via tool/RAG/specialist, never hardcode): the **specific scheme, deadline, eligibility, and contact**.
- **Privacy boundary.** The worker shares personal details **directly with the lawyer/NGO/scheme, never in this channel.** Talking to a lawyer is confidential and is **not** the same as involving police — the worker controls if police are ever involved.
- **Corridor routing (Asia → Gulf).** For PH/ID/BD/NP/IN/LK workers in GCC destinations, the strongest enforceable head is **restitution of illegal recruitment fees** (C181 Art. 7). Combine destination labour-court / Wage Protection System / end-of-service claims with **origin-state recruitment regulators** (Philippines POEA/DMW, BP2MI; Bangladesh BMET; Nepal DoFE) and **origin-state welfare funds** (OWWA, SLBFE, WEWB, ICWF) plus consular routes.

## Honest framing guardrails (do not overclaim)

1. **De jure ≠ de facto.** Independent monitoring (GRETA on Warsaw Arts 15/26; EU and US restitution studies) confirms most victims never actually *receive* compensation even when a court awards it. State the **entitlement** and the **multiple parallel routes**; do not imply a payout. Pair the right with the realistic barriers (time limits, evidentiary thresholds, perpetrator insolvency, CICA's "crime of violence" framing).
2. **Treaties bind only ratifying states.** The **largest destination corridor — the GCC (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain, Oman)** — has **not** ratified ICRMW, C143, C97, C181, or (mostly) the 2014 Forced Labour Protocol. They **are** Palermo parties, so **Art. 6(6) applies in principle**; but practical recovery there runs through **national mechanisms** (Wage Protection Systems, labour courts, bilateral channels) plus origin-state funds, not the supporting ILO instruments. The compensation right is genuinely **operational in at least the US, UK, and EU corridors** and via the origin-state welfare funds.
3. **Cite only the instrument that governs the worker's actual forum.** Do not assert a domestic statute (TVPA, MSA, an EU directive) applies in a country that has not enacted it. Use the universal floor (P029 Art. 4(1), Palermo Art. 6(6)) as the *right's source*, and a forum-specific vehicle as the *route*.
