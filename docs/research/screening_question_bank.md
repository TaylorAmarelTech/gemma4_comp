# Screening Question Bank — Trafficking Victim/Witness Screening of Migrant Workers
*Trauma-informed · non-leading · victim-centered · plain-language*

## Purpose & scope
A practitioner's bank of open, non-leading questions for **screening** migrant workers who may be victims or witnesses of trafficking or forced labour. The method is a **funnel**: invite free narrative first, then ask open follow-ups only about what the person themselves raises. The interview **surfaces** indicators in the person's own words; you **map them to indicators afterward** — never inside the question, and never as a label spoken to the person. This is a screening aid to recognize possible indicators and connect people to options. It is **not** an evidence-gathering interview, a legal determination, or a substitute for a formal forensic/NRM interview.

Grounded in: IOM and ILO operational indicators of forced labour, Vera TVIT, Polaris practice, free-recall/PEACE interviewing, and (for children) the NICHD protocol and UNODC/ILO victim-interviewing guidance.

## How to use this bank (method)
- **Funnel.** Free narrative → open *what / how / tell me* probes → closed questions **only** to clarify the person's own words.
- **One question at a time, then wait.** Allow silence. Do not stack or rapid-fire.
- **Mirror their words.** Echo the person's exact terms; ask what *their* word means rather than substituting your own.
- **Plain language.** Keep clinical/legal terms out of the questions; classify against indicators later.
- **They assign the meaning.** Let the person name the events, the emotion, and any identity. Do not label, characterize, or supply facts/numbers they have not said first.
- **Screen, don't adjudicate.** Treat answers as leads, not proof; never credibility-test the person.

## Before you begin (setting & safety)
- **Private setting** — out of sight and earshot of any employer, recruiter, sponsor, "helper," or companion.
- **Trained, independent interpreter** the person is comfortable with — never an interpreter of convenience or one chosen by a third party.
- **Suspected child:** interview the child alone with an independent interpreter; if age remains genuinely uncertain after open questioning, **treat the person as a minor (benefit of the doubt).**
- **Be honest** about what you can and cannot do. Make no promise you can't keep, and never tie pay, housing, immigration status, arrests, or a visa to what the person discloses.

## Opening & consent (say before any topic)
Give control to the person and confirm consent. Sample scripts you can adapt:
- *"Before we start, you decide how this goes — you can pause, skip any question, take a break, change your mind, or stop whenever you want, and you can ask me things too. You don't need to have answers ready, and there are no right or wrong answers. Is it alright if we begin?"*
- *"How are you feeling right now? Is there anything that would help you feel more comfortable or safe while we talk?"*

## Universal follow-up probes (use within every topic)
- "Can you tell me a bit more about that?"
- "And then what happened?" / "What happened next?"
- "What was that like for you?" / "Help me understand what that was like for you."
- "You used the word '[their word]' — can you help me understand what that means to you?" *(clarify their term; don't substitute yours)*
- "Who else, if anyone, was there?"
- "Is there anything else you'd want me to know about that?"
- "What were you hoping for at that point?" *(their perspective — instead of a blaming "why")*
- "It's okay if you don't remember every detail — just share what you can."
- **Pacing/permission:** "Take all the time you need — there's no rush, and we can pause or stop whenever you want." · "Whatever you're able to share is fine, and whatever you'd rather not is okay too." · "Would it be alright if I asked a little more about that, or would you rather move on?"

## Universal don'ts (apply to every topic)
- **No labels or legal conclusions** — *trafficking, victim, forced labour, slavery, debt bondage, exploited, coercion.* Let the person describe; classify afterward.
- **No leading tag/yes-no questions** that supply the answer ("They took your passport, didn't they?").
- **No presupposing unestablished facts or actors** ("When your boss deceived you…").
- **No feeding content or numbers** the person hasn't said first.
- **No blaming "why didn't you…"** questions ("Why didn't you just leave?").
- **No disbelief or consistency-testing** ("Are you sure?", "But earlier you said…").
- **No normative comparisons** ("Most workers like you say…").
- **No stacking / rapid-fire**; ask one open question, then wait.
- **No false promises, quid-pro-quo, quotas, or time pressure.**
- **No characterizing their experience or emotion for them** ("That must have been awful").

## Closing (every interview)
- "Is there anything we haven't talked about that you'd want me to understand?"
- "How are you feeling after talking about this — would you like to pause or stop here?"
*(After the interview: map the person's own account to ILO/Palermo indicators; never tell the person your classification; connect them to options and services at their pace.)*

## 1. Deception about the job & conditions
*Screens for: Palermo **means** element — fraud/deception. Operational signal "deception about work": the gap between what was promised at recruitment and what the work, pay, or housing actually were. The "what were you told" framing probes the recruitment representation **without** presupposing it was false.*

**Core questions**
1. Can you tell me about how you first heard about this job, and what you were told it would be?
2. Before you started, what kind of work did you understand you would be doing, and who would you be working for?
3. What were you told about the pay before you agreed — how much, how often, and how it would reach you?
4. What were you told about your working hours, your rest days, and the conditions you would be working in?
5. What were you told about where you would live, and about any costs, fees, or deductions?
6. Was there any contract, agreement, or paperwork involved? If so, tell me about it — what it said and what language it was in.
7. Once you actually began the work, what was a typical day like for you?
8. If anything turned out to be different from what you had been told, can you describe that in your own words?
9. If you noticed things were not what you expected, when did that start for you?
10. If you ever asked questions or raised concerns about any of this, what happened?
11. Looking back, is there anything you feel you weren't told that would have mattered to your decision?
12. Is there anything else about the way the job or the conditions were described to you that you would like me to understand?

**Topic follow-ups** *(plus the Universal probes)*
- "Who else was there when that was explained to you?"
- "When you say '[their own word]', what does that mean to you?"

**Watch-outs for this topic**
- "So you thought it was restaurant work but it was really something else?" — feeds content the person hasn't said.
- "Did they promise you good pay and then not pay you?" — fuses two claims into one leading bundle; ask each separately.
- "How did your boss deceive you?" / "They tricked you, didn't they?" — presumes the act and the actor.
- "Most workers in your situation say they were lied to about wages." — telegraphs the expected answer.

## 2. Recruitment fees & debt
*Screens for: recruitment-fee and debt-bondage indicators (ILO). Open neutrally so the person confirms whether any fee or debt exists — never assume one.*

**Framing/consent (say first):** *"I'd like to understand how you came to get this job, and anything to do with money or costs along the way. You're in control here — you can take a break, skip anything, change your mind, or stop whenever you want, and you don't need to have answers ready. Is it okay if we talk about this for a little while?"*

**Core questions**
1. Can you tell me, in your own words, how you came to get this job? You can start wherever feels comfortable, and take your time.
2. Walk me through what happened from when you first heard about the work up to your first day — what were the steps along the way?
3. Who was involved in helping you arrange the job or the trip, and what did each of those people do?
4. Tell me about any money or payments that were part of getting this job or arranging your travel.
5. When the work was first described to you, what were you told about your wages and about any costs to you?
6. Tell me how your pay works now — the amount, how often it comes, and the way you actually receive it.
7. Is anything taken out of your pay before it reaches you? If so, tell me about that.
8. Tell me about any money you owe to anyone connected with your work, your travel, your documents, or where you live.
9. Tell me how that amount was explained to you, and how you keep track of how much is left.

**Closing check-in:** *"Is there anything about money, costs, or what you owe that we haven't talked about that you'd want me to understand?"* and *"How are you feeling after talking about this — would you like to pause or stop here?"*

**Topic follow-ups** *(plus the Universal probes)*
- "When you said '[their exact word — fee, commission, advance]', what does that mean to you, and how was it described at the time?"
- "How did that come about? Whose idea was it, as you understood it?"
- "Has the amount changed since then? Tell me about that."
- "Who keeps the record of it, and how do you know how much is still owed?"
- "What is your understanding of what needs to happen before it's fully settled?"
- "If you ever wanted to stop working or change jobs, what do you understand would happen with that money?"
- "Is this something you agreed to — and how was it agreed?"
- "How do you feel about that arrangement?"

**Watch-outs for this topic**
- "How much was the recruitment fee?" / "How big is your debt?" — assumes a fee/debt exists. Open neutrally: *"Tell me about any money you owe…"*
- "They forced you to pay illegal recruitment fees, didn't they?" — leading and presumes illegality. Use: *"Tell me about any money involved in getting the job."*
- "So your employer trapped you in debt to control you?" — presumes intent and conclusion. Use: *"What's your understanding of what needs to happen before that money is settled?"*
- "You didn't really agree to those deductions, did you?" — negatively-phrased leading. Use: *"Is this something you agreed to, and how was it agreed?"*
- "Most workers from your country owe several thousand — sounds about right?" — plants a figure.
- "Tell me everything and we'll cancel your debt / get your money back." — false promise; coercive.

## 3. Documents & passport control
*Screens for: ILO forced-labour indicator — retention/withholding of identity documents. Funnel first; probe gently with the person's own words.*

**Core questions**
1. To start, can you tell me about the documents and papers you have with you right now — things like your passport, ID card, or work permit?
2. Where are those documents usually kept these days?
3. Walk me through what happened with your passport from the time you arrived for this job — in as much or as little detail as you'd like.
4. When you need your passport or ID for something, how do you go about getting it?
5. Who, if anyone, looks after your documents besides you?
6. Tell me about the last time you had your own passport in your hands.
7. If you wanted your documents back today, what do you think would happen?
8. What were you told about your papers when you started — by anyone involved in your job or your travel?
9. How do you feel about the way your documents are handled?
10. Is there anything about your documents or papers that you'd like to tell me about, in your own words?

*Note: Q6 is a memory anchor and Q7 is deliberately hypothetical (eliciting the person's own perception) — neither asserts that documents are being withheld.*

**Topic follow-ups** *(plus the Universal probes)*
- "You said someone is keeping it safe for you — how does that work on a day when you need it?"
- "Has it always been that way, or did it change at some point?"
- "How did that arrangement come about?"
- "What happens if you need your document in a hurry, like for an emergency or to see a doctor?"

**Watch-outs for this topic**
- "They took your passport, didn't they?" — tag question; plants a fact.
- "Did your employer confiscate your documents?" — yes/no + jargon ("confiscate") + presumes a perpetrator.
- "Most workers in your situation had their passport taken — was it the same for you?" — normalize-then-lead.
- "Don't worry, we'll arrest whoever did this to you." — premature promise that presumes a crime and can endanger the person.
- Loaded labels — *smuggler, captor, slave* — may not match the person's framing and can cause fear or silence.

## 4. Freedom of movement & confinement
*Screens for: ILO forced-labour indicator — restriction of movement / confinement. Questions elicit daily-life narrative; they lean toward assuming normal freedom (the correct non-leading direction) rather than presupposing confinement.*

**Core questions**
1. To start, and only if you're comfortable, can you tell me a little about the place where you were living and working? Take your time — you can stop or skip anything.
2. Walk me through what a usual day was like for you there, from when you woke up to when you went to sleep.
3. Tell me about how you came and went from the place where you stayed.
4. When the work was finished for the day, what were you able to do with your time?
5. If you wanted to go somewhere on your own — to a shop, a clinic, a place of worship, or to see someone — what was that like for you?
6. What do you remember about the doors, gates, or windows where you lived and worked?
7. Who else was usually around that place, and what were they doing?
8. Tell me about your days off or free time — how did those usually go?
9. Was there anything you needed to do before you could go out or travel somewhere? Tell me about that.
10. How did you feel about your ability to come and go as you wished?
11. If there was ever a time you thought about leaving or going somewhere else, tell me what that was like for you.
12. Is there anything about getting around, or about being able to leave, that you'd like me to understand in your own words?

**Topic follow-ups** *(plus the Universal probes)*
- "Help me picture it — what did the place look like?"
- "How did that affect your day-to-day life?"

**Watch-outs for this topic**
- "Were you locked in?" / "You couldn't leave, could you?" — closed questions that presuppose confinement.
- "When they wouldn't let you leave, how did you feel?" — presumes a contested fact.
- "Did they lock the door, or post a guard outside?" — forced-choice that supplies the answer.
- "That must have felt like a prison, didn't it?" — tag question + characterizing their experience.
- Spoken jargon — "restriction of movement," "freedom of movement" — keep clinical terms out of the questions.

## 5. Wages & deductions
*Screens for: ILO forced-labour indicators — withholding or non-payment of wages, unlawful deductions, and wage manipulation. Free-recall first; specifics only later, in the person's own words.*

**Core questions**
1. To start, can you walk me through how getting paid works in your job — from doing the work to the money actually reaching you?
2. Before you began, what were you told you would earn, and who told you that?
3. How does what you actually receive compare with what you were expecting?
4. Can you describe your most recent payday for me, step by step?
5. Who decides the amount you are paid, and who actually hands you the money?
6. How and when does your pay normally reach you?
7. Tell me about anything that changes the amount you take home.
8. If money is ever taken out of your pay, what have you understood it to be for?
9. Tell me about anything you have had to pay for that is connected to this job — whether before you started or since.
10. Tell me about where you live and how you get your meals, and how those connect to your pay, if at all.
11. If you ever need money before a payday, what happens then?
12. How do you keep track of what you have earned and what you have actually been paid?
13. When there is a question or disagreement about your pay, how is that handled, and by whom?
14. Once you have your money, who decides how it is used or where it goes?
15. Is there anything about your pay or money that you have been thinking about that we have not talked about yet?

**Topic follow-ups** *(plus the Universal probes)*
- "When you say the money was [held / kept / taken], what does that look like day to day?"
- "Has the way you are paid changed at all over time? If so, tell me about that."
- "What did you understand the agreement to be, and what actually happened?"
- "If you decided to leave this job, how would that work with your pay or with anything you might owe?"
- "Is there anything written down — a payslip, a contract, a message — that shows how this works? Only if you have it and feel comfortable sharing it."

**Watch-outs for this topic**
- "They were stealing your wages, weren't they?" — supplies your conclusion for agreement.
- "Did your employer illegally withhold your pay or make illegal deductions?" — asserts a legal conclusion; also closed.
- "How much did they cheat you out of?" — presumes a crime and an amount.
- "Were you paid? On time? In full? In cash?" — rapid-fire closed chain; use one open prompt.
- "When they took your passport to control your pay, how did that feel?" — embeds unverified facts (passport taken; purpose was control).
- "If you tell me they stole from you, I can get all your wages back." — inducement that contaminates the account.

## 6. Threats, coercion & safety
*Screens for: ILO forced-labour indicators — intimidation and threats, menace of penalty, isolation. Use conditional, perpetrator-neutral framing (the safe counterpart to "Did they threaten you?").*

**Core questions**
1. How are you feeling right now? Is there anything that would help you feel more comfortable or safe while we talk?
2. When you're ready, can you tell me in your own words about your work and where you're living?
3. Could you walk me through what a normal day looks like for you, from when you wake up to when you sleep?
4. What, if anything, has been worrying you or weighing on your mind lately?
5. Can you describe how decisions get made about things like your hours, your time off, or where you can go?
6. Tell me about the people around you day to day, at work and where you stay.
7. How do you keep in touch with your family or friends, and what has that been like?
8. If you wanted to take time off, change jobs, or leave, what do you think would happen?
9. Is there anything you've been told you have to do, or that you must not do? Can you tell me more about that?
10. Are there things that have made you feel afraid or unsafe? You can share as much or as little as you want.
11. Can you tell me about your passport or ID documents, where they are kept and who has them?
12. When you think about your own safety, or the safety of people you care about, what comes to mind?
13. Who do you turn to when something goes wrong or you need help?
14. What would need to change for you to feel safer or more at ease?

**Topic follow-ups** *(plus the Universal probes)*
- "How did that affect your day-to-day life?"
- "Whatever you choose to share, or not share, is your decision."

**Watch-outs for this topic**
- "Did they threaten you?" / "They forced you, didn't they?" — supply the answer. Use: *"What, if anything, were you told would happen if you didn't do what was asked?"*
- "Your boss took your passport, right?" — presupposes a perpetrator and event.
- "How many times did he hit you?" — presumes violence and an actor before any disclosure.
- "Most people in your situation were abused, were you?" — normative pressure suggesting the expected answer.
- "You're safe now, everything will be fine." — false reassurance you can't guarantee; be honest about what you can and can't do.

## 7. Identifying a minor
*Screens for: whether a person is a child (NICHD child-interview protocol; UNODC/ILO guidance). Assess age **indirectly** through caregivers, schooling, and life events. Interview the child **alone** with an independent interpreter; if uncertain, treat as a minor.*

**Core questions**
1. To start, can you tell me a little about yourself and how things have been for you lately?
2. Tell me about the people you grew up with — who looks after you, or who do you go to when you need help?
3. When you think about your age or your birthday, what can you tell me about it?
4. Tell me about school — the last time you went, what it was like, and what you were learning.
5. What do you remember happening in your family or your town around the time you were born, or when you were very small?
6. Walk me through what a regular day was like for you before you came here, from waking up to going to sleep.
7. Who are the adults you trust, and is there someone who knows where you are right now?
8. What kinds of things do you like to do when you have free time?
9. In your own words, tell me about how you came to be here.
10. If there are any papers or anything written about you, tell me what you have.
11. Where would you go, or who would you stay with, if you left here today?
12. Is there anything about your situation that worries you, or that you'd like some help with?

**Topic follow-ups** *(plus the Universal probes)*
- "There's no right or wrong answer here — whatever you remember is okay."
- "Just so I have it right, you said [reflect their words back] — is that right?"
- "Would it feel easier to talk about this a different way, or with someone else here with us?"
- "You're not in any trouble. I'm only here to understand and to help."

**Watch-outs for this topic**
- "You're under 18, aren't you?" — closed and leading; telegraphs the wanted answer.
- "You look so young, you can't really be an adult." — disbelief; shaming and rapport-breaking.
- "Just tell me you're a minor and then I can help you." — coaches the answer; contaminates the account.
- "You're lying about your age." — accusatory; destroys trust.
- "Show me your ID right now to prove your age." — interrogation tone; triggers fear of police/immigration.
- **Never** question the child with a sponsor, employer, recruiter, or older "companion" present or interpreting, and never tie a threat or reward to the answer. When age stays genuinely uncertain, **default to minor (benefit of the doubt).**

## 8. Consent & abuse of vulnerability
*Screens for: Palermo **means** element — abuse of a position of vulnerability; and whether agreement was informed and freely given. Goal: understand the person's choices and circumstances without labeling, presupposing, or pressuring.*

**Control/consent (say first):** *"Before we start, you decide how this goes — you can pause, skip any question, take a break, or stop whenever you want, and you can ask me things too. Is it alright if we begin?"*

**Core questions**
1. *(Open invitation)* To begin, could you tell me in your own words about your work and how things have been going for you?
2. *(The original agreement)* When you first decided to take this job, what were you told about it — the pay, the hours, the kind of work, and where you would live?
3. *(Who described it)* How was the job described to you, and who described it?
4. *(Promise vs. reality)* How does what you were promised compare with how things have actually turned out?
5. *(What changed)* What, if anything, has changed about the work or the agreement since you started?
6. *(Leaving / changing your mind)* What was your understanding about whether you could stop, leave, or change your mind if you wanted to?
7. *(Options at the time)* Thinking back to when you agreed, what choices did you feel you had then?
8. *(Home situation)* What was your situation at home around the time you made this decision?
9. *(A real alternative)* If you had said no to this job, or to part of it, what do you think would have happened?
10. *(How it was arranged)* How was your coming here arranged, and who helped to arrange it?
11. *(Dependency)* Who, if anyone, were you relying on to get this work or to keep it?
12. *(Obligation / debt)* Is there anything you feel you owe to the people who arranged or gave you this work? Could you tell me about that?
13. *(Finding your footing)* What has it been like for you finding your way here — with the language, the area, and the people around you?
14. *(Everyday autonomy)* When it comes to your everyday decisions now — where you go, what you do, who you can talk to — how does that work for you?
15. *(Open the floor)* Is there anything about how you came to this work, or how decisions get made, that feels important for you to share?

**Topic follow-ups** *(plus the Universal probes)*
- "When you say '[their word]', what does that mean to you?" *(clarify their meaning — don't substitute your own)*
- "What did you understand the agreement to be, and what actually happened?"
- "Would it be easier to talk about this in your first language? We can bring in an independent interpreter you're comfortable with."

**Watch-outs for this topic**
- "They kept your passport, didn't they?" / "You were forced to stay, right?" — tag questions that put words in the person's mouth.
- "Did you come for work, or were you deceived?" — false either/or that omits their real answer; stay open.
- Using *consent*, *coercion*, *trafficking*, or *victim* as a judgment — let the person describe events; labels can feel untrue and can taint a later account.
- "You need to tell me everything now." / "Start at the beginning and don't leave anything out." — pressures disclosure and a fixed chronology.
- "Tell me everything and I'll make sure you get a visa / you'll be safe." — leverage that is coercive and destroys trust.

---

# Screening-interview reasoning protocol

# Screening-Interview Reasoning Protocol
### How an AI safety assistant should reason over a migrant worker's account: mark each topic, ASK the gaps, then map to Palermo and remedies

> This protocol governs the assistant's **internal chain of thought** while processing a worker's first-person account during a trauma-informed, non-leading screen. It is built on the PEACE investigative-interviewing model (information-gathering, not interrogation). Its single hardest rule: **the assistant produces a structured, evidence-tagged screening aid and a list of questions still to ask — it does not produce a verdict.** The trafficking determination is a trained human's job, made later, on a fuller record.

---

## 0. What this protocol is and is not

| It IS | It is NOT |
|---|---|
| A reasoning discipline for sorting an account into YES / NO / **UNKNOWN** per topic, against the evidence the person actually gave | A legal determination of trafficking, forced labour, or victim status |
| A generator of the next **non-leading** questions needed to close the gaps | An interrogation, a credibility test, or a confession-seeking script |
| A private mapping from lived facts to Palermo elements and ILO indicators | A vocabulary to use *with* the person ("so you were trafficked / enslaved") |
| A trigger for **immediate** safety and referral, independent of how complete the legal picture is | A reason to delay safety until the elements are "proven" |

The legal frame lives **only in the assistant's head**. It shapes what gaps matter; it never shapes the words spoken to the person.

---

## 1. The core discipline: observe → mark → ASK, never conclude early

### 1.1 Three-state marking, with an evidence standard

Every checklist topic is marked **YES**, **NO**, or **UNKNOWN**, and the mark must carry the evidence:

- **YES** — the person **stated or described** the thing. Quote or closely paraphrase their own words as the evidence. (Indicator affirmatively present.)
- **NO** — the person **affirmatively indicated the absence** ("I keep my own passport, it's here in my bag"). A real, stated negative.
- **UNKNOWN** — anything else: not raised, raised ambiguously, partially described, or the person could not/would not say. **This is the default.**

### 1.2 The silence rule (the most common screening failure)

> **Absence of mention is UNKNOWN, never NO.**

If the person did not talk about violence, do not mark violence "NO." Mark it **UNKNOWN** and carry forward a gentle question. Treating silence as "indicator absent" is the single biggest cause of under-identifying victims. A frightened, trauma-affected, or coached person omits far more than they invent.

### 1.3 Partial evidence stays partial

If the account supports part of a topic but not the whole ("a man took my passport *for processing*"), mark the confirmed part and keep the rest UNKNOWN ("can she retrieve it on demand?" — not yet known). **Do not round a partial up to a confident YES, or down to NO.**

### 1.4 Two layers, kept strictly apart

| Layer | Contents | Spoken aloud? |
|---|---|---|
| **Observation** | What the person said, in their words | The person hears only open, non-leading questions |
| **Private mapping** | Which Palermo element / ILO indicator each fact informs | **Never.** Stays in the assistant's reasoning |

The legal characterization must never leak backward into the question. The assistant privately needs to know "did a *means* operate?" — and asks "**What happened to your passport?**", not "Did they confiscate your documents to control you?"

---

## 2. Cross-cutting reasoning rules (standing instructions, every pass)

These outrank the checklist structure.

1. **Needs and safety before narrative.** Before substantive reasoning, confirm the gate (Cluster 0). A hungry, exhausted, frightened, or surveilled person cannot give a reliable account, and the assistant must not push for one.
2. **Free recall first.** Let the whole account arrive uninterrupted before generating any targeted question. Freely-recalled memory is the most accurate; questions reshape it.
3. **Funnel, every time.** Open invitation → open probe → a **specific closed question only to fill a gap the person already opened.** Never start narrow.
4. **TED + 5WH.** Frame questions as "**T**ell me…", "**E**xplain…", "**D**escribe…", then who / what / where / when / how. Use **"why" sparingly** — to a victim "why didn't you leave?" reads as blame.
5. **Never lead.** A leading question supplies its own answer and contaminates the account. Every generated question is checked against this before it is offered.
6. **One question at a time.** No compound or multiple-choice questions.
7. **Don't relabel.** Use the person's own words for their experience. "Trafficked," "enslaved," "victim," "forced labour" are mapping terms, not interview terms.
8. **Inconsistency ≠ lying.** Trauma fragments memory — non-linear, sensory, patchy, flat affect, repetition. Gaps are a clinical feature, **not** a credibility deduction. Never lower a mark because the account is disordered.
9. **No false promises.** The assistant never implies a visa, money, non-deportation, or a prosecution outcome. Broken promises destroy trust and are often the exact lie the trafficker used.
10. **Child flag is a hard interrupt.** If the person may be under 18, flag it immediately — it changes the Palermo test (see §5) and triggers child-specialist referral (e.g., ECPAT-type services). When age is uncertain, treat as a child for protection purposes.
11. **Smuggling ≠ trafficking — keep the screen open.** "They paid to cross illegally" or "they came on a false visa" does **not** close a trafficking screen. Smuggling is consensual movement (a crime against the state, ending at the border); trafficking is exploitation (a crime against the person, continuing after). A person can be smuggled and **then** trafficked. Never let irregular entry knock out a means or purpose mark.

## 3. Per-topic chain-of-thought template

For every checklist topic, the assistant reasons in this fixed shape:

```
TOPIC:            <name>
PRIVATE MAPPING:  <which Palermo element / ILO indicator this informs>   [never spoken]
EVIDENCE:         <the person's own words, quoted or close-paraphrased — or "none in account">
MARK:             YES | NO | UNKNOWN
REASONING:        <only what the evidence supports; silence/ambiguity -> UNKNOWN>
NEXT QUESTION:    <TED-framed, non-leading question — present whenever MARK != YES,
                   and even on YES when corroborating detail is still needed>
AVOID:            <the leading version that must NOT be asked>
```

The presence of a **NEXT QUESTION** on every non-YES (and many YES) lines is what operationalizes *ask, don't conclude*. The assistant's output is as much a question queue as it is a findings table.

## 4. The screening checklist

Each topic lists what it privately informs, a **non-leading** question to ask when it is UNKNOWN or partial, and the **leading anti-pattern** to avoid. Cluster 0 is a gate; Clusters 1–7 are the indicator set (aligned to the ILO indicators of forced labour and to Palermo's Act/Means/Purpose).

### Cluster 0 — Safety, capacity, and the conditions for a reliable account *(gate — resolve before substantive topics)*

| Topic | Ask (non-leading) | Avoid (leading) |
|---|---|---|
| Controller present / reachable / phone monitored | "Tell me who knows you're here right now." / "Tell me about your phone — who can see it?" | "Is your boss outside?" (yes/no, and tips your hand) |
| Immediate physical safety & needs (food, water, warmth, medical, rest) | "Before we talk, tell me what you need right now." | (none — just meet the need) |
| Able to engage (not in acute crisis, exhaustion, intoxication) | "How are you feeling — are you up to talking, or do you need to rest first?" | Pressing on regardless |
| Independent, neutral interpreter in correct language/dialect, ideally same gender | (operational, not asked of the person) — **never** a family member, fellow worker, or anyone the controller could reach | Using whoever the person arrived with |
| **Age / possible child** | "Tell me your date of birth." / "How old were you when this began?" | "You're an adult, right?" |
| Understanding & voluntariness | "Before we go on, I want to explain what happens with what you tell me — is that okay?" | Implying obligation to answer |

> If Cluster 0 is not safe/ready, the assistant **pauses substantive screening**, surfaces safety actions, and asks only what is needed to secure the person.

### Cluster 1 — Recruitment & journey  → *Palermo ACT; Means: deception, abuse of vulnerability*

| Topic | Ask | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| How recruited (agency, broker, friend, online ad) | "Tell me how you came to take this job." | "Did a recruiter trick you?" |
| Promises vs reality (job, pay, conditions) — **deception** | "Describe what you were told the job and pay would be." *(then later)* "Describe how it turned out." | "They lied about the wages, didn't they?" |
| Who arranged transport/travel — **act: transportation** | "Walk me through how you travelled here — who arranged it?" | "Did they smuggle you in?" |
| Recruitment/placement fees charged — **origin of debt** | "Tell me about any money you had to pay to get the job." | "How much did they charge you to traffic you?" |

### Cluster 2 — Documents, movement, isolation  → *ILO: document retention, restriction of movement, isolation*

| Topic | Ask | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Identity/travel documents — who holds them, retrievable on demand | "Tell me about your passport and papers — where are they now?" | "Did your boss take your passport?" |
| Freedom of movement (come and go from work/accommodation) | "Walk me through a normal day — how do you come and go from where you stay?" | "Are you locked in?" |
| Isolation (contact with outside, phone, guarded, surveilled) | "Tell me how you keep in touch with people outside." | "They keep you cut off, right?" |

### Cluster 3 — Money, debt, wages  → *ILO: debt bondage, withholding of wages; Means: position of vulnerability, coercion*

| Topic | Ask | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Paid what was promised, in full, on time | "Tell me how your pay works." / "Describe what you've actually received." | "They're not paying you, are they?" |
| Deductions (housing, food, "fees") | "Tell me about anything taken out of your pay." | "They deduct everything, don't they?" |
| Debt to employer/recruiter — size, how calculated, whether it grows, ever payable — **debt bondage** | *(only if they raised a debt)* "You mentioned money owed — explain how that came about and how it changes." | "It's a debt you can never repay, isn't it?" |
| Control of own earnings / bank account | "Tell me who handles the money you earn." | "They control your bank account, right?" |

### Cluster 4 — Coercion, threats, violence  → *ILO: intimidation & threats, physical/sexual violence; Palermo Means: force, coercion, threat; ILO C29 "menace of any penalty"*

| Topic | Ask | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Belief about consequences of leaving/refusing — **the core of "menace of any penalty"** | "Tell me what you understood would happen if you stopped working or left." | "They threatened you if you tried to leave, didn't they?" |
| Threats (to person or family; immigration, police, shame) | "Tell me about anything that's made you afraid." | "Did they threaten your family?" |
| Violence — physical or sexual (self or witnessed) | *(open first)* "Describe how people are treated where you work." *(specific only to fill a gap they opened)* | "Did he hit you?" / any forced-choice on abuse |
| Withholding of identity as control / threats to report to authorities | "Tell me what you were told about your visa or your status here." | "They said they'd call immigration on you, right?" |

> Sensitive abuse topics: ask **open**, accept silence, and leave the door open ("you can tell me about that whenever you're ready"). UNKNOWN here is expected and is **not** evidence of absence.

### Cluster 5 — Work & living conditions  → *ILO: abusive working/living conditions, excessive overtime*

| Topic | Ask | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Hours, rest days, breaks — **excessive overtime** | "Walk me through your working hours and days off." | "You work all the time with no breaks, right?" |
| Working conditions (safety, equipment, refusal possible) | "Describe the work and the conditions you do it in." | "It's dangerous and they don't care, correct?" |
| Living conditions (where they sleep, who pays, crowding, freedom to leave) | "Describe where you sleep and eat." | "They keep you in a crowded room, don't they?" |
| Ability to refuse work or overtime | "Tell me what happens if you say you're too tired or unwell to work." | "You can't ever say no, can you?" |

### Cluster 6 — Nature/purpose of the work  → *Palermo PURPOSE (exploitation type)*

| Topic | Ask | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| What kind of work/service (labour, domestic, sex, begging, forced criminality, organ removal) — sets the **exploitation category** | "Tell me about the work you actually do day to day." | Naming a category for them |

### Cluster 7 — Consent & "choice" *(reason about privately; do not challenge)*

- Privately note whether any **means** operated (force, fraud, deception, coercion, abuse of vulnerability/power, payment to a controller). **If a means operated, the person's consent to the intended exploitation is legally irrelevant** (Palermo Art. 3(b)).
- Therefore **never** mark a means "NO" because "they agreed to come" or "they could have left." Those are not negatives — they are often the product of the means itself.
- **Never** ask "why didn't you leave?" That question both contaminates the screen and re-traumatizes.

## 5. Mapping to Palermo *(after gathering, in the assistant's head)*

Palermo Art. 3 defines trafficking by three elements. Map the marked checklist onto them, **carrying the UNKNOWNs forward as gaps, not as zeros.**

| Element | Built from | Adult test | Child (<18) test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACT** (recruit / transport / transfer / harbour / receive) | Clusters 1, 2 | Required | Required |
| **MEANS** (force, threat, coercion, abduction, fraud, deception, abuse of power **or of a position of vulnerability**, payment to a controller) | Clusters 1, 3, 4, 7 | Required | **Not required** |
| **PURPOSE** (exploitation — "at a minimum" sexual exploitation, forced labour/services, slavery or similar, servitude, organ removal; list is non-exhaustive) | Clusters 5, 6 | Required | Required |

**Provisional logic:**

- **Adult:** trafficking is *indicated* only when **ACT and MEANS and PURPOSE are each YES**. If any is **UNKNOWN**, the screen is **incomplete** — output the gap questions; **do not record "no trafficking."**
- **Child:** **ACT + PURPOSE** suffices; means is irrelevant. A child flag therefore lowers the evidentiary bar and raises urgency.
- **Consent:** where any means is present, consent is irrelevant — do not let "they agreed" subtract from MEANS.
- **Smuggling:** irregular/illegal entry does not satisfy or negate any element — keep it out of the Palermo reasoning except as context for *abuse of vulnerability*.

State the provisional mapping with its uncertainty explicitly, e.g.: *"ACT = YES; PURPOSE = YES (domestic servitude); MEANS = YES-leaning (document retention + debt + threat of arrest) but exact recruitment promise UNKNOWN. Indication strong; one means detail outstanding."*

---

## 6. Mapping to ILO forced labour *(parallel frame)*

Trafficking and forced labour overlap but are not identical — map to **both**, because remedies differ and forced labour can exist with no "movement"/act.

- **ILO Convention No. 29 test:** work or service **exacted under the menace of any penalty**, for which the person **has not offered themselves voluntarily.** Cluster 4 (penalty/menace) and Clusters 1/7 (voluntariness) are the core.
- **The 11 ILO indicators**, mapped from the checklist: abuse of vulnerability · deception · restriction of movement · isolation · physical/sexual violence · intimidation & threats · retention of documents · withholding of wages · debt bondage · abusive working & living conditions · excessive overtime.
- Record each indicator as YES / **UNKNOWN** / NO with its evidence. **Several YES indicators strongly suggest forced labour**, but the assistant reports the pattern; it does not pronounce the conclusion.

---

## 7. Mapping to remedies & referral *(triggered by what the screen surfaced, not by a final verdict)*

Remedies are sequenced. **Immediate-tier actions fire now, regardless of how many topics are UNKNOWN.**

1. **Immediate (always, on any reasonable indication or even safety doubt):** secure physical safety; meet basic and medical needs; move to a safe location away from the controller; engage a trained independent interpreter; **refer to the national referral pathway / anti-trafficking first responder.**
   - *Volatile contacts (hotline numbers, NRM portals, shelter intake lines, office addresses) must be pulled from a current resource lookup or vetted knowledge pack — never recited from memory, as they change.* Name the **mechanism** ("the national human-trafficking hotline / National Referral Mechanism first responder"); resolve the specific number/link at runtime.
2. **Status protection:** reflection/recovery period; **non-punishment principle** (the person must not be penalized for offences committed as a direct consequence of being trafficked — including immigration offences); appropriate immigration relief (e.g., trafficking-victim residence permit / T-visa-type protection). **Treat the person as a possible victim, not as an immigration offender.**
3. **Financial remedies:** recovery of unpaid/withheld wages; compensation/restitution; labour-tribunal or civil claims for the conditions endured.
4. **Justice (only if the person chooses):** report to police / specialist trafficking unit; victim support and protection through any proceedings — **never promised as an outcome.**
5. **Longer-term:** legal aid; regularization; **voluntary, safe** return only if the person chooses it and it is safe; NGO casework continuity.

**Named organizations** to route toward as appropriate (public bodies/NGOs, safe to name): Polaris Project, International Justice Mission (IJM), ECPAT (children), Anti-Slavery International, La Strada (Europe), and origin-state labour-migration bodies (e.g., POEA/DMW and BP2MI, HRD-type services in Nepal). Match the corridor and the person's needs; confirm current contact details via lookup.

## 8. The output the assistant produces (per screening pass)

The assistant emits a structured aid, **not** a conclusion:

1. **Safety flags** — controller presence, surveillance, acute needs, danger-on-leaving.
2. **Child flag** — yes / no / **age UNKNOWN → treat as child**.
3. **Per-topic table** — `topic | mark (YES/NO/UNKNOWN) | evidence (their words) | next question`.
4. **Open-questions queue** — every non-YES topic's non-leading question, ordered for a funnelled follow-up.
5. **Palermo provisional** — Act / Means / Purpose each as YES / NO / **UNKNOWN**, with the adult-vs-child test applied and gaps named.
6. **ILO indicators** — the 11, each marked with evidence.
7. **Recommended immediate actions + referral mechanisms** (contacts resolved via lookup).
8. **Standing disclaimer:** *"This is a trauma-informed screening aid generated from the person's account. It is not a legal determination of trafficking, forced labour, or victim status; that decision belongs to a trained human assessor on a fuller record. Several topics remain UNKNOWN and require the questions above."*

## 9. Worked example *(composite — synthetic, no real person)*

**Account (free recall, paraphrased):** *"Maria"* says a friend's contact arranged a cleaning job abroad with "good pay"; she paid a fee to get it; on arrival a man took her passport "for processing"; she works about 16 hours a day and sleeps in the kitchen; she has not been paid for three months "until the debt is cleared"; she was told that if she leaves, the police will arrest her because her visa is "under the boss." She does not mention any violence.

**Reasoned marks:**

| Topic | Mark | Evidence | Next question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruitment / deception | UNKNOWN (partial) | "good pay," fee paid; exact promise vs reality not stated | "Describe exactly what you were told the pay and job would be." |
| Document retention | YES | "a man took my passport for processing"; not returned | "Tell me what happens if you ask for your passport back." *(retrievability still UNKNOWN)* |
| Restriction of movement | UNKNOWN | sleeping in kitchen ≠ confinement | "Walk me through how you come and go from the house." |
| Withholding of wages | YES | "not paid for three months" | "Tell me about any pay you have received, and when." |
| Debt bondage | YES-leaning | "until the debt is cleared" | "Explain how that debt was worked out and whether it changes." |
| Threats / menace of penalty | YES-leaning | "police will arrest me if I leave" (immigration-based coercion) | "Tell me who told you that, and what you understood it to mean." |
| Violence | **UNKNOWN (not NO)** | not mentioned | *(open, gentle)* "Describe how people are treated where you work." |
| Excessive overtime / abusive conditions | YES | "16 hours a day," "sleeps in the kitchen" | "Walk me through your hours and your days off." |
| Purpose | YES (domestic servitude / forced labour) | nature of the work | — |
| Child? | UNKNOWN | DOB not given | "Tell me your date of birth." |

**Palermo provisional (adult, pending age):** ACT = YES (harbouring/receipt) · MEANS = YES-leaning (document retention + debt + immigration-based threat + abuse of vulnerability; recruitment-deception detail UNKNOWN) · PURPOSE = YES-leaning (forced labour/domestic servitude). **Indication strong; do not conclude — close the UNKNOWNs first.**

**Action now, despite the UNKNOWNs:** secure safety away from the "boss"; check whether her phone is monitored; meet immediate needs; engage an independent interpreter; refer to the national anti-trafficking first responder; apply the non-punishment lens to her "illegal" visa status. The legal determination waits; the safety response does not.

## 10. Failure modes / guardrails (self-check before emitting)

- **Premature conclusion.** Any element or topic marked NO that is really only *unmentioned* → correct to UNKNOWN.
- **Leaked legal frame.** Any generated question containing a Palermo/ILO term, a relabel, or a supplied answer → rewrite as TED/open.
- **Leading or compound questions.** Any "…didn't they?", forced-choice, or two-in-one → split and de-lead.
- **Credibility penalty for trauma.** Any mark lowered because the account was fragmented, repetitive, or flat → reverse; inconsistency ≠ lying.
- **Means erased by consent.** Any means marked NO because "they agreed / could have left" → restore; consent is irrelevant where means operate.
- **Smuggling closing the screen.** Any element zeroed out because of irregular entry → reopen.
- **Child test missed.** Age UNKNOWN but adult test applied → switch to child test / treat as child.
- **False promise.** Any output implying a visa, money, non-deportation, or prosecution outcome → remove.
- **Safety deferred.** Immediate-tier actions withheld pending "proof" → fire them now.

> The protocol succeeds when the assistant ends a pass with **more well-formed questions and a clear safety action than conclusions.** Concluding is the human assessor's privilege; **asking, safely and without leading, is the assistant's job.**
