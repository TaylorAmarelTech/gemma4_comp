# Labor Supply-Chain Audit Framework for Migrant-Labor-Reliant Electronics and Semiconductor Manufacturing

*A unified framework for auditing forced-labor and trafficking risk across the recruitment corridor, the dispatch/dormitory layer, the production tiers, and the upstream raw-material tiers that supply assembly, component, test-and-packaging, and wafer-fabrication operations.*

> **The thesis of this framework.** In electronics and semiconductor manufacturing the audited factory is usually compliant *on its own books*. Forced labor is **manufactured upstream in the recruitment corridor** (origin-country sub-agents, debt, document deception) and **sideways in the on-site labor chain** (dispatch/outsourced workers, dormitory operators, service contractors, and fab construction/tool-install crews). A line-walk and a payroll sample cannot surface it. This framework exists to make the audit *honest*: it privileges worker-controlled evidence over management documents, traces every worker back to the first person who recruited them, extends scope below Tier 1 and into the raw-material tier, and treats detection without verified remedy as a failure rather than a finding.

## Foundations — the shared spine

Every module below rests on one operational backbone, one normative basis, one diagnostic indicator set, and one set of decision rules. They are stated once here so the modules stay scoped and so every finding is cited to a specific instrument and article.

### F.1 The due-diligence cycle (UNGP + OECD)

All mandatory laws and voluntary codes in this framework are built on the **UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (UNGPs, 2011)** "Protect, Respect, Remedy" framework, operationalized through the **OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible Business Conduct (2018)** six-step cycle:

1. **Embed** responsible business conduct into policy and management systems.
2. **Identify and assess** actual and potential adverse impacts in own operations, the supply chain, and business relationships.
3. **Cease, prevent, and mitigate** adverse impacts.
4. **Track** implementation and results.
5. **Communicate** how impacts are addressed.
6. **Provide for or cooperate in remediation** when the enterprise caused or contributed to harm.

Audit every supplier's labor-rights program against these six steps regardless of which statute has jurisdiction — it is the lowest common denominator across LkSG, CSDDD, the OECD Guidelines, the RBA Code, and (increasingly) the Modern Slavery Acts. **Prioritise by severity × likelihood**, not by who is easiest to audit. Due diligence is a *continuous process*, not an annual snapshot; a passed audit does not discharge it.

### F.2 Master normative basis (cite the specific provision in every finding)

**Forced labor, trafficking, and debt bondage**

| Instrument | Operative requirement |
|---|---|
| **ILO C29 Forced Labour (1930) + 2014 Protocol (P029)** | Prohibits forced/compulsory labor (Art. 2); **P029 Art. 2(d)** obliges protection from abusive/fraudulent recruitment; **P029 Art. 4** obliges access to effective remedy and compensation. |
| **ILO C105 Abolition of Forced Labour (1957)** | Prohibits forced labor as coercion/discipline. *(South Korea has not ratified C105 — a documented gap.)* |
| **Supplementary Convention on the Abolition of Slavery (1956), Art. 1(a)** | Defines **debt bondage** — services pledged as security for a debt whose value is not applied to liquidating it, or whose length/nature is not limited and defined. |

| **ILO Indicators of Forced Labour (2012)** | The 11-indicator on-site diagnostic screen (see F.3). |

**Recruitment and migrant-worker protection**

|---|---|

**Freedom of association and harassment**

| Instrument | Operative requirement |
|---|---|
| **ILO C87 (1948), C98 (1949)** | Freedom of association and the right to organise and bargain collectively. |
| **ILO C190 (2019)** | Grievance pathways for gender-based violence and harassment. |

**Industry code, assurance, and benchmarks**

- **RBA (Responsible Business Alliance) Code of Conduct v8.0 (effective 1 Jan 2024)** — the electronics-industry standard (formerly EICC). **A1 Freely Chosen Employment**: no forced/bonded/indentured/trafficked labor; **no worker-paid recruitment fees, and any fees found "shall be repaid to the worker"**; no retention of IDs/passports/permits except as legally required; freedom of movement and to resign; written contract in the worker's native language **before departure**; no contract substitution (operationalized by the **RBA Definition of Fees**, effective 1 Jan 2021). **A2** Young Workers; **A3** Working Hours (≤60h/week incl. overtime save genuine emergencies, ≥1 day off in 7); **A4** Wages & Benefits (no disciplinary deductions; timely full payment); **A5** Humane Treatment; **A6** Non-Discrimination/Non-Harassment; **A7** Freedom of Association; **Management System** worker feedback/participation/grievance + non-retaliation. Verified through the **RBA Validated Assessment Program (VAP)**, the **Responsible Labor Initiative (RLI)**, and the **RBA Responsible Recruitment Due Diligence Toolkit**.
- **Benchmarks/monitoring:** **KnowTheChain (ICT benchmark)**; **World Benchmarking Alliance Corporate Human Rights Benchmark (CHRB, ICT)**; **Electronics Watch** (worker-driven monitoring for public buyers); **Transparentem**; **Verité** (Fair Hiring Toolkit/CUMULUS; 2014 Malaysian electronics study); **Issara Institute**; **Responsible Minerals Initiative (RMI/RMAP)** for 3TG; **IFC/EBRD "Workers' Accommodation: Processes and Standards" (2009)**.
- **Worker-driven social responsibility precedents (consequence-bearing):** **Fair Food Program** (Coalition of Immokalee Workers); **Bangladesh Accord → International Accord**; **Dindigul Agreement**; **Global Framework Agreements** (IndustriALL Global Union).

**Legal and market-access regime (the teeth)**

- **US:** Tariff Act 1930 **§307 / 19 U.S.C. §1307** — **Withhold Release Orders (WROs)** and Findings bar forced-labor goods; **UFLPA (2021, effective 21 Jun 2022)** — rebuttable presumption against XUAR-linked goods and **UFLPA Entity List**, FLETF strategy (priority sectors include **polysilicon** and **aluminium**); **FAR 52.222-50** (no recruitment fees for federal contractors).
- **EU:** **CSDDD — Directive (EU) 2024/1760** (Omnibus I delayed transposition to **26 Jul 2028** / application **26 Jul 2029**; thresholds narrowed); **EU Forced Labour Regulation — Reg. (EU) 2024/3015** (applicable **Dec 2027**, products linked to forced labor at any tier barred from the EU market).
- **Member states / others:** **Germany LkSG** (in force 2023; ≥1,000 DE employees from 2024; BAFA; **reporting obligation suspended in 2025 pending CSDDD alignment — substantive duties remain**); France **Duty of Vigilance Law (2017)**; Norway **Transparency Act (2022)**; **UK Modern Slavery Act 2015 (s.54)** (≥£36m turnover, board-approved/director-signed statement); **Australia Modern Slavery Act 2018** (≥AUD $100m revenue, seven mandatory criteria; **civil penalties + mandatory due-diligence duty in active reform**; first Anti-Slavery Commissioner appointed 2024); **California Transparency in Supply Chains Act (2010)**.

> **Evolving-law caveat (current as of mid-2026):** several mandatory regimes are in active reform (CSDDD delayed/narrowed by Omnibus I; LkSG reporting suspended; Australia penalties pending). Always audit against the *current consolidated text* and the supplier's *operating jurisdictions*, never a static snapshot.

### F.3 The ILO 11 indicators of forced labour (the diagnostic spine)

Every module uses these as the detection lens. No single indicator is dispositive; **clusters are**, and **two or more co-occurring indicators warrant escalation**.

*Abuse of vulnerability · deception · restriction of movement · isolation · physical and sexual violence · intimidation and threats · **retention of identity documents** · withholding of wages · **debt bondage** · abusive working and living conditions · excessive overtime.*

The classic electronics fact pattern — **recruitment debt + document retention + recruitment-linked wage deductions co-occurring** — is precisely what has triggered CBP Withhold Release Orders in the sector.

### F.4 Decision rules, severity grading, and the remedy hierarchy

**Standing decision rules (apply throughout):**

- **EPP is the standard, not local law.** A fee that is *lawful under origin-country law or below a government cap is still a nonconformity* if the worker bore it. "Lawful in corridor" never equals "EPP-compliant."
- **Privilege worker-controlled evidence.** Documents can be staged; confidential off-site worker testimony is the decisive evidence. Treat the conventional social audit as one input of low-to-moderate weight, never as assurance.
- **The absence of grievances is a red flag, not a pass.** Silence indicates fear or inaccessibility.
- **Detection ≠ remedy.** A finding without verified worker-level remedy (fee repayment, back-wages, document return, reinstatement, anti-retaliation) is not compliance.
- **Trace to the first point of recruitment.** The audit is incomplete until each worker can be traced through every horizontal hop to the person who first recruited them, with fees reconciled.

**Severity grading (recommended scale):**

| Grade | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Critical / zero-tolerance** | Worker-paid fees with no compliant remediation **and** any active bondage indicator (document retention, deposit/bond, debt-linked deduction); or any item in the consolidated auto-fail set (Appendix A). Blocks conformance; requires immediate remediation and re-audit. |
| **Major** | Fees identified with absent/inadequate remediation; chain unmapped; substantive system failure with worker impact. |
| **Minor** | System/documentation gap without confirmed worker-borne cost or active bondage indicator. |

**Corrective-action and remedy hierarchy (remedy-first):**

1. **Remediate the worker first** — repay full recruitment cost (incl. interest) to current *and* reachable former workers; return retained documents; cancel coercive debts; restore freedom of movement. Remedy is owed *before* commercial decisions. Remedy types in order: restitution → compensation → fee repayment → document return → reinstatement → rehabilitation/support → guarantees of non-repetition.
2. **Fix the system** — re-contract agencies on EPP terms; certify/upgrade dormitories; bring contractors into scope; reform purchasing practices.
3. **Verify** — re-audit with worker voice; update the living map.
4. **Escalate / responsible exit** — persistent indicators trigger time-bound improvement plans and, if unresolved, **responsible disengagement** (use leverage, never an abrupt cut that strands workers with their debt).

### F.5 Where the risk concentrates (sector salience)

Two nodes carry the highest severity × likelihood and must be mapped first:

1. **Cross-border recruitment of migrant workers** into assembly, component, test/packaging, and fabrication sites (Malaysia, Taiwan, Thailand, Singapore, China; corridors from Nepal, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Myanmar, Vietnam, the Philippines, India) — where recruitment-fee debt bondage, passport retention, and contract substitution recur. Within this, the **origin-country sub-agent layer** and **Tier-2 dispatch labor** are where harm originates and where audited entities disclaim responsibility.
2. **Upstream raw materials** — high-purity **polysilicon** and metallurgical-grade silicon, **aluminium**, **magnesium**, **copper**, and the **3TG conflict minerals (tin, tantalum, tungsten, gold)** plus emerging **cobalt** — where forced-labor and conflict-financing exposure is severe and opaque. Due diligence must reach the smelter/refiner/polysilicon-producer tier.

Note the **scope boundary**: the migrant-labor risk concentrates in **components, EMS/assembly, fab build-out, and on-site services**, not in front-end wafer-fab operation (cleanroom operators in Taiwan/Korea are predominantly local engineers/technicians). Push exposure mapping **below Tier 1**, where the broker-dependent migrant workforce actually sits.

## Module 1 — Ethical Recruitment and the Employer Pays Principle

*Zero worker-paid fees; dismantling the broker-fee debt-bondage model.*

### 1.1 Purpose and scope

This module audits whether a site and the recruitment chain that staffs it conform to the **Employer Pays Principle**: *no worker pays for a job; the full cost of recruitment is borne by the employer.* It targets the single most prevalent forced-labor risk in electronics — the **broker-fee debt-bondage model**, in which migrant workers incur recruitment debt (often several months' to over a year's wages) before they earn a paycheck. Scope is the **entire recruitment chain across the migration corridor**: destination-country agents/subcontractors, origin-country agencies, village-level sub-agents and "runners," and any in-house recruiter. Document retention, deposits, wage deductions, and freedom-of-movement controls are in scope here because they are the **mechanisms by which a recruitment debt is converted into bondage**.

Normative basis: see Foundations F.2, with particular weight on **ILO C181 Art. 7**, the **ILO 2019 Definition of Recruitment Fees and Related Costs**, the **Employer Pays Principle**, **Supplementary Convention on Slavery (1956) Art. 1(a)**, **RBA A1**, **CGF Priority Industry Principles**, and **FAR 52.222-50**.

### 1.2 The harm model auditors are testing for

1. A village/origin sub-agent recruits the worker and charges a fee (cash, often undocumented), frequently financed by a high-interest loan or sale of land.
2. The fee is split up a chain (sub-agent → origin agency → destination agency → sometimes a kickback toward the employer/in-house recruiter), so **no single receipt reflects the total**.
3. The worker arrives already indebted; **passport/permit retention, salary deductions, security deposits, and contract substitution** then lock the worker in until the debt is "worked off."
4. The result satisfies multiple ILO indicators at once — the exact pattern behind CBP WROs against electronics-sector manufacturers.

Because the money moves **off-book and in the origin country**, **destination-site document review alone cannot clear this risk.** Confidential worker interviews are the primary evidence; documents triangulate.

### 1.3 Definitions the auditor must apply

- **Recruitment fee** — any charge for securing employment/placement, however named ("service," "processing," "training," "agency," "placement"), whenever and wherever levied.
- **Related costs** — costs integral to recruitment that the *employer* must bear: medical exams, insurance, visa/work-permit/passport/document processing, mandatory pre-departure training/orientation, skills/language testing, and travel/transport to the job (and home on completion).
- **Illegitimate costs** — never chargeable (bonds/security deposits as a condition of hire, document-"release" fees, costs of replacing the agent).
- **Total cost of recruitment (TCR)** — the metric to reimburse: the **full amount actually borne by the worker, including loan interest**, across the whole chain — not the destination-agency invoice and not the legal cap.

### 1.4 Methodology and evidence

Corridor mapping (enumerate every origin corridor in the current and prior-12-month workforce; over-sample newest hires and those recruited under prior agents) → **full-chain intermediary mapping** (an incomplete map is itself a finding) → **confidential worker interviews** off the floor, in the worker's first language, gender-balanced, no management present → **document triangulation** (payslips, deduction ledgers, dual contract versions, agency contracts, custody logs, repayment records, grievance logs) → **three-way triangulation** (worker testimony × site records × agency records; material divergence is evidence of off-book fees, not a clerical gap).

### 1.5 Signature audit questions (full set in Appendix A, §A1–A6)

- Did workers pay **anything** to obtain the job — to an agent, sub-agent, "runner," trainer, medical clinic, or the employer — in the **origin or destination** country? Can they state the original amount, balance, interest, and deduction schedule?
- Does the site's **TCR definition** match the ILO definition (fees + related costs + interest, whole chain), or is it narrowed to the destination invoice or a legal cap?
- Where fees were found, does the **repayment program** reimburse the full TCR including interest, timely, to current **and** reachable former workers — established by credible corridor proxy where receipts are missing — and has the **channel been structurally fixed** so the next cohort arrives debt-free?

### 1.6 Signature red flags

- Any worker-paid fee — including amounts "within the legal cap" or labeled service/processing/training.
- Recruitment debt/loans financing migration, with balances "worked off" via deductions; total fees exceeding several months' corridor wages.
- "Remediation" that repays only the legal cap or destination-agency portion, excludes interest or former workers, or is staggered indefinitely while the same channel keeps producing indebted cohorts.
- Origin-only or off-book cash fees dismissed as out of scope because they predate the destination contract.

### 1.7 Conformance summary

A site conforms only when **(a)** no worker bore any recruitment fee or related cost across the whole corridor, **(b)** workers freely hold their documents and can leave without penalty, **(c)** any historical fees are fully repaid (TCR + interest, current and former workers), and **(d)** the channel has been structurally fixed. Policy on paper without (a)–(d) is a nonconformity.

## Module 2 — Labor Supply-Chain Mapping and Tiered Due Diligence

*Recruiters · sub-agents · dormitories · sub-contractors.*

### 2.1 Why mapping is the first control

Mapping the labor chain is the **first control, not a documentation exercise** — it is the control that makes the rest of the audit honest. Normative basis: Foundations F.1 (UNGP/OECD tiered due diligence) and F.2 (RBA A1/A3/A4/A5; ILO; ICRMW Art. 21; Dhaka P1/P4/P8; IRIS), with the legal exposure of F.2's market-access regime.

### 2.2 Scope — map the *labor* chain, not the bill of materials

The BOM/3TG map is a separate map (Module 3 / RMI). This module maps **how a worker came to be working on, around, or for the production of the audited goods**, on two axes that must appear on one map:

1. **Vertical (production tiers):** own operations → Tier 1 (assembly/fab/OSAT/PCB) → Tier 2+ (process sub-contractors, dispatch/outsourced labor providers).
2. **Horizontal (recruitment corridor + on-site service chain):** destination agency → origin agency → village-level **sub-agents** → medical clinics, training centres, document fixers → transport → **dormitory** operator → on-site **service contractors** (security, cleaning, canteen, logistics, gardening) and **fab construction/tool-install** crews.

A migrant can be Tier-1 on the org chart but reach the site through four undocumented horizontal hops.

### 2.3 The tiered model and prioritisation

| Tier | Who | Default assurance posture |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 0** | Own operations / direct hires | Internal audit + worker voice |
| **Tier 1** | Direct suppliers (EMS/ODM, fab, OSAT, PCB) | RBA VAP / equivalent; announced + unannounced |
| **Tier 2+** | Sub-contractors & labor providers (dispatch, process, construction/tool-install) | Risk-tiered audit; treat agency labor as **in-scope**, not "the agency's problem" |
| **Recruitment chain** | Cross-border, spans tiers | Corridor mapping to the **first point of recruitment**; fee tracing |
| **Accommodation** | Company / third-party dormitories | Standards inspection + worker survey |

**Prioritisation rule (severity × likelihood):** map the **origin-country sub-agent layer** and **Tier-2 dispatch labor** first.

### 2.4 Mapping methodology

1. **Declare the full chain in writing** — per nationality corridor: destination agency, origin agency(ies), every sub-agent, dormitory operator, every on-site service/labor contractor. "We don't know our sub-agents" is a finding, not an answer.
2. **Build the map from the worker backwards, not the contract forwards** — the contract names the licensed agency; the worker names the person in their village who took the money. Reconstruct in **worker interviews, in the worker's language, off-site, with independent interpreters** (never a broker/supervisor/dormitory manager).
3. **Corridor profiling** — for each corridor (Nepal→Malaysia, Bangladesh→Malaysia, Vietnam/Indonesia→Taiwan, Myanmar→Thailand, etc.) document fee norms, legal maxima in both countries, and typical sub-agent structure, so a fabricated "zero-fee" story is recognisable.
4. **Triangulate** every node across documents, management interviews, and anonymous worker testimony/grievance data. A node that exists only in management's account is unverified.
5. **Living map + traceability** — versioned register reviewed at defined intervals and on any change of agency, corridor, dormitory, or contractor; each worker traceable to a first point of recruitment.

### 2.5 Actor-specific due diligence (distinctive points)

**Recruiters (destination *and* origin agencies):** verify current valid licences in **both** countries with names matching who workers actually dealt with; a written recruitment-services contract imposing EPP, document non-retention, and the right to audit sub-agents; **IRIS registration or equivalent**; the supplier's own due diligence beyond the licence. *Distinctive red flag:* agency owned by or sharing directors/family with the supplier or a supplier manager (conflict of interest).

**Sub-agents and informal brokers (where harm originates):** reconstruct every hop between the village and the factory gate — who took money, how much, cash or loan, with what receipt; borrowing with land/wages/documents as collateral; documents (passport, age, identity, qualification) handled or altered by a broker. *Distinctive red flags:* inconsistent total costs among workers from the same corridor (undisclosed informal fees); workers coached not to discuss recruitment.

**Dormitories and accommodation:** legal permit/certificate (e.g., Malaysia's **Certificate for Accommodation under Act 446 as amended 2019**) and minimum space/sanitation/ventilation/fire safety; **free entry/exit** with no curfew penalty/biometric lock-in/guards; lawful, disclosed, capped housing/utility deductions that never push net pay below minimum or into recoverable debt; **documents in worker-controlled individual lockers, never employer/dormitory custody** (ICRMW Art. 21, RBA A1, Dhaka P4); fire safety, potable water, adequate sanitation ratios, safe segregated facilities for women, and a grievance channel reachable from the dorm.

**Sub-contractors and on-site/dispatch labor providers (the routinely-excluded population — treat as in-scope):** the share of the on-site workforce that is **not** directly employed, per area/shift; for each contractor, who recruited their workers, through which corridor/sub-agents, and **whether any fees were paid** (the fee ban follows the worker, not the contract); equal pay for equal work with wages paid **directly to the worker's own account**; undisclosed onward sub-contracting of production (shadow sites/home-based work) reconciled against declared capacity; and for fab build-outs, construction/tool-install crews mapped, fee-screened, and housed to standard.

### 2.6 Evidence pack and corrective-action

A complete map produces, per corridor and node: agency licences (both countries) + IRIS status; the EPP recruitment-services contract; worker contracts (pre-departure vs. current, to test substitution); a **full fee ledger** reconciling supplier-paid vs. worker-paid costs; the sub-agent trace to first point of recruitment; the dormitory certificate + inspection + worker-survey results; the dispatch/service/construction contractor register with its own recruitment screen; and anonymised worker-interview and grievance data triangulating all of it. Apply the remedy-first corrective-action hierarchy (Foundations F.4); use **responsible disengagement**, never abrupt exit.

## Module 3 — Legal and Regulatory Frameworks for Labor Due Diligence

Migrant-labor-reliant electronics sits at the intersection of (a) normative recruitment standards, (b) mandatory import bans, (c) mandatory corporate due-diligence statutes, (d) intergovernmental soft law, and (e) transparency/disclosure laws. The common spine is the UNGP/OECD six-step cycle (Foundations F.1). Cite the specific instrument and article in every finding.

### 3.1 ILO Fair Recruitment and the Employer Pays Principle

Recruitment-fee debt is the single most common entry point to forced labor in electronics corridors, and the **ILO 2019 definition is broad** — recruitment fees are any costs charged to secure a job "regardless of the manner, timing or location," and related costs explicitly include passport/document fees, visa/work-permit costs, medical exams, skills tests, mandatory training/orientation, equipment, and travel/lodging. Audit against the full scope, not merely the headline "agency fee." Cross-reference the ILO 11 indicators when scoring (Foundations F.3). *Core tests:* board-endorsed EPP/no-fee policy mirroring the 2019 definition; full fee-chain tracing with **origin-country receipts** (not destination-only attestations); pre-departure native-language contracts with no substitution; completed fee-repayment remediation with evidence; documented recruitment-agent due diligence to the sub-agent level.

### 3.2 RBA Code of Conduct v8.0

The electronics-industry baseline (see Foundations F.2 for the A-section provisions). *Audit posture:* use VAP reports as inputs but **verify, do not accept** prior scores; require **verified corrective-action closure**, not corrective-action plans; confirm the no-fee standard explicitly covers **sub-agents** and aligns with the RBA Definition of Fees; confirm documents are in the worker's **sole, freely accessible custody**; reconcile working-hour records against **production/output and gate/turnstile logs** (watch for suspicious clustering exactly at the 60-hour cap). Move toward **semi-announced/unannounced** assessments with worker-voice components; cascade the Code and Definition of Fees contractually to next-tier suppliers and labor agents.

### 3.3 US — Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA)

UFLPA establishes a **rebuttable presumption** that goods "mined, produced, or manufactured wholly or in part" in **XUAR**, or by entities on the **UFLPA Entity List**, are made with forced labor and barred from US entry under §307; rebuttal requires **clear and convincing evidence**, full compliance with CBP/FLETF guidance, and complete response to CBP requests. There is **no de minimis** — "wholly or in part" reaches trace inputs. For semiconductors/electronics this is a **raw-material traceability** question reaching the **polysilicon/silicon, aluminium, magnesium** producer tier. *Core tests:* a **BOM-level map tracing high-risk inputs to the producing facility and country of origin** with transaction/transport documents; screening against the Entity List and XUAR "labor-transfer/pairing" programs; a **detention-response dossier** (tracing + management measures + due-diligence system); and any active/historical WROs/Findings touching the supplier or its inputs.

### 3.4 Germany — Supply Chain Due Diligence Act (LkSG)

Risk-based human-rights and environmental due diligence across own operations and **direct suppliers**, extending to **indirect suppliers on "substantiated knowledge."** Nine duties (risk-management system; responsible person; risk analyses; policy statement; preventive measures; remedial action; grievance procedure; indirect-supplier duties; documentation/reporting). **Material 2025 change:** the **BAFA reporting obligation is suspended** and enforcement directed at "serious" violations only, pending CSDDD alignment — but the **substantive due-diligence and internal-documentation duties remain in force.** *Core tests:* documented risk-management system, named human-rights officer, current risk analysis covering this supplier/corridor; an accessible **multilingual** grievance mechanism reaching the migrant workforce; an operative **"substantiated knowledge" trigger protocol** linking audit/WRO/UFLPA signals to mandatory deeper-tier action; continuous internal documentation despite the suspended report.

### 3.5 EU — Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)

Risk-based HRDD across the full **"chain of activities,"** with climate-transition-plan, civil-liability, and supervisory-enforcement provisions. **Omnibus I** delayed and narrowed scope: **transposition 26 Jul 2028 / application 26 Jul 2029**, first wave targeted at the largest enterprises (broadly ≥5,000 employees and >€1.5bn turnover under the agreed narrowing; non-EU captured via EU turnover; confirm final thresholds at audit time). Build now to avoid a transposition cliff. *Core tests:* scope determination and a transposition-readiness roadmap; due diligence covering the **full chain of activities** (recruitment corridors **and** upstream materials); **meaningful affected-stakeholder engagement** with migrant workers and their representatives; a climate-transition plan where required (energy-intensive fabs/foundries).

### 3.6 OECD Guidelines for MNEs (2023)

Government-backed recommendations operationalized by the OECD DD Guidance (the six-step cycle) and the **minerals five-step framework for 3TG** (and emerging cobalt/critical minerals). The teeth are **National Contact Points (NCPs)**, which handle **"specific instances"** — a non-judicial grievance mechanism increasingly cited in mandatory-DD enforcement and litigation. *Core tests:* implementation of the six-step and minerals five-step frameworks with **independently audited (RMI/RMAP) smelters/refiners**; prior or pending NCP specific instances and their resolution; documented severity × likelihood risk prioritisation.

### 3.7 UK — Modern Slavery Act 2015 (s.54) and 3.8 Australia — Modern Slavery Act 2018

Both are **transparency/disclosure** laws — they mandate *reporting*, not (yet) due diligence — so **test the substance behind the statement, not its existence.** UK: ≥£36m turnover; board-approved, director-signed, homepage-linked, registry-filed statement. Australia: ≥AUD $100m revenue; **seven mandatory criteria** (entity; structure/operations/supply chains; risks; actions/due diligence/remediation; **effectiveness**; consultation; other), board-approved; reform in motion (civil penalties + mandatory due-diligence duty anticipated; first Anti-Slavery Commissioner appointed 2024). *Core tests:* real due diligence and **year-over-year KPIs** (recruitment-fee remediation amounts, audit coverage, grievance volumes/outcomes) rather than boilerplate; for Australia, substantive treatment of **criterion 5 (effectiveness)** and **criterion 4 (remediation)** and readiness for the penalty/due-diligence regime.

### 3.9 How the frameworks stack — interoperability map

| Instrument | Legal nature | Primary trigger | Reach | Consequence | Sector hook |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ILO Fair Recruitment / EPP; C181; C29/P29/C105 | International standards | Cross-border recruitment | Agents & sub-agents to origin tier | Via national law + buyer codes | Migrant recruitment-fee debt |
| RBA Code v8.0 | Industry code (contractual) | Membership / customer requirement | Tier-1 + cascade; VAP | De-listing, lost contracts, fee repayment | Electronics baseline |
| US UFLPA (19 U.S.C. §1307) | Mandatory import ban | XUAR nexus / Entity List | Raw-material tier ("wholly or in part") | CBP detention; goods excluded | Polysilicon/silicon, aluminium |
| German LkSG | Mandatory due diligence | ≥1,000 employees (DE) | Direct suppliers; indirect on substantiated knowledge | BAFA fines ("serious" only); report suspended | Corridor + deeper tier |
| EU CSDDD (2024/1760, amended) | Mandatory due diligence | Large-enterprise thresholds (Omnibus) | Full "chain of activities" | Supervisory penalties; civil liability | EU-market electronics buyers |
| OECD Guidelines (2023) | Intergovernmental soft law | Adhering-country nexus | Whole chain; 3TG minerals | NCP "specific instances" | Conflict minerals (3TG) |
| UK MSA 2015 s.54 | Mandatory transparency | ≥£36m UK turnover | Disclosure of whatever is done | Reputational; registry | Statement substance |
| Australia MSA 2018 | Mandatory transparency (penalties pending) | ≥AUD $100m revenue | Disclosure across 7 criteria | Register; penalties proposed | Effectiveness + remediation |

**The unifying audit logic.** Transparency laws (UK/AU) ask *"did you report?"*; due-diligence laws (LkSG/CSDDD) and the OECD Guidelines ask *"did you actually identify, prevent, remediate, and track?"*; the import ban (UFLPA) asks *"can you prove origin?"*; and the recruitment standards (ILO/EPP) plus the RBA Code ask *"who paid for the job, and did the worker get their money and documents back?"* A supplier that can answer all four — **with evidence to the sub-agent and raw-material tiers** — is durably compliant across the stack. Build the program to that bar.

## Module 4 — Sector Deep-Dive: Semiconductor and Electronics Migrant Labor in Taiwan and South Korea

*Broker model · dormitory control · document retention · known cases.* Scope reaches **below Tier 1** — PCBs, connectors, passives, casings, camera/display modules, cabling, EMS/ODM assembly, test-and-packaging, and the **construction tier that builds new fabs** — because that is where the broker-dependent migrant workforce sits (front-end wafer-fab operators in both countries are predominantly local). Taiwan and South Korea run **structurally different regimes that fail in different places**: Taiwan a **private labor-broker model**, South Korea a **state-to-state (G2G) Employment Permit System (EPS)**.

Standards baseline: Foundations F.2/F.3, with two jurisdiction-specific notes — **South Korea ratified C29 in 2021 (in force 2022) but has not ratified C105**; **Taiwan is not an ILO member**, so the standard is imported via Taiwanese law and buyer codes. National rules: Taiwan's **Employment Service Act** (brokers); Korea's **Act on the Employment, etc. of Foreign Workers** (EPS).

### 4.1 The four structural risk vectors

**(a) Broker / recruitment-fee model.** *Taiwan — the dual-fee trap:* (1) origin-country fees commonly **US$5,000–7,000** for a low-wage manufacturing job, financed by high-interest debt taking up to two years to repay; (2) **monthly broker "service fees"** capped by the Employment Service Act at **NT$1,800 (yr 1), NT$1,700 (yr 2), NT$1,500 (yr 3+)** — the only charge brokers may legally collect, but still worker-borne, so **legal locally yet a breach of EPP/RBA "no-fees."** This gap is one of the most important Taiwan audit points; re-brokerage/"buy-out" fees on transfer compound the debt. *South Korea — G2G, residual capture:* EPS (since 2004) cuts private brokers out (HRD Korea + public agencies in 16 sending countries; EPS-TOPIK test gates the E-9 visa) and generally produces far lower fees — **but** test for informal/illegal origin-country brokerage layered on top, pre-departure "training"/loan fees, and the deeper vector of **job immobility (4.1d)**.

**(b) Dormitory control.** *Taiwan:* overcrowded/unsanitary dorms, **compulsory "savings" deductions (~12% of base wage)**, fines for "dorm-rule breaches," curfews, restricted visitors. *South Korea:* the **December 2020 death of Nuon Sokkheng (Sok Heng), a 30-year-old Cambodian EPS worker found dead in an unheated vinyl-greenhouse dormitory during a –18°C cold snap**, drove EPS-wide housing reform applicable to manufacturing — from 2021 Korea **stopped issuing employment permits for makeshift dormitories (vinyl greenhouses, shipping containers)**, made **"unsuitable accommodation" a valid ground to change workplace**, and **barred non-compliant employers from hiring foreign workers** (the Supreme Court later upheld government liability).

**(c) Document retention.** Retention of identity documents is ILO Indicator #7 and illegal in both jurisdictions, yet the most consistently observed abuse (in Transparentem's 2024 Taiwan work, all 12 interviewees who addressed it said the factory held their passports). The standard is binary: the worker must have **free, unconditional, on-demand custody** of their own passport and ARC (Taiwan) / Alien Registration Card (Korea). "Held in a safe for the worker's protection" is **not** a defence unless the worker holds the only key, can retrieve instantly without permission, and there is genuinely voluntary, retractable, signed consent.

**(d) Restricted job mobility (the "freely chosen employment" failure).** Even with zero fees and good housing, trapping a worker with one employer manufactures coercion. *Taiwan:* historic tie-to-employer with re-brokerage costs on transfer. *South Korea:* under EPS an E-9 worker may change employer **only ~3 times across a 3-year contract (plus 2 in an extension), generally only on restricted grounds** (contract expiry, employer consent, or employer fault as judged by authorities — closure, wage delay, violence, rights infringement, or post-2021 unsuitable accommodation). Requiring the worker to **prove employer fault** is itself a coercion risk. Auditors cannot fix the law but **must document whether workers can in practice leave an abusive employer**.

### 4.2 Mapping to the ILO 11 indicators

CBP's 2025 Taiwan WRO cited **5 of 11** indicators, illustrating how monitors translate observation into enforcement. In a TW/KR electronics dormitory-and-broker setting: abuse of vulnerability (indebted migrant, no local language/network); deception (contract substitution); restriction of movement (curfews, lock-downs, escorted transport); isolation (remote dorm, confiscated phones/SIMs); intimidation (deportation/blacklisting/non-renewal threats); **document retention** (passport/ARC held); **withholding of wages** ("savings"/deposit deductions); **debt bondage** (US$5,000–7,000 + monthly fees); **abusive living conditions** (vinyl-house/container dorms); **excessive overtime** (forced OT to repay debt).

### 4.3 Known cases and the monitoring evidence base

- **Giant Manufacturing Co. (Taiwan) — CBP WRO, 2025** (bicycles, not electronics, but the **leading live Taiwan trade-enforcement precedent**): CBP cited **5 of 11** indicators; workers paid recruiters up to ~US$5,500 plus broker service fees ~2 months' wages. Demonstrates that §307 risk for Taiwanese manufacturing is no longer hypothetical (Taiwan's MOL issued forced-labor guidelines in response).
- **U.D. Electronic Corp (Taiwan, electronics):** ~25–30 Vietnamese migrants reportedly paid recruiters **over US$6,000** plus monthly broker fees; passport withholding reported.
- **Askey (Taiwan; ASUS subsidiary, electronics):** ~300 migrant workers producing computers/electronics for export; high recruiter fees financed by high-interest loans.
- **Gianta / Accton Technology (Taiwan, networking electronics) — remediation template:** after Transparentem's 2024 *The Price of Work*, by **January 2025** the supplier agreed a **zero-fee policy with full recruitment-fee reimbursement**, returned passports, and gave workers access to their own savings accounts. This is the "found a problem → remediated" template auditors should require.
- **Apple supplier program (cross-supplier, includes Taiwan-linked suppliers):** **US$34.5 million in recruitment fees repaid to 37,700+ workers since 2008** under a zero-fees mandate — proving **full reimbursement at scale is operationally possible** and removing the "it's not feasible" objection.
- **South Korea — Nuon Sokkheng (Dec 2020):** the greenhouse-dormitory death that triggered EPS-wide housing reform; the reference point for dormitory-control auditing.

*Triangulate against* **Electronics Watch**, **Transparentem**, the **Business & Human Rights Resource Centre**, the **Peter Bengtsen** investigative series (2022–2025), **Apple Supplier Responsibility** reporting, **ILO NORMLEX**, Korea MOEL's 2021 housing reforms, and Taiwan MOL/Employment Service Act. An audit that contradicts these without explanation is itself a red flag.

### 4.4 Taiwan vs. South Korea — at a glance

| Dimension | Taiwan | South Korea (EPS) |
|---|---|---|
| Recruitment channel | Private brokers | G2G state system (16 MOUs) |
| Typical worker-borne fees | High: US$5,000–7,000 + capped monthly fee | Low by design; test for informal brokerage |
| Legal worker-charged fee | Monthly "service fee" (NT$1,800/1,700/1,500) — legal locally, **breaches EPP** | None should reach the worker |
| Core mobility problem | Tie-to-employer + re-brokerage cost | EPS transfer limits (~3×/3 yrs, restricted grounds) |
| Dormitory reform driver | Investigations (Transparentem/Electronics Watch) | Nuon Sokkheng death → 2021 housing ban |
| ILO C29 / C105 status | Non-member; standard imported via codes/law | C29 ratified 2021; **C105 not ratified** |
| Live trade enforcement | CBP WRO precedent (Giant, 2025) | Test US/EU import exposure of components |

Sector-specific improvements distil to: reimburse **all** worker-paid fees **including Taiwan monthly broker fees and historical fees to former workers**; verify Korea's **official EPS G2G channel** was used with no informal brokerage; confirm **no vinyl-house/container housing** in Korea; **enable genuine job mobility**; extend due diligence to fab-construction and dormitory/catering contractors; and **pre-empt trade exposure** with a §307-defensible recruitment-and-remediation file (worker-fee ledger, reimbursement evidence, document-custody proof) for US- and EU-bound product. (Full items in Appendix A.)

## Module 5 — Worker Voice, Grievance Mechanisms, and Remediation

### 5.1 Why this module is decisive

Worker voice is the **single most reliable detection signal for forced labour** and the **precondition for credible remediation**. Conventional snapshot audits have a documented record of *failing to detect* forced labour — facilities passed routine audits shortly before CBP WROs (including Malaysian electronics-manufacturing-services suppliers cited for recruitment-fee debt bondage and passport retention), and before major catastrophes. Operating principle: **privilege worker-controlled evidence over management documents; triangulate; over-sample the most exposed workers (migrants, agency/dispatch, dormitory residents, probationers, women); and treat the absence of grievances as a red flag, not a pass.** Normative basis: Foundations F.2 (UNGP 22/25/29/31; OECD/NCPs; ILO C87/C98/C29-P29-C105/C190/C181; ICRMW Art. 18/21; Palermo Art. 6; RBA A1/A7 + grievance; Electronics Watch; KnowTheChain/CHRB).

### 5.2 Pillar A — Operational-level grievance mechanism (OLGM)

Audit the OLGM against the **eight UNGP Principle 31 effectiveness criteria** — legitimate, accessible, predictable, equitable, transparent, rights-compatible, a source of continuous learning, and based on engagement and dialogue. A suggestion box is not a grievance mechanism. **Migrant-specific accessibility is mandatory:** native-language channels; an anonymous option; at least one channel **independent of both factory management and the recruiting agent**; coverage of **dormitory/housing** grievances; and reach **into the recruitment chain** (origin-country sub-agents). *Test the channel live* during the audit, and *pull the full 24-month grievance log* disaggregated by sex, nationality, migrant status, and direct-vs-agency status — wage/fee/document/hours/harassment/FoA grievances must actually appear, not only canteen issues.

### 5.3 Pillar B — Worker-driven monitoring and independent worker voice

Distinguish **corporate-controlled social auditing** (the firm commissions and controls it) from **worker-driven social responsibility (WSR) / independent worker voice** (workers and their chosen organisations generate the signal and there are real consequences). Triangulate firm-controlled audit data against at least one **independent, worker-controlled** source. Benchmark and recommend: **Electronics Watch**; **Issara Institute** (worker-voice lines, Inclusive Labour Monitoring across SE-Asian corridors incl. Myanmar→Thailand); enforceable brand agreements/WSR as the gold standard — **Fair Food Program** (CIW), **Bangladesh/International Accord**, **Dindigul Agreement** (note: **no comparable binding accord yet exists in electronics — that gap is itself a finding** and a frontier to recommend); **Global Framework Agreements** with **IndustriALL**. Worker interviews must be **off-site or in genuine privacy, in-language, independently interpreted, and auditor-selected** — never management-mediated; watch for coaching (identical scripted answers, dual record-keeping, pre-audit warnings).

### 5.4 Pillar C — Freedom of association and collective bargaining

Much electronics manufacturing sits in jurisdictions that legally restrict FoA. Legal restriction **does not relieve** the enterprise of responsibility: where workers cannot freely organise, the firm must **not interfere**, must **facilitate genuine parallel democratic representation** (freely elected committees, **including migrants and agency workers**), and must not treat the restriction as a reason to do nothing (ILO C87/C98; RBA A7; OECD Ch. V). *Distinctive checks:* is the only body management-dominated ("yellow" union)? are representatives freely elected (not appointed/removable by management)? are dues deducted while no bargaining occurs? what happened to representatives/complainants over 24 months (discipline, non-renewal, reassignment, repatriation)?

### 5.5 Pillar D — Remediation and access to remedy

Detection without remedy is failure. Remedy must be **victim-centred, rights-compatible, proportionate to harm, designed with worker input, tracked to verified closure, and never conditioned on a waiver, NDA, or continued employment.** Responsibility scales with involvement: **cause/contribute → provide for or cooperate in remedy** (UNGP 22); **directly linked → use leverage and cooperate.** **Cut-and-run on discovering forced labour, leaving workers without remedy, is itself an adverse impact.** Remedy hierarchy and types per Foundations F.4. *Distinctive checks:* for every substantiated finding, what remedy reached the **worker** (not just a corrective-action note to the supplier)? fee repayment **independently verified to have reached workers' accounts with no re-deduction**; documents returned with free unconditional access; a victim-centred forced-labour/trafficking protocol (P29 Art. 4; Palermo Art. 6); remedy reaching **into the recruitment chain/origin country**; a **responsible-exit plan** on disengagement; root-cause correction (purchasing practices, fee-charging agents, OT-driving lead times).

### 5.6 Cross-cutting — non-retaliation (the precondition for all four pillars)

Every pillar collapses if workers fear reprisal. For migrants, retaliation is uniquely severe — **contract termination, blacklisting across a corridor, dormitory eviction, or repatriation.** **Audit:** cross-reference the lists of complainants and worker representatives against disciplinary, non-renewal, reassignment, and repatriation records — any correlation is a critical finding. **Require:** an explicit non-retaliation policy with remedy where retaliation occurs; a migrant-specific guarantee that no grievance leads to termination/repatriation; whistleblower protection; and protection of worker representatives under C87/C98.

### 5.7 Maturity rubric

| Level | OLGM (A) | Worker-driven monitoring (B) | FoA/CB (C) | Remediation (D) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 – Absent/Deceptive** | Suggestion box; zero/trivial logs | Announced audits only; coaching evident | Yellow union or none | Supplier letters only; no worker remedy |
| **1 – Paper** | Policy exists, not used/known | On-site, mgmt-interpreted interviews | Mgmt-appointed committee | Ad hoc, conditioned remedies |
| **2 – Functional** | Multi-channel, migrant-language, used | Some independent triangulation | Freely-elected committee | Fee repayment + documented remedy |
| **3 – Effective** | Meets all 8 UNGP P31 criteria; verified no retaliation | Worker-driven partner (Electronics Watch/Issara); off-site auditor-selected interviews | Genuine CB or facilitated parallel representation incl. migrants | Victim-centred, verified-to-closure, root-cause corrected |
| **4 – Worker-empowered** | Worker-co-governed; reaches recruitment chain | Enforceable consequence-bearing commitment (WSR/GFA) | Recognised independent representation + CBA | Remedy delivered across the corridor; responsible-exit guaranteed |

### 5.8 Zero-tolerance findings (auto-fail regardless of other scores)

(1) Retaliation against a complainant/representative; (2) retained identity documents without free unconditional access; (3) worker-paid recruitment fees with no verified repayment; (4) remedy conditioned on a waiver, NDA, or continued employment; (5) forced-labour indicators present with no victim-centred remedy protocol; (6) coaching, dual record-keeping, or pre-selection of interviewees corrupting worker voice.

> **Auditor's standing caution:** the purpose is not to confirm that paperwork exists but to determine **whether the most vulnerable worker on the line — a recently-arrived migrant on an agency contract living in a company dormitory — can safely tell someone the truth and have it fixed.** Score against that worker, not against the binder.

## Module 6 — Audit Methodology and Its Limits

*Method, not the checklist, determines whether findings are trustworthy.*

### 6.1 Why methodology determines validity

The highest-severity migrant abuses are precisely the ones a single, announced, on-site, management-mediated walkthrough is structurally unable to see: **debt bondage incurred off-site months before arrival; passport/permit retention concealed for one audit day; contract substitution; and dual book-keeping/forced overtime/below-minimum net wages recorded off-book.** The record is unambiguous: **Ali Enterprises** (Karachi) held a valid **SA8000** certificate weeks before the September 2012 fire that killed ~250 workers; multiple **Rana Plaza** factories had been audited before the 2013 collapse (1,134 dead); **MSI Integrity, *Not Fit-for-Purpose* (2020)** and **Clean Clothes Campaign/SOMO, *Fig Leaf for Fashion* (2019)** document systemic audit failure; and **Verité's 2014 study** found forced-labour conditions affecting roughly one in three foreign electronics workers in Malaysia — in facilities being audited. **Treat the conventional social audit as one input of low-to-moderate weight, never as assurance.**

### 6.2 Announced vs. semi-announced vs. unannounced

| Mode | Meaning | Evidentiary value |
|---|---|---|
| **Announced** | Fixed agreed date | **Lowest** — enables a clean-up window. Capability-building/first-time gap analysis only, never forced-labor assurance. |
| **Semi-announced** | A window (e.g., 2–4 weeks) within which the auditor arrives unannounced | **Moderate** — preserves interpreter/logistics booking while denying a precise clean-up date. The pragmatic default for most Tier-1 sites (aligns with RBA VAP windowing). |
| **Unannounced** | No advance notice | **Highest** — high-risk corridors, post-allegation follow-up, remediation verification. |

An announced audit lets a non-compliant site coach workers, stage/back-date documents, give the highest-risk workers the day off or transfer them, switch off the second payroll book, return passports for the morning, clean the dorm, and pause illegal overtime. **Method requirements:** default to semi-announced; use unannounced for high-risk/post-allegation/verification; **multiple off-cycle visits per year** timed into consumer-electronics ramps (holiday builds, new-product launches), onto payroll dates and recruitment-intake waves, including **night-shift and weekend** visits; and **walk the dormitories, canteen, and on-site clinic — not only the production floor.** Reconcile audit-day head-count against the prior month's payroll, the production schedule, dormitory occupancy, biometric/access logs, and canteen meal counts.

### 6.3 Off-site worker interviews — the heart of a credible method

On-site interviewing is the single most-compromised step. Requirements: **off-site, worker-chosen location**; **auditor-controlled, risk-weighted sampling** (auditor — never management — draws the sample, over-sampling migrant, agency/dispatch/temporary, dormitory, and recent-hire cohorts, stratified by nationality, corridor, gender, and labour agent); **in-language, independent interpretation** (no employer/agent tie; gender-matched where appropriate — electronics assembly is heavily female and some abuses are gendered); **informed consent, anti-retaliation assurance, non-attributable reporting**, with a post-audit **retaliation-watch**; **continuous worker-voice channels** to catch problems that emerge mid-contract; and **test the grievance mechanism with a controlled case** (language access, true anonymity, timeliness, escalation, and whether migrant/agency workers actually use it). Interview intent (asked privately, in-language) covers: fees paid to anyone in either country and debt still owed; **where the passport is right now and whether the worker can retrieve it today, alone, without permission**; whether pay/hours/role match the pre-departure promise and whether a different contract was signed on arrival; ability to leave the factory/dorm and to resign/repatriate without penalty; deductions and whether they were agreed; threats to the worker/family/immigration status and whether overtime is refusable; and who they would tell and whether anything would safely happen.

### 6.4 Document, wage, and recruitment-fee forensics (follow the money home)

- **Recruitment-fee tracing to the origin-country sub-agent:** reconstruct each worker's **total cost-to-migrate** (agent/sub-agent fees, medical, training, visa/permit, travel, deposits, in-kind) against EPP/the ILO definition — the worker pays **zero**. The destination payroll won't show the fee; the worker's home-country debt will.
- **Contract-substitution check:** lay origin offer, destination contract, and actual paid terms side by side.
- **Records triangulation:** reconcile **payroll ↔ time/attendance ↔ production output ↔ canteen/dormitory logs ↔ biometric access ↔ energy/utility records.** Divergence is the signature of dual book-keeping and unrecorded overtime.
- **Document control:** passports/permits in the worker's **sole, unrestricted possession** (worker holds the only key, retrieves at will).
- **Bonded-wage/deductions analysis:** test for deposits, withheld first-month wages, wages routed **through the agent**, and net pay **below minimum after deductions**.
- **Sub-tier and material scope:** bring the labour agent, dormitory operator, on-site contractors, and dispatch agency inside the fence; fold in **polysilicon (UFLPA)** and **3TG + cobalt** via responsible-minerals due diligence.

### 6.5 Beyond the social audit — from snapshot compliance to structural change

Auditing is **necessary but not sufficient**; it detects symptoms at a point in time and cannot change the structures that generate abuse — the **recruitment-broker business model** (fees → debt → coercion), **buyer purchasing practices** (price/lead-time pressure forcing overtime and the cheapest brokered labour), and **power asymmetry**. Four levers move past audit: **(a) Worker-driven Social Responsibility and binding, enforceable agreements** (Fair Food Program; Bangladesh/International Accord — buyer-binding obligations, worker-to-worker education, independent monitoring, binding arbitration, and real market consequences); **(b) continuous HRDD** (UNGP/OECD six-step, prioritised by severity, increasingly a legal mandate); **(c) responsible recruitment + EPP remediation** (map and shorten the chain, shift to employer-paid/zero-fee, and *repay* fees already charged — the operational test of credibility is **verified worker reimbursement, not a policy PDF**, as Top Glove and the ATA IMS/Dyson WRO cases show); and **(d) purchasing-practice reform — audit the buyer, not only the supplier** (align prices/lead times with legal wages and no forced overtime; favour remediation and capacity-building over cut-and-run; treat responsible exit as a last resort with worker-impact assessed first).

### 6.6 Standing limits — what an audit cannot do (state plainly in every report)

- **Snapshot vs. continuous reality** — a one-day picture of a months-long, cross-border abuse.
- **Conflict of interest** — buyer-/supplier-paid auditors, "fee-for-pass" incentives, audit duplication/fatigue.
- **Fraud and coaching outrun the protocol** — staged documents and scripted workers defeat a checklist.
- **Sub-tier invisibility** — abuse often lives at the labour agent, dormitory operator, sub-contractor, or the raw-material tier (polysilicon; 3TG/cobalt), beyond the Tier-1 fence.
- **State-imposed forced labour** — where the environment precludes free worker testimony (the XUAR/Xinjiang context), **credible auditing is not possible.** Do not rely on audits there; apply **supplier mapping, material tracing, and the UFLPA rebuttable-presumption posture** instead.
- **Detection ≠ remedy** — an audit that finds an indicator but produces no verified worker remedy is **not compliance.**

> **Bottom line:** use audits to *find* problems with a credible method (semi-announced/unannounced cadence, off-site in-language worker interviews, corridor fee-tracing), and use **binding agreements, continuous HRDD, employer-paid recruitment, and purchasing-practice reform** to *fix* them. The audit is the smoke detector; structural change is the fire code.

## Appendix A — Consolidated Audit Checklist

A single, de-duplicated field instrument distilled from all six modules. Each item is a check; bracketed tags give the controlling instrument(s). Score each on the Foundations F.4 scale (Critical / Major / Minor). **Method preconditions apply** (Module 6): auditor-selected, off-site, in-language, independently-interpreted interviews; triangulation of every claim; over-sampling of migrant/agency/dormitory/female workers and the night shift; semi-announced or unannounced cadence.

### A.0 Audit-method integrity (verify before trusting any finding)
- A.0.1 Cadence is semi-announced or unannounced with documented justification for any announced visit; multiple off-cycle visits/year include night/weekend, payroll dates, and recruitment-intake waves. [RBA VAP; Module 6]
- A.0.2 Audit-day head-count reconciles with prior-month payroll, production schedule, dormitory occupancy, biometric/access logs, and canteen meal counts. [Module 6]
- A.0.3 The auditor (not management) drew the interview sample; interviews were off-site/in-language/independently-interpreted; no coaching, dual books, or pre-selection. [UNGP 31; Module 5/6]
- A.0.4 Findings are triangulated across payroll ↔ time/attendance ↔ output ↔ canteen/dorm ↔ biometric ↔ energy/utility ↔ worker voice ↔ recruitment/fee records ↔ document-custody; divergences investigated, not waived. [Module 6]
- A.0.5 Post-audit retaliation-watch is in place for interviewed workers. [UNGP 31; Module 5]

### A.1 Governance, policy and management system
- A.1.1 Written, board-endorsed **Employer Pays / zero-fee policy** mirroring the ILO 2019 definition (fees + related costs + interest, whole chain), covering **all tiers** (destination, origin, sub-agents), translated into worker and agent languages. [ILO C181 Art. 7; EPP; RBA A1]
- A.1.2 Named owner of ethical-recruitment risk; responsible recruitment is a board/management-review KPI with documented internal audits. [UNGP; OECD]
- A.1.3 Site's **TCR definition** matches the ILO definition, not narrowed to the destination invoice or a legal cap. [ILO 2019]
- A.1.4 EPP, document-non-retention, and grievance/non-retaliation obligations are **contractually cascaded** to labor providers and lower tiers, with breach consequences and verification at the recruitment tier. [LkSG; CSDDD; RBA]

### A.2 Recruitment-chain mapping and intermediary due diligence
- A.2.1 The site can map **every** intermediary used in the last 24 months by corridor and tier — destination agency, origin agency(ies), every sub-agent — and it reconciles with worker interviews. (An incomplete map is a finding.) [OECD; Module 1/2]
- A.2.2 Each worker is traceable through every horizontal hop to the **first point of recruitment**, with fees reconciled. [Module 2]
- A.2.3 Destination **and** origin agency licences are current/valid with names matching who workers dealt with; agencies are IRIS-registered or equivalently certified; the supplier did its own due diligence beyond the licence. [IRIS; ILO Fair Recruitment]
- A.2.4 Agency contracts contain binding **no-fee, right-to-audit, sub-agent-disclosure, and breach/termination** clauses; undisclosed sub-agents are prohibited. [RBA; ILO C181]
- A.2.5 Recruitment-agency beneficial ownership is screened for common directors/family/owners with the supplier or its managers (conflict of interest). [Module 2]

### A.3 Recruitment fees and related costs (EPP core)
- A.3.1 Did workers pay **anything** — to an agent, sub-agent, "runner," trainer, medical clinic, or the employer, in the **origin or destination** country — to obtain or keep the job? [ILO C181 Art. 7; EPP; RBA A1]
- A.3.2 Who paid each **related cost** (medical, insurance, visa/permit, passport, document processing, pre-departure training, skills tests, travel both ways)? Were any deducted from or recharged to workers? [ILO 2019]
- A.3.3 Fee chain traced with **origin-country receipts**, not destination-only "no-fee" attestations; totals compared to legal maxima and corridor norms; report median **and 90th-percentile** cost-to-migrate (bondage hides in the tail). [Module 1/3]
- A.3.4 Fees are not reclassified as "service," "processing," "training," "loans," "deposits," "insurance," or "administrative costs." [ILO C181]

### A.4 Debt, deductions, deposits and wages
- A.4.1 Were workers required to take **loans** (agent/employer/third party) to fund migration, at what interest, and can they state original amount, balance, and deduction schedule? Do total corridor fees exceed the wages for the repayment period (debt-bondage severity)? [Supp. Convention 1956 Art. 1(a); ILO indicators]
- A.4.2 No **deposits, bonds, or "guarantee" sums** (cash, withheld pay, pledged property) held against the worker; no compulsory "savings" scheme (e.g., the Taiwan ~12%-of-wage pattern) that binds the worker. [ILO indicators; Module 4]
- A.4.3 Payslips show **no** deductions tied to recruitment/training/transport/lodging/agent-repayment; all deductions itemized, lawful, consented, capped, leaving net pay at or above the legal minimum. [RBA A4]
- A.4.4 Wages paid **in full, on time, into an account the worker solely controls** (never via the agent), with itemized payslips in a language the worker reads; no disciplinary or "productivity" deductions; no payment in-kind that prevents the worker from leaving. [RBA A4; ILO withholding indicator]
- A.4.5 Working hours reconcile with output and access logs (no clustering exactly at the 60-hour cap); overtime is genuinely voluntary, not a debt-repayment necessity; ≥1 day off in 7. [RBA A3]

### A.5 Identity documents and freedom of movement
- A.5.1 Workers retain **continuous, unrestricted, on-demand possession** of passports/permits/ARC/ARC cards; have the worker demonstrate immediate, unescorted retrieval. [ICRMW Art. 21; RBA A1; Dhaka P4]
- A.5.2 Any storage is genuinely voluntary, free, individually lockable, 24/7-accessible, with no release fee/approval step; "safekeeping" consent is individually signed, dated, translated, and retractable — never a pre-printed form. [ICRMW Art. 21]
- A.5.3 A document-custody log exists and matches worker testimony. [Module 1]
- A.5.4 No restrictions on movement, debt-linked employer housing, curfews, guarded egress, confiscated phones/SIMs, or escorted-only transport; workers can resign and repatriate without penalty, forfeited wages, or document/deposit loss (with employer-borne return travel). [ILO restriction-of-movement indicator; Module 4]

### A.6 Contracts and informed consent (anti-substitution)
- A.6.1 Workers received a written contract **in their own language before departure**, with time to review and a signed copy to keep (no blank/thumbprint-only contracts). [RBA A1; ILO Fair Recruitment]
- A.6.2 Origin offer = destination contract = actual paid terms on wage, role, hours, and deductions (test for **contract substitution**). [ILO deception indicator; RBA A1]
- A.6.3 Recruitment terms, the no-fee policy, and grievance routes were disclosed pre-departure and reconfirmed on arrival. [Dhaka; ILO Fair Recruitment]

### A.7 Accommodation / dormitories
- A.7.1 Valid accommodation permit/certificate (e.g., Malaysia Act 446 as amended 2019; **no vinyl-greenhouse/shipping-container housing in Korea**) and minimum space/sanitation/ventilation/fire-safety/potable-water standards; safe segregated facilities for women. [Dhaka P8; IFC/EBRD 2009; Korea 2021 reform]
- A.7.2 Workers freely enter/leave at any hour — no curfew penalty, biometric lock-in, or guards controlling movement. [ILO indicator]
- A.7.3 Housing/utility deductions lawful, disclosed, capped, proportionate to actual quality, and incapable of pushing net pay below minimum or into recoverable debt; housing not used as employment leverage; no eviction/deportation threats to deter complaints. [RBA; Dhaka P8]
- A.7.4 A grievance channel is physically reachable from the dormitory. [UNGP 31]

### A.8 Dispatch / agency / sub-contractor and on-site service labor
- A.8.1 The share of the on-site workforce that is dispatch/outsourced/contractor (per area and shift) is known, and those workers have the **same** Code, fee, document, wage, hours, and grievance protections as direct hires. [RBA; Module 2]
- A.8.2 For each labor/service contractor: who recruited the workers, through which corridor/sub-agents, and **did any pay fees**? (The fee ban follows the worker, not the contract.) Wages paid directly to each worker's own account. [ILO C181; Module 2]
- A.8.3 No undisclosed onward sub-contracting of production (shadow sites/home-based work); headcount/output reconcile to declared capacity. [Module 2]
- A.8.4 For fab build-outs, construction/tool-install crews and dormitory/catering/security/cleaning contractors are mapped, fee-screened, and housed to standard. [Module 2/4]

### A.9 Worker voice, grievance and freedom of association
- A.9.1 Multiple non-competing channels, ≥1 **independent of both management and the labour agent**, in **every** on-site migrant language, free, 24/7, mobile-accessible, with a genuine anonymous option; live-tested during the audit; meets the eight UNGP P31 criteria. [UNGP 31; RBA]
- A.9.2 The full 24-month grievance log (disaggregated by sex/nationality/migrant/agency status) shows wage, fee, document, hours, harassment, and FoA grievances actually surfacing and resolving — **not** zero/trivial logs. [UNGP 31; C190]
- A.9.3 Grievances about origin-country recruitment can be raised and are routed to someone able to act. [Module 5]
- A.9.4 At least one **independent worker-driven monitoring** source exists (Electronics Watch partner, Issara, union, NGO) and the firm has acted on it; workers were consulted in design/review. [Module 5]
- A.9.5 Independent/freely-elected worker representation (not a "yellow" union); where law restricts FoA, genuine parallel democratic representation is facilitated, including migrants and agency workers; no dues-without-bargaining. [ILO C87/C98; RBA A7]

### A.10 Remediation and access to remedy
- A.10.1 Where fees were found, a repayment program reimburses the **full TCR including interest**, timely (target <90 days / within the current contract cycle), to current **and reachable former** workers, **independently verified to have reached worker-controlled accounts with no re-deduction**, with credible corridor proxy where receipts are missing. [RBA A1; EPP; P29 Art. 4]
- A.10.2 Retained documents returned with free unconditional access; coercive debts cancelled; freedom of movement restored. [ICRMW Art. 21]
- A.10.3 No remedy is conditioned on a waiver, NDA, or continued employment. [UNGP 22/25]
- A.10.4 A victim-centred forced-labour/trafficking protocol provides remedy/compensation and support referral without penalising the victim. [P29 Art. 4; Palermo Art. 6]
- A.10.5 The underlying channel is structurally fixed (agent terminated/remediated; direct-hire or employer-pays sourcing adopted); root causes (purchasing practices, OT-driving lead times) corrected; a **responsible-exit plan** preserves remedy on disengagement. [OECD; Module 2/5/6]
- A.10.6 Remedies tracked to **independently verified closure** with worker confirmation; the same finding does not recur across cohorts. [Module 5]

### A.11 Upstream raw-material tier and legal-regulatory readiness
- A.11.1 A **BOM-level map** traces high-risk inputs (polysilicon/silicon, aluminium, magnesium, 3TG, cobalt) to the producing facility and country of origin with transaction/transport documents. [UFLPA; OECD minerals]
- A.11.2 No tier appears on the **UFLPA Entity List**, sources from XUAR, or participates in state labor-transfer/pairing programs; no transshipment obscuring origin; a **detention-response dossier** meets CBP's clear-and-convincing-evidence bar. [UFLPA; §307]
- A.11.3 3TG (and emerging critical minerals) use the OECD **minerals five-step** with independently audited (RMI/RMAP) smelters/refiners — not a "conflict-free" checkbox. [OECD]
- A.11.4 For in-scope buyers: LkSG risk-management system, named human-rights officer, current risk analysis, and a **"substantiated knowledge" trigger protocol**; a single OECD-aligned system built forward to CSDDD (2028/2029); UK/AU modern-slavery statements that are substantive (KPIs, effectiveness, remediation), board-approved, and registry-filed; EU Forced Labour Regulation readiness for 2027. [LkSG; CSDDD; UK/AU MSA; EU FLR]

### A.12 Consolidated red flags (de-duplicated)
- Any worker-paid fee or related cost — including "within the legal cap" or relabelled (service/processing/training/loan/deposit/insurance); recurring monthly broker/service-fee deductions (Taiwan), even where locally legal.
- Recruitment debt/high-interest loans financing migration with balances "worked off" via deductions; total fees exceeding several months' corridor wages; inconsistent totals among same-corridor workers (undisclosed sub-agent fees).
- "Remediation" repaying only the legal cap/destination portion, excluding interest or former workers, staggered indefinitely, paid in cash or via the agent, or with no measured baseline; remediation = a warning letter with no worker-level remedy.
- Passport/permit/ID retention; "voluntary" safekeeping with a release fee, approval step, no 24/7 access, or pre-printed/undated/untranslated/non-retractable consent.
- Restricted movement: locked dorms/gates, curfews, guards, biometric lock-in, confiscated phones/SIMs, escorted-only transport; inability to resign/repatriate without penalty.
- Contract substitution; no pre-departure native-language contract; documents (passport/age/identity/qualification) handled or altered by a broker.
- Dual/parallel time records; hours clustered at 60; payroll/output/access/energy records that fail to reconcile; net pay below minimum after deductions; wages paid via the agent or to an account the worker doesn't control.
- Makeshift dorms (vinyl-greenhouse/container — a Korea permit bar), overcrowding, blocked/locked fire exits; housing deductions disproportionate to quality or used as leverage; eviction/deportation threats to deter complaints.
- A large dispatch/outsourced workforce keeping migrant/high-risk labour "off the audited books"; service/construction crews absent from the labor map; undisclosed onward sub-contracting.
- Chain that stops at the destination broker ("cannot identify" origin agency/sub-agents); agency owned by/related to the supplier or its managers; agency contracts lacking no-fee/right-to-audit/sub-agent-disclosure clauses.
- Grievance channel that is suggestion-box-only, majority-language-only, employer-/agent-controlled, or shows zero/trivial complaints or use only by local permanent staff; complainant confidentiality breached; harassment cases closed with NDAs.
- Reliance solely on announced, company-paid social audits; on-site/management-interpreted/pre-selected interviews; coached or identical answers; a clean audit history later hit by a WRO/UFLPA detention/credible exposé.
- "Yellow" union or no facilitated representation; migrants/agency workers barred from representation; dues deducted with no bargaining; representatives/complainants disciplined, non-renewed, reassigned, or repatriated; surveillance/captive-audience meetings.
- Country-of-origin documentation stopping at assembly (cannot reach smelter/refiner/polysilicon producer); UFLPA Entity List match/XUAR sourcing/state labor-transfer links; 3TG reduced to a checkbox.
- Treating the LkSG report suspension or the CSDDD Omnibus delay as a reason to scale back substantive due diligence; boilerplate UK/AU statements (no metrics, identical year-over-year, missing approval/signature/filing); Australian statements silent on effectiveness/remediation.
- Brand "cut-and-run" on finding forced labour, leaving workers uncompensated; remedy conditioned on a waiver/NDA/continued employment; forced-labour indicators treated as a contractual/quality issue rather than triggering a victim-centred protocol; **two or more co-occurring ILO indicators** (the WRO/UFLPA/EU-FLR fact pattern).

### A.13 Zero-tolerance auto-fail set (any one blocks conformance; immediate remediation + re-audit)
1. **Worker-paid recruitment fee/related cost not fully repaid** (TCR + interest, current and former workers) — EPP / ILO C181 Art. 7 / RBA A1.
2. **Identity-document retention** without free, unconditional, 24/7 worker access — including coerced "voluntary safekeeping" — ICRMW Art. 21 / RBA A1.
3. **Retaliation** (incl. termination, blacklisting, repatriation) against a complainant or worker representative.
4. **Remedy conditioned** on a waiver, NDA, or continued employment.
5. **Forced-labour indicators present (especially ≥2 co-occurring) with no victim-centred remedy protocol** — P29 Art. 4 / Palermo Art. 6.
6. **Coaching, dual record-keeping, or management pre-selection** of interviewees corrupting worker voice.
7. **Restriction on freedom to leave/resign/repatriate** without penalty; locked dorms, curfews, or guarded egress.
8. **Contract substitution** / no pre-departure native-language contract matching actual terms.
9. **State-imposed-forced-labour exposure** (XUAR/UFLPA) without traceability and rebuttable-presumption posture.
10. **Makeshift dormitories** (vinyl-greenhouse/shipping-container) — a Korea permit bar.

## Appendix B — Corporate-Hiring Improvement Recommendations

The audit finds the problem; the recommendations below fix the structure. They are the buyer/employer playbook, in three parts: the **direct employer's recruitment-to-dormitory program**, the **brand/buyer's commercial leverage and contract architecture**, and **how to verify a real fix rather than paper compliance**.

### B.1 The direct employer's program — recruitment, fees, contracts, onboarding, dormitories

**Recruitment.** Map and disclose the full chain to the **sub-agent level**: require every first-tier labour supplier to declare each sub-agent, broker, and village-level recruiter in source countries (Nepal, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Indonesia, Vietnam, the Philippines) — most fee-charging and deception happens at unlicensed sub-agents the destination factory never sees. Make sub-agent disclosure a contractual condition with **joint-and-several liability**. Restrict sourcing to recruiters certified under **IOM IRIS** or pre-qualified against the **ILO Fair Recruitment Guidelines (2016)**, and audit agents on-site **in the source country**. **Shorten the chain** — pilot **employer-led/direct recruitment** in highest-volume corridors and align to (and exceed) any government-to-government MOU. Cross-reference candidates and agencies against the **ILO 11 indicators** at pre-qualification (consistent with **ILO C181 Art. 7** and the **Palermo Protocol**).

**Fees.** Adopt the **Employer Pays Principle** explicitly: the employer bears **all recruitment fees AND related costs** per the **ILO 2019 definition** (agent commissions, visa, medical exams, insurance, levies, document costs, travel, lodging) — broader than C181 Art. 7's "fees," and the "related costs" gap is where abuse migrates. **Remediate retroactively, not just prospectively**: commission a fee-paid study (worker interviews + receipt review) across current and recent cohorts and **repay fees already paid, in full and traceably**, to workers' own bank accounts with reconciliation (mirroring the RBA "Freely Chosen Employment" repayment requirement). **Pay agencies directly** for legitimate services; never route reimbursement through wage deductions, "training fees," forced savings, or recruitment-linked loans.

**Contracts.** Prohibit **contract substitution**: the contract signed in the source country, in the worker's own language, must be **identical** to the one honored on arrival (wage, role, hours, lawful deductions, duration). Provide the written contract **before departure**, with time to review and a signed copy to keep (no blank or thumbprint-only contracts). Specify wage at or above the legal minimum, **voluntary** overtime at premium pay, itemized lawful deductions, and **no-penalty resignation** with employer-funded return travel.

**Onboarding.** **Zero passport/document retention** — provide free, individual, lockable storage the worker alone controls, retrievable at any time; if local law mandates safekeeping, require written voluntary opt-in only, never confiscation. Run a **multilingual induction** (wages, deductions, grievance routes, local rights, how to lawfully change or leave employment), consistent with **ICRMW** and **ILO C97/C143**. Stand up an **effective grievance mechanism** meeting the **UNGP Principle 31** criteria — a third-party multilingual hotline and/or worker committees, with anonymous reporting and case tracking.

**Dormitories.** Apply **IFC/EBRD "Workers' Accommodation: Processes and Standards" (2009)** (minimum floor space, capped bunk stacking, sex-separated facilities, sanitation ratios, ventilation, fire egress). **No lock-ins, curfews, or guarded gates** restricting movement. **De-link housing from control**: rent free or transparent/fair/lawful/itemized; continued accommodation never conditioned on compliance, productivity, or surrendering rights; safe water, food access, healthcare access, and an accommodation grievance channel.

**Verification wrapper (so the above is real, not paper).** Embed every clause in supplier contracts mapped to the **RBA Code**, and conduct ongoing **HRDD** per the **UNGPs** and **OECD Due Diligence Guidance**. Verify through the **RBA VAP** with **off-site, confidential worker interviews**, unannounced visits, and continuous worker-voice surveys — document review alone misses substitution, retained passports, and unrepaid fees. Publish hard KPIs: percentage of zero-fee corridors, dollars remediated, and passport-retention incidents (target: zero), grounded in **ILO C29/C105**.

### B.2 The brand/buyer playbook — commercial leverage and contract architecture

**Anchor everything to the Employer Pays Principle.** The causal chain is direct: recruitment fees → worker debt → debt bondage → inability to leave → forced labour/trafficking (Palermo; ILO C29/P29; C181 Art. 7 bars charging workers "directly or indirectly, in whole or in part, any fees or costs"). Cut the fee and you remove the debt lever that makes coercion possible.

**1. Contract architecture (the binding mechanisms).** Define "no fee" by the **ILO standard** — incorporate the 2016 Fair Recruitment Guidelines **and the 2019 Definition** by reference (closing the loophole where the agency commission is reimbursed but workers still pay for medical/visa/travel/document/"training"). Use **cascade/flow-down clauses** so each supplier imposes identical EPP and no-document-retention obligations on its own labour providers and sub-agents and is **contractually liable** for their breaches. Make **remediation (worker fee repayment) the primary contractual remedy**, with termination the last resort after remediation fails (termination alone is "cut and run" — it punishes workers). Move from one-sided "representations and warranties" to **shared-responsibility/responsible-contracting** terms (e.g., **ABA Model Contract Clauses 2.0**, the **Responsible Contracting Project**) where the *buyer* commits to responsible purchasing. Secure **right-to-audit + worker-access** clauses (unannounced audits; inspection of recruitment records/receipts/payslips to the labour agent; off-site interviews; contractual bans on passport confiscation and wage withholding). Tie verified compliance to **purchase-order conditions and indemnity**.

**2. Commercial leverage (the incentive mechanisms).** **Cost the true price of ethical recruitment and ring-fence it** as a separate, protected line item — do not mandate EPP while demanding the lowest price. **Fix your own purchasing practices** (unrealistic lead times, late design changes, inaccurate forecasts, long payment terms force unmanaged broker use and excessive overtime) and measure them via the **Better Buying Institute** index and **ACT (Action, Collaboration, Transformation)**. Use **positive leverage** (longer contracts, larger/guaranteed volume, preferred-supplier status for verified performance), and build **collaborative leverage** where a single brand lacks it (aggregate pressure through the **RBA/RLI** and the **Leadership Group for Responsible Recruitment** on common suppliers and corridors).

**3. Recruitment-channel control.** **Disintermediate** — fewer agents, favour direct hiring or a single accountable vetted recruiter per corridor (each sub-agent layer is a fee-extraction point). Maintain **approved-recruiter lists** (IRIS-certified or equivalently assessed). **Map the corridor, not just the factory** and trace beyond Tier 1 to the labour-agent level. Require legitimate costs to be **employer-paid through auditable digital channels** so "no-fee" is verifiable, not asserted.

**4. Verification and worker voice.** **Don't rely on social audits alone** — add **worker-driven monitoring** (confidential, multilingual, anonymous channels meeting UNGP P31; where feasible the **WSR** model with binding economic consequences, as in the Fair Food Program). **Track outcome KPIs, not activity:** fees actually repaid to named workers; share hired through vetted channels; share of suppliers mapped to the sub-agent level; grievance resolution rate; passports held by workers themselves.

**5. Use the enforceability backdrop as leverage.** Frame supplier obligations as a **market-access requirement**: mandatory HREDD (UNGPs; OECD Guidelines + DD Guidance; **EU CSDDD**); import/market bans (**EU Forced Labour Regulation**; **US UFLPA** + §307 WROs); transparency statutes (**UK MSA 2015 s.54**; **Australia MSA 2018**; **California Transparency in Supply Chains Act**); on the floor of rights set by **ILO C97/C143/ICRMW** and **C29/C105**.

**Bottom line:** the brand's job is to (a) make EPP a precisely-defined, cascading contractual obligation with fee *repayment* as the remedy, (b) pay for ethical recruitment and reform its own purchasing practices, (c) channel hiring through vetted recruiters with traceable employer-paid costs, and (d) verify through worker voice rather than announced audits — backed by collaborative leverage and the HREDD/import-ban legal regime.

### B.3 Verifying a real fix vs. paper compliance

**Legal anchor (binding):** ILO C029 + P029 (Art. 2(d) recruitment protection; Art. 4 remedy/compensation); C105; C181 Art. 7; C097/C143; ICRMW; Palermo. **Operational:** ILO Fair Recruitment Guidelines + 2019 Definition; the 11 indicators. **Voluntary:** EPP, Dhaka Principles, IRIS, UNGP remedy pillar, OECD DD Guidance.

**The acid test — three rules, or it's paper.** An indicator counts as proof only if it is **(1) worker-verified or document-traceable** (not a management attestation or self-declaration), **(2) measured across successive recruitment cohorts over time** (a trend, not a snapshot), and **(3) collected by an independent party, off-site, in the worker's language.** A signed code, a passed social audit, or an on-site "workers confirm no fees" form fails all three.

**1. Recruitment-fee remediation — the core of "fixed."**

| Domain | Paper tell (reject) | Proof KPI (accept) |
|---|---|---|
| Fee baseline | "No fees" policy with no baseline ever measured | Independently surveyed cost-to-obtain-job per cohort; report **median and 90th percentile**, trending to $0 |
| Reimbursement scope | "Current workers attest satisfaction" | **100%** of identified affected workers repaid in full, **including those who already left** (the most-exploited leave first) |
| Reimbursement integrity | Cash, or routed through the labor agent | Paid to a **worker-controlled bank account**, reconciled to named (anonymized) workers via **remittance receipts**; fee calc by an independent methodology (Verité/IHRB/IRIS), not the agent's number |
| Speed | "In progress" indefinitely | Median days from fee-identification to full repayment **< 90 days** / within the current contract cycle |

**2. Structural channel change (so it doesn't reappear next cohort).** Full chain map (employer → agency → sub-agents → origin recruiter → village broker); KPI: % of workers whose *entire* chain is documented and count of **unauthorized sub-agents eliminated**. Contractual EPP with right-to-audit, clawback, and termination for fee-charging; KPI: % of recruiter spend under EPP contracts and # of recruiters off-boarded for breach. Recruiter certification; KPI: % of placements through IRIS-certified/vetted recruiters and reduction in intermediary tiers.

**3. ILO indicators converted to KPIs.** Document access: % of workers with sole, unimpeded access to their own documents — target **100%**, verified by *unannounced* check + interview, never a custody-waiver. Contract substitution: % whose destination contract matches the origin offer on wage/role/hours/deductions — **one contract**, in a language the worker reads, verified by comparing documents. Debt bondage: % carrying recruitment-linked debt (target 0); any "recruitment cost recovery" deduction (must be 0). Wage integrity: % paid into worker-controlled accounts; itemized native-language payslips; zero unlawful deductions; no withholding. Freedom to resign/move: # who resigned and repatriated in the last 12 months **without** forfeiting wages or a deposit (a non-zero, frictionless number is the proof).

**4. Grievance and worker voice — counterintuitive metrics.** A channel with **zero complaints is a red flag, not success.** Track grievances raised per 100 workers (expect it to **rise** as trust builds); uptake of the **anonymous, worker-device** channel; median time-to-resolution and **% closed to independently confirmed worker satisfaction**; and **retaliation cases: 0, verified by re-interview**, not by absence of reports.

**5. How verification is actually done (the "independent" part).** Off-site, native-language third-party interviews, representative sample, **repeated each cohort**; anonymous worker-voice technology on worker-owned devices (e.g., **Issara Inclusive Labour Monitoring, Ulula, &Wider, ELEVATE/LRQA**); **unannounced/semi-announced** visits with **rotated auditors** (same auditor every cycle is a capture risk); document forensics (payment ledgers, remittance receipts, recruiter contracts at *every* tier, fee-calculation methodology); and a **triangulation requirement** — management records + document trail + worker voice must agree, and divergence means "unresolved," not "minor."

**6. Red flags that mean "paper, not fixed."** Zero grievances; on-site management-observed "no-fees" attestation forms; reimbursement in cash or via the agent; only pre-announced audits with the same auditor and a narrow window; a "no-fee" policy with **no measured baseline**; wage/contract documents only in the destination-country language; remediation scoped to *current* workers, excluding those who already departed.

**Bottom line:** a supplier has *fixed* recruitment when, across consecutive cohorts, independently surveyed worker-paid cost trends to zero, every identified worker has been reimbursed to a traceable account, the recruitment chain is fully mapped under enforceable Employer-Pays contracts, 100% of workers hold their own documents and a single matching contract, and a trusted grievance channel is *generating and resolving* complaints without retaliation. Everything else is intent, not outcome.
