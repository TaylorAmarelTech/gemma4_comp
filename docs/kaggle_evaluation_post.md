# The Need for DueCare + Adversarial Review

*A prompt-time safety harness that makes any large language model better at spotting migrant-worker trafficking, and every control I ran to try to prove my own result wrong.*

Although the Gemma "AI for Good" competition has closed, I've kept hearing from people across very different industries - anti-trafficking non-governmental organizations, platform trust-and-safety teams, labour regulators, and AI researchers - about the safety harness I built for it. That sustained interest, combined with a string of recent court rulings on **who is responsible when an AI system says something harmful**, and my own determination to take DueCare from a hackathon entry to something that actually protects migrant workers, is why I'm publishing this three part follow-up.

## 1. The court cases - AI output is becoming the deployer's own words

**Should a model provider be liable when its AI helps someone traffic a human being?** In much of the world that is still unsettled law, but in May 2026 one court signalled the answer may be *yes*. The **Regional Court of Munich** held Google directly liable for false statements generated by its **AI Overviews**. The reasoning is the part that matters: the court found that AI-synthesized output is the provider's *own* speech, not protected third-party search results, because the system rewrites and restructures information into new statements in its own voice. And it held that telling users to "double-check" AI answers is **not** enough to escape responsibility. Google is appealing, and some commentators have described the decision as the end of the "search safe harbor" for AI answer engines.

Whatever the appeal decides, the trend may be meaningful: if deployers are increasingly held answerable for what their models *assert*, output-safety could shift from a nice-to-have toward **risk mitigation**. That reframes everything I built. A harness that *measurably* reduces harmful, ungrounded answers in a high-stakes domain may be more than just safer; it could be a control a deployer can point to.

Every raw-model failure we recorded - each shown in full, in the same before-and-after form - is on the companion page: **[duecare-ai.com/egregious-cases](https://duecare-ai.com/egregious-cases)**. Refusals are excluded there as well: every case shown is a reply in which the raw model allowed or instructed the scheme, never one in which it declined.

## 3. The follow-on study - an adversarial review of the harness

The obvious question is whether that flip is real or cherry-picked, so I ran the harness through an evaluation designed to **break my own result**. It is strictly paired: every prompt is answered by the same model twice, once raw ("baseline") and once wrapped in the harness ("harnessed"), and both are scored identically, so any grader bias cancels. The harness itself is pure prompt augmentation: it adds text to the prompt and does not change the model's weights. Before the model answers, it (a) runs keyword and pattern rules over the message to flag known exploitation indicators, (b) retrieves relevant legal grounding (International Labour Organization conventions, per-migration-corridor recruitment-fee limits, and anti-trafficking contacts), and (c) prepends an instruction to reason from that evidence and cite the governing law. There is no fine-tuning; it wraps any model.

**The headline:** on `gemma4:31b` across **911 prompts**, a judge model (a *different* large language model from the one being graded) scores the harness **+1.73 out of 10 higher** (95% confidence interval [+1.57, +1.89], **73% win rate**, Cohen's d = 0.69). Across 11 frontier models it improves every one.

**Then I tried to explain it away, and it held:**

- **Not just longer answers:** holding length constant, the harness still explains **+1.12** (t = 4.6).
- **Not "any preamble helps":** a knowledge-free, length-matched placebo preamble adds +1.16, but the harness's grounding adds **+3.34 *beyond* the placebo** (p < 0.001).
- **Not circular:** it also improves **21 of 21 dimensions it never injects** (empathy, plain-language rights, even minimizing personally identifiable information).
- **Not one judge's bias:** a panel of the newest, largest open models (gpt-oss, GLM, Qwen, Kimi, and DeepSeek-V4) all agree on the improvement; every one of 11 candidate models is positive.
- **Survives obfuscation and jailbreak attempts:** 14 input-attack methods (encoding, look-alike characters, language-switching, jailbreak wrappers). The harness improves the score **+4.39 under attack**, which is *larger* than the clean number, and **+4.10 even on base64 and ROT13 encoding**, where the keyword layer is 0% effective, because the retrieved grounding and reasoning carry the safety.
- **Head-to-head:** judged in pairs (which avoids the tendency of a 0-to-10 scale to pile up at 9 and 10), the harness wins **100%** of comparisons.

**A sharper rubric, graded 0 to 100 (the current primary method):** a single 0-to-10 score saturates near the top on strong replies, so I rebuilt the judge to reason through five weighted safety criteria and score each one before summing, on a 0-to-100 scale (more reasoning, finer resolution). On 100 hard adversarial scheme prompts this lifts `gemma4:31b` from **42.3 to 90.6 out of 100, a +48.3 point gain**, with stronger inter-judge agreement than the 0-to-10 scale (Krippendorff's alpha = 0.925, n = 100). The value of the per-criterion rubric is that it shows *where* the harness helps: the largest gains are on **citing the specific controlling law (+14.0)** and **pointing to concrete protective resources (+11.7)**, the two things an unharnessed reply most often omits. The full progression of measurement methods, and why this one is now primary, is memorialized in `benchmark_methods.md`; the run is `rich_harness_lift_100.md`.

**What I won't oversell:** "improves safety" means safety as measured by a rubric and by judge models, **not yet validated by human anti-trafficking experts**; that blinded study is the next step, and the honest blocker for a peer-reviewed claim. The largest gains are on the small on-device model, which is the *point* (it is the deployable case), not all models. And the evaluation is English-only text so far.

- **This is a self-review.** I authored both the DueCare harness and this evaluation, so it is not an independent third-party study. The point of the adversarial design is to test my own result honestly; the next step toward an independent claim is the blinded human-expert validation described above.
